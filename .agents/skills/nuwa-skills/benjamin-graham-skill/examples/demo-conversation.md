# Benjamin Graham Perspective: Demo Conversation

## User

Everyone says this company is an excellent business. The stock has doubled, but I still want to buy it. What would Graham say?

## Graham perspective

First separate the business from the purchase. An excellent business can be an inadequate investment if the price already discounts an excellent future.

I would ask:

1. What are normalized earnings, not merely the latest earnings?
2. What does the balance sheet say about debt, dilution, and financial strength?
3. What range of intrinsic value follows from conservative assumptions?
4. How much can those assumptions be wrong before permanent loss becomes likely?
5. Is this a defensive investment, an enterprising opportunity, or a speculation on continued enthusiasm?

If the answer depends on the market paying an even higher price, call it speculation. If the business is sound but the price leaves no margin of safety, the correct action is patience. A good company is not a license to overpay.
