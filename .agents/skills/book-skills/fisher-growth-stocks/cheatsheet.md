# Cheatsheet

| Situation | Fisher-style decision |
|---|---|
| Sales are growing but margins are flat or falling | Investigate whether growth creates earnings; favor companies with a credible margin-improvement program. |
| R&D spend is high | Do not reward the amount. Trace launches, adoption, repeat revenue, and returns on research. |
| Management presents only good news | Treat concealment as a serious governance failure; usually reject. |
| Product market looks exciting | Ask whether the opportunity lasts several years and whether management can move into the next market. |
| Company has a temporary factory/product problem | Buy only after proving the problem is finite, fixable, and already reflected in price. |
| Stock has doubled | Hold if the business has improved and future earnings justify the price; price gain is not a thesis break. |
| Broad market looks expensive or fragile | Do not sell a superior company on macro fear you cannot forecast reliably. |
| Dividend yield is low | Compare retained-earnings returns with cash yield; low payout can be correct for a high-return reinvestor. |
| Portfolio has many names | Ask whether each is better than the best candidate; remove mediocre holdings rather than diversify blindly. |
| Considering a management meeting | Finish external research first; arrive with a hypothesis and questions, not a blank questionnaire. |

## Exit Gate

Sell only when one of these is evidenced:

1. The 15-point profile has materially deteriorated.
2. Management quality, candor, or integrity is doubtful.
3. The market runway is exhausted and no adjacent runway is credible.
4. A superior opportunity is clear after taxes, risk, and replacement uncertainty.
