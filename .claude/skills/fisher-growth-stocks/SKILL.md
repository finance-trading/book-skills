## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The 15 Points for Growth Stocks (15个要点)
Fisher's core checklist for evaluating whether a company has exceptional long-term growth potential.

1. **Market potential** — products/services with sizable sales increase potential for several years
2. **R&D determination** — management determined to continue developing new products/processes
3. **R&D efficiency** — effective R&D relative to company size
4. **Sales organization** — above-average sales capability
5. **Profit margins** — worthwhile margins that grow with scale
6. **Labor relations** — maintaining/improving labor and personnel relations
7. **Executive relations** — outstanding executive team dynamics
8. **Management depth** — sufficient depth so the company isn't one-person dependent
9. **Cost analysis** — strong cost analysis and accounting controls
10. **Competitive moat** — other aspects giving competitive advantage
11. **Long-range outlook** — management focused on long-term profits, not short-term
12. **No dilution** — growth won't require excessive dilution of existing shareholders
13. **Open communication** — management talks freely in good times and bad
14. **Unquestionable integrity** — management of the highest ethical standards
15. **Buyback policy** — willing to buy back shares when undervalued

**When to use**: As a comprehensive pre-investment checklist. Score each point. A company scoring well on most is a candidate for concentrated, long-term holding.

**How**: Research through public records, scuttlebutt (customers/suppliers/competitors), management interviews.

### The Scuttlebutt Method (闲聊法)
Gather intelligence by talking to people directly involved with the company — customers, competitors, suppliers, former employees — not just management or published data.

- **When to use**: Always. Scuttlebutt verifies management's claims and uncovers what annual reports don't say.
- **How**: Ask open-ended questions. Listen for inconsistencies. Cross-check across sources. Build a mosaic.
- **Key insight**: Best questions arise spontaneously from previous answers — it's an art, not a script.

### The Four Elements of Conservative Investing (保守型投资四要素)
1. **First element**: Production, marketing, research, and financial capability — operational excellence
2. **Second element**: Human factors — management quality, integrity, "vivid spirit"
3. **Third element**: Business characteristics — industry structure, "honey jar" metaphor, scale economies
4. **Fourth element**: Price — the concept of "appraisal" (评价), how the financial community perceives value

### The Appraisal (评价) Concept
Stock prices move not because of what actually happens at a company, but because of what the financial community believes is happening. Appraisal is subjective and can diverge from fundamentals for years — but reality eventually wins.

**Four-tier risk stratification**:
- Tier 1 (lowest risk): Excellent fundamentals + appraisal below fundamentals = ideal buying opportunity
- Tier 2: Excellent fundamentals + appraisal in line = hold and let growth work
- Tier 3: Excellent fundamentals + appraisal above fundamentals = hold, don't buy new money
- Tier 4-5 (highest risk): Poor fundamentals or appraisal far exceeding fundamentals = dangerous

### Three Layers of Appraisal
Appraisal operates at three levels: (1) overall market, (2) industry, (3) individual company. Company-level appraisal matters most — a great company in an unpopular industry can still deliver extraordinary returns (Motorola example).

### The "Others Turn Left, You Turn Right" Principle
When the financial community undervalues quality due to temporary problems or market-wide fear, buy decisively. Requires deep fundamental analysis and patience.

### Selling Criteria (Three Valid Reasons Only)
1. You made a mistake in your original analysis
2. The company's fundamentals have deteriorated permanently
3. You find a significantly better opportunity (very rare)

Never sell for: price decline, temporary setback, market forecasts, or to "lock in profits."

### The Three-Year Rule
Don't evaluate investment performance in less than 3 years. First-year results are mostly luck.

### Market Timing Math
Short-term market prediction: ~60% accuracy. Long-term fundamental analysis: ~90% accuracy. The expected value overwhelmingly favors holding great companies over trying to time markets.

### The Seven Concluding Principles
1. Buy companies with systematic plans for significant earnings growth
2. Concentrate in companies out of favor (contrarian buying)
3. Hold until fundamentals change or growth stalls
4. De-emphasize dividends for capital appreciation seekers
5. Accept some mistakes as unavoidable costs; learn quickly
6. Outstanding companies are rare — concentrate when found
7. (Implicit) Deep research + patience + independence = extraordinary returns
