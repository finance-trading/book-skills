# Cheatsheet — 怎样选择成长股

## Quick Decision Guides

### "Should I buy this stock?"
```
1. Score the 15 Points (Ch 2). Most must be strong.
2. Run scuttlebutt (Ch 2, 9). Talk to customers, competitors, suppliers.
3. Apply four-tier classification (Ch 14):
   - Tier 1 (excellent + undervalued) → BUY
   - Tier 2 (excellent + fair) → HOLD / buy cautiously
   - Tier 3 (excellent + overvalued) → DON'T BUY
   - Tier 4-5 (poor or extreme) → AVOID
4. Can you hold for 3+ years? If no → don't buy.
5. Is this one of your best ideas? Concentrate capital (Ch 20).
```

### "Should I sell this stock?"
```
ONLY sell if:
  ✓ Company's fundamental character changed (management, competitive position)
  ✓ Growth permanently stalled (not temporarily)
  ✓ Found significantly better opportunity (very rare)

DO NOT sell for:
  ✗ Price decline or temporary setback
  ✗ Market forecasts or economic predictions
  ✗ To "lock in profits"
  ✗ Because P/E looks high (if fundamentals intact, earnings will catch up)
```

### "Is this stock cheap or expensive?"
```
1. DON'T look at current P/E alone
2. Estimate FUTURE earnings (3-5 years)
3. Apply reasonable P/E to future earnings
4. Compare to current price
5. Check appraisal gap (Ch 14):
   - Appraisal < fundamentals = bargain
   - Appraisal > fundamentals = dangerous
6. Remember: low P/E ≠ cheap (may signal declining fundamentals)
```

### "Is the market efficient?"
```
NO. Fisher's evidence:
- Stanford experiment: best 5 stocks +600%, worst 5 -68%, same 5 years
- Raychem: all info presented openly, market took 1-2 years to absorb
- Every decade produces extraordinary opportunities for diligent investors
- 60% accuracy for timing vs. 90% for fundamental analysis
→ Individual stock selection creates enormous value
```

## The 15 Points at a Glance

| # | Point | Key Question |
|---|-------|--------------|
| 1 | Market potential | Can sales grow sizably for several years? |
| 2 | R&D determination | Will management keep developing new products? |
| 3 | R&D efficiency | Is R&D effective relative to company size? |
| 4 | Sales organization | Above-average sales capability? |
| 5 | Profit margins | Worthwhile margins that grow with scale? |
| 6 | Labor relations | Good labor/personnel relations? |
| 7 | Executive relations | Strong executive team dynamics? |
| 8 | Management depth | Not one-person dependent? |
| 9 | Cost analysis | Strong cost controls and accounting? |
| 10 | Competitive moat | Other competitive advantages? |
| 11 | Long-range outlook | Focused on long-term, not short-term? |
| 12 | No dilution | Growth without excessive dilution? |
| 13 | Open communication | Talks freely in good AND bad times? |
| 14 | Integrity | Unquestionable ethical standards? |
| 15 | Buyback policy | Buys back shares when undervalued? |

## Four Elements of Conservative Investing

| Element | What It Covers | Key Test |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| 1st | Production, marketing, R&D, finance | Low-cost producer? Strong marketing? Effective R&D? Financial excellence? |
| 2nd | Human factors | Management quality, integrity, "vivid spirit" |
| 3rd | Business characteristics | Honey jar industry? Scale economies? Inherent profitability? |
| 4th | Price / Appraisal | Appraisal vs. fundamentals? P/E gap? Four-tier classification? |

## Seven Concluding Principles (Summary)

1. **Buy growth** — companies with systematic plans for significant earnings growth
2. **Buy contrarian** — concentrate in companies out of favor when fundamentals support
3. **Hold long-term** — until fundamentals change or growth stalls; never for short-term reasons
4. **Ignore dividends** — growth companies that retain earnings compound faster
5. **Accept some mistakes** — recognize quickly, learn, move on. Let winners run
6. **Concentrate** — outstanding companies are rare; bet heavily when found
7. **Research deeply** — scuttlebutt + fundamentals + patience + independence = extraordinary returns

## Anti-Pattern Quick Reference

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Buying low P/E automatically | Low P/E may signal declining fundamentals |
| Selling for "reasonable" profits | Opportunity cost is permanent and usually larger |
| Market timing (60% accuracy) | 40% error rate means missing best days |
| Relying only on financial statements | Published data lags reality; scuttlebutt is essential |
| Evaluating in < 3 years | First year is mostly luck |
| Obsessing over exact prices | Miss the forest for the trees (nickels-and-dimes) |
| Diversifying away from best ideas | Outstanding companies at good prices are rare; concentrate |
| Overpaying for growth | Even great companies are bad investments at absurd prices |
