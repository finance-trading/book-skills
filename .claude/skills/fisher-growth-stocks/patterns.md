# Patterns & Techniques — 怎样选择成长股

## Research Patterns

### Scuttlebutt Research (闲聊法)
**When**: Before any investment decision. After reading annual reports and financial data.
**Steps**:
1. Identify 5-10 customers, competitors, suppliers, and former employees
2. Ask open-ended questions — let answers guide follow-up questions
3. Listen for inconsistencies between sources
4. Cross-check: do customers' complaints match competitors' observations?
5. Build a mosaic — no single conversation is sufficient
6. Compare what people say with what published data shows — gaps are clues

**Key**: Best questions arise spontaneously. This is an art, not a script. Never rely on a single source.

### The 15-Point Scoring Method
**When**: Evaluating a potential investment candidate.
**Steps**:
1. Score each of the 15 points on a scale (strong / adequate / weak)
2. A company needs strong marks on most points to qualify
3. Points 1-3 (market potential, R&D) and 4-5 (sales, margins) are most critical
4. Points 10-15 (management quality, integrity) are non-negotiable
5. If a company fails on integrity (point 14), stop — no amount of growth compensates

### Industry Deep Dive
**When**: Selecting which industries to specialize in.
**Steps**:
1. Choose 2-3 industries you can understand deeply
2. Spend months learning the competitive landscape
3. Identify which companies have genuine advantages vs. apparent ones
4. Track how the industry evolves over multiple business cycles
5. Build relationships with industry participants for ongoing intelligence

**Fisher's example**: Chose chemicals and technology post-WWII. Spent months researching before selecting Dow Chemical in 1947.

## Decision Patterns

### The Four-Tier Classification
**When**: Deciding whether to buy, hold, or avoid a stock.
**Steps**:
1. Assess how well the company meets the first three elements (operational, human, business characteristics)
2. Assess the financial community's current appraisal (P/E ratio as proxy)
3. Compare appraisal to fundamentals
4. Classify:
   - Tier 1: Excellent + undervalued → BUY
   - Tier 2: Excellent + fair value → HOLD / buy cautiously
   - Tier 3: Excellent + overvalued → HOLD only, don't buy new money
   - Tier 4-5: Poor fundamentals OR extreme overvaluation → AVOID

### The "Others Turn Left" Contrarian Check
**When**: Considering a stock that the financial community dislikes.
**Steps**:
1. Why does the financial community dislike this stock?
2. Has your scuttlebutt research found something the crowd missed?
3. Are the problems temporary or permanent?
4. If temporary and fundamentals are strong → buy (contrarian opportunity)
5. If permanent or your research confirms the crowd → the crowd is right
6. Commit to holding for at least 3 years (three-year rule)

### The Three-Year Evaluation Discipline
**When**: Assessing whether an investment thesis is working.
**Steps**:
1. At purchase, write down exactly why you're buying (the thesis)
2. Commit to not evaluating performance for 3 years
3. After 1 year, the results are mostly luck — ignore them
4. After 3 years, assess: has the thesis played out?
5. Exception: If management integrity proves questionable, sell immediately — don't wait 3 years

### The 80% Decline Conviction Test
**When**: Deciding whether to hold through a severe drawdown.
**Steps**:
1. Stock has dropped 80% from peak (like TI in 1962)
2. Ask: has the fundamental thesis changed?
3. If fundamentals unchanged → hold. The decline is psychology, not reality.
4. If fundamentals changed → sell. Your thesis was wrong.
5. Remember: Fisher held TI through 80% decline; it eventually rose to new highs

### The "Sell Only for Fundamentals" Discipline
**When**: Considering whether to sell a holding.
**Steps**:
1. Is the company's fundamental character changed? (management deterioration, competitive position lost)
2. Has growth stalled permanently (not temporarily)?
3. Have you found a significantly better opportunity? (very rare)
4. If none of the above → DO NOT SELL
5. Specifically do NOT sell for: price decline, market forecasts, temporary setbacks, or to "lock in profits"

## Valuation Patterns

### Future Earnings Valuation
**When**: Assessing whether a stock is cheap or expensive.
**Steps**:
1. Estimate what the company will earn in 3-5 years (not what it earns today)
2. Apply a reasonable P/E multiple to those future earnings
3. Compare the result to current price
4. If current price is well below → potential bargain even if current P/E looks high
5. If current price is above → potential trap even if current P/E looks low

### The Appraisal Gap Analysis
**When**: Deciding whether the market has mispriced a stock.
**Steps**:
1. What does the financial community currently believe about this company?
2. What do fundamentals (your research) actually show?
3. Is appraisal above, below, or in line with fundamentals?
4. Below → buying opportunity (Tier 1)
5. In line → fair value (Tier 2)
6. Above → hold if you own it, avoid if you don't (Tier 3-5)
7. How long can the gap persist? Years — but reality eventually wins

### The Overvaluation Danger Check
**When**: A stock you're considering has an extremely high P/E.
**Steps**:
1. Is the high P/E justified by exceptional fundamentals? (Tier 3 — hold, don't buy)
2. Or does appraisal far exceed what circumstances can support? (Tier 5 — dangerous)
3. Fisher's examples of Tier 5: Memory Technology $173, Ampex $49, Levitz $60, Mohawk Data $81, Litton $101, Kalvar $176 — all crashed
4. When reality breaks through overvaluation, losses are severe
5. Rule of thumb: If you need everything to go perfectly, something won't

## Behavioral Patterns

### The Era Delusion Detector
**When**: Feeling swept up in market optimism or pessimism.
**Steps**:
1. What does "everyone know" about the market right now?
2. Is this the same pattern as past era delusions? (1927-29 euphoria, 1946-49 depression fear, 1969 tech euphoria)
3. Every era's "obvious" conclusion has been wrong
4. If consensus is euphoric → be cautious. If consensus is terrified → be alert.
5. Make decisions based on individual company fundamentals, not mass psychology

### The Nickels-and-Dimes Prevention
**When**: Hesitating to buy because the price isn't exactly what you want.
**Steps**:
1. Is the company fundamentally worth far more than the current price?
2. If yes, obsessing over a few cents is destroying dollar gains
3. A great company at $33⅜ is a bargain at $33½ too
4. The client who demanded exactly $33⅜ missed a 50%+ gain
5. Act on value, not on perfect prices

### The Trading Temptation Resistance
**When**: Tempted to sell a stock for a quick trading gain.
**Steps**:
1. If you sell this company, can you buy it back at a reasonable price?
2. If no → don't sell. You're trading a great long-term holding for small短期 gains.
3. Fisher's California Packing example: 3 successful trades, each profitable, all dwarfed by what holding would have produced
4. Trading great companies is the enemy of compounding

## Anti-Patterns to Remember

- **Relying solely on financial statements** → always supplement with scuttlebutt
- **Buying low P/E stocks automatically** → low P/E may signal declining fundamentals
- **Selling great stocks for "reasonable" profits** → opportunity cost is permanent
- **Market timing** → 60% accuracy produces terrible long-term results
- **Diversifying away from best ideas** → concentrate when you find rarity
- **Evaluating too quickly** → three-year rule exists for a reason
- **Overpaying for growth** → even great companies can be bad investments at absurd prices
