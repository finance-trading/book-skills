# Glossary — 怎样选择成长股

**15个要点 (15 Points)** — Fisher's comprehensive checklist for evaluating growth stock candidates. Covers market potential, R&D, sales, margins, management quality, and competitive advantages. (Ch 2)

**闲聊法 (Scuttlebutt Method)** — Research technique of gathering intelligence from a company's customers, competitors, suppliers, and former employees. The best investment information comes from people directly involved, not published data. (Ch 2)

**评价 (Appraisal)** — The financial community's subjective perception of a company's value. Stock prices move based on appraisal changes, not actual events. Can diverge from fundamentals for years but eventually converges. (Ch 14)

**三层评价 (Three Layers of Appraisal)** — Appraisal operates at three levels: (1) overall market sentiment, (2) industry attractiveness, (3) individual company perception. Company-level appraisal matters most. (Ch 15)

**保守型投资 (Conservative Investing)** — Fisher's framework for low-risk investing through four elements: operational excellence, human factors, business characteristics, and price. Requires growth for capital preservation in an inflationary world. (Ch 11–16)

**四要素 (Four Elements)** — The four requirements for conservative investing: (1) production/marketing/R&D/finance capability, (2) human factors, (3) business characteristics, (4) price/appraisal. (Ch 11–14)

**四层风险分级 (Four-Tier Risk Stratification)** — Classifying stocks by (1) how well they meet the first three elements and (2) whether appraisal is below, in line with, or above fundamentals. Lowest risk: excellent fundamentals + undervalued appraisal. (Ch 14)

**双重效应 (Dual Effect)** — When earnings growth and P/E expansion work together to drive extraordinary stock price gains. The P/E expansion often contributes more than the earnings growth itself. (Ch 14)

**别人往左走，你往右走 (Others Turn Left, You Turn Right)** — Contrarian investing principle: buy when the financial community undervalues quality due to temporary problems or market-wide fear. Requires deep analysis and patience. (Ch 18)

**三年法则 (Three-Year Rule)** — Don't evaluate investment performance in less than 3 years. First-year results are mostly luck; only after three years can skill be distinguished from randomness. (Ch 18)

**时代妄想 (Era Delusion)** — Each era has its dominant mass psychology that seems obvious to contemporaries but is wrong in retrospect: 1927-29 "New Era" euphoria, 1946-49 post-war depression fear, 1969 tech euphoria. (Ch 19)

**蜜糖罐比喻 (Honey Jar Metaphor)** — Industries with inherently attractive characteristics (like a jar of honey that keeps attracting bees) versus those with structurally poor economics. Some businesses are inherently more profitable than others. (Ch 13)

**活生生的精神 (Vivid Spirit)** — The intangible quality of exceptional management teams. Not just competence, but a contagious energy and commitment that permeates the entire organization. (Ch 13)

**吉尔伯特与萨利文原则 (Gilbert and Sullivan Principle)** — Don't emphasize irrelevant statistics. Past prices and past earnings don't predict future performance. Focus on what matters for the future. (Ch 8)

**低本益比陷阱 (Low P/E Trap)** — A low P/E ratio is not automatically a bargain. It may signal declining fundamentals. True value depends on future earnings trajectory, not current earnings. (Ch 17)

**闲聊法三层面 (Scuttlebutt Three Levels)** — (1) Talk to customers: what do they love/complain about? (2) Talk to competitors: what do they fear/respect? (3) Talk to suppliers: who pays on time, who's reliable? Cross-check all sources. (Ch 2)

**集中投资 (Concentrated Investing)** — Outstanding companies at attractive prices are rare. When found, concentrate capital in the best ideas rather than diversifying broadly. (Ch 20)

**股利无关论 (Dividend Irrelevance)** — For investors seeking capital appreciation, dividends are largely irrelevant. Growth companies should retain earnings to reinvest at high rates of return. High dividend payout reduces the pool of ideal investments. (Ch 6, 19)

**市场效率谬误 (Efficient Market Fallacy)** — The academic claim that stock prices immediately reflect all known information. Fisher's 40 years of evidence (Stanford experiment, Raychem) prove that dispersion is enormous and markets are NOT efficient for diligent long-term investors. (Ch 20)

**永久持有原则 (Hold-Forever Principle)** — Hold stocks until (a) company fundamentals fundamentally change, or (b) growth can no longer exceed the overall economy. Never sell for short-term reasons, market forecasts, or to lock in profits. (Ch 5, 20)

**发展周期时机 (Development Cycle Timing)** — Companies go through development cycles. Buy during early stages of a new product/market cycle when growth is about to accelerate. Avoid late-stage companies where growth is decelerating. (Ch 4)

**管理三测试 (Three Management Tests)** — (1) Willingness to sacrifice short-term profits for long-term gains, (2) Excellence in daily operations, (3) Ability to acknowledge and correct mistakes. (Ch 17)

**赫伯特·道原则 (Herbert Dow's Principles)** — "Never promote someone who hasn't made mistakes" and "if you can't do something better than others, don't do it." Principles that outlived the founder and remained embedded in Dow Chemical's culture. (Ch 19)
