# Chapter 6: Accounting for Profitability

## Core Idea
Measure profit against the net assets used by the operating business. RNOA is more coherent than ROA, EBITDA, or ad hoc returns because it separates financing assets and includes operating liabilities in the investment base.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Residual operating income**: `ReOI_t = (RNOA_t − r_firm) × NOA_(t−1)`. Use when judging whether operations earned more than the required return on opening net operating assets.
- **RNOA measurement**: Divide after-tax operating income by beginning NOA, where NOA is operating assets minus operating liabilities. Remove debt assets from the operating base and net interest from operating income.
- **Operating liability leverage**: Analyze how trade payables, deferred revenue, and other operating obligations lower shareholder capital committed to assets. The leverage can add value when the terms are durable and not offset by higher prices or costs.
- **Balance-sheet protection**: Value both opening NOA and residual operating income. A high accounting return caused by expensing investment or omitting intangible assets is not automatically extra value: lower book value and higher residual income can offset.

## Key Concepts
- **ROA**: Operating return divided by total assets, which may include financing assets and ignore operating liabilities.
- **RNOA**: Return on net operating assets, aligned to business operations.
- **EBITDA**: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization; omits real capital consumption and tax costs.
- **Economic profitability**: Return on the full investment that produced the income, including investments accounting may have expensed.
- **Accounting profitability**: Return on reported book assets, which may differ from economic profitability.

## Mental Models
- Match the income numerator to exactly the assets and liabilities in the denominator.
- Ask whether reported profitability comes from customers, supplier financing, or a denominator altered by accounting.
- Use the balance sheet and income statement together; no stand-alone profitability metric is a value estimate.

## Anti-patterns
- **Stripping goodwill from the base while keeping acquisition earnings**: It makes acquired profit look free.
- **EBITDA as owner cash**: The business must still replace assets, pay tax, and finance investment.
- **Capitalizing a high RNOA mechanically**: Unrecorded brand or R&D investment may raise the measured ratio without increasing true value.

## Worked Example
The authors compare two lemonade stands with $400 of inventory. One pays suppliers on delivery and earns $48 on $400 NOA, or 12% RNOA. The other gets $200 supplier credit, leaving $200 NOA. If income stayed at $48, RNOA would jump to 24%; but the supplier charges a higher price for sugar, lowering income to $44 and actual RNOA to 22%. Operating liability leverage raises the return only after accounting for its implicit financing cost.

## Key Takeaways
1. Calculate RNOA from reformulated statements.
2. Charge the operating asset base before calling earnings value added.
3. Investigate both the economics and accounting behind a high profitability figure.
