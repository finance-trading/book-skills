# Chapter 12: Investing by the Book: Investing in Style

## Core Idea
“Value,” “growth,” and size style labels are crude proxies. Earnings yield and book-to-price embed accounting choices, expected growth, profitability, and risk; fundamental analysis is needed to avoid value and size traps.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Joint E/P and B/P reading**: Do not rank on either ratio alone. For a given E/P, a higher B/P implies lower ROE; ask whether that ROE reflects temporary pressure, poor economics, or future risk.
- **ROE risk analysis**: Separate operating return, leverage, accounting effects, and uncertainty in future earnings before deciding whether low P/B is an opportunity.
- **Size-trap analysis**: Small firms may offer growth but with wider return dispersion, financing vulnerability, and downside tails. Large firms may offer apparent safety while much of their price rests on difficult-to-sustain growth.
- **Style-to-fundamentals bridge**: Use style screens only to choose candidates; then reformulate statements and challenge the market's implied growth and risk.

## Key Concepts
- **E/P**: Earnings yield, affected by accounting earnings quality and growth expectations.
- **B/P**: Book-to-price, affected by reported book value and expected ROE.
- **Value premium**: Historical return difference associated with low-multiple portfolios; not guaranteed for a specific stock or period.
- **Value trap**: Cheap-looking price attached to high risk or decaying profitability.
- **Size trap**: Mistaking small capitalization for an intrinsic return advantage without examining risk and business quality.

## Mental Models
- Multiples are compressed statements about economic forecasts, not reasons for a trade.
- A high-ROE growth stock can have an expensive price yet still be attractive, or a low P/B stock can be too expensive for its risk.
- Look at the dispersion and downside of a style strategy, not just its average historical payoff.

## Anti-patterns
- **Buying style-factor exposure as if it were company analysis**: Factors leave company-specific risk and accounting effects unresolved.
- **Assuming cheap stocks are safe**: High book-to-price can signal deteriorating returns.
- **Assuming large firms are safe**: A high price may capitalize fragile growth.

## Worked Example
The chapter compares portfolios formed jointly on E/P and B/P and then contrasts their ROE and risk. An identical E/P can coexist with different B/P because the associated ROE differs; one portfolio may hold firms with weak underlying profitability rather than genuine discounts. The authors use this joint reading to explain why a simple low-P/E or low-P/B style could perform poorly during a stress year such as 2008 or 2015.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use E/P and B/P together, then investigate what produces ROE.
2. Interpret style performance through risk, accounting, and business drivers.
3. Treat screens as triage, never as a finished valuation.
