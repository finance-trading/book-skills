# Chapter 7: Accounting for Growth

## Core Idea
A growth company grows residual operating income, not merely revenue, EPS, or accounting earnings. Investigate the sustainability of operating profit, the asset investment needed to support sales, and the competitive durability of each driver.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Residual operating income growth**: Start from `ReOI = (RNOA − r_firm) × NOA`. Earnings growth creates value only when the operating return exceeds the charge for the additional NOA.
- **Sustainable operating income**: Separate recurring sales profit from transitory gains, restructuring, investment revaluations, tax effects, and other items that cannot support a forward run rate.
- **Value-driver tree**: Decompose `RNOA = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover`, where margin is sustainable after-tax operating income from sales / sales and turnover is sales / NOA. Decompose margin into gross margin, operating expenses, and tax; decompose NOA into receivables, inventory, fixed assets, and operating liabilities relative to sales.
- **Sensitivity by driver**: Change one operating assumption at a time, propagate through margin or turnover into RNOA, then into ReOI and the market-implied growth path. Use scenarios rather than an unsupported point forecast.

## Key Concepts
- **Sustainable profitability**: A defensible earnings base after stripping nonrecurring or non-sales items.
- **Profit margin (PM)**: Operating profit earned per dollar of sales.
- **Asset turnover (ATO)**: Sales produced per dollar of NOA.
- **Fade**: Erosion of excess growth or profitability as competition acts.
- **Revenue driver**: Customer volume, price, capacity, subscriptions, or other business-specific determinant of sales.

## Mental Models
- Sales growth without adequate incremental returns can destroy value.
- Treat a high future ReOI path implied by price as a set of operating-driver claims to test.
- Keep the balance sheet in view: fast sales growth often requires more receivables, inventory, plant, or working capital.

## Anti-patterns
- **Buying EPS growth from financing leverage**: It is not operating value growth.
- **Using a temporary gain as the base for forecasts**: Revaluation and one-time accounting items reverse or disappear.
- **Forecasting a single top-line growth rate**: It omits margin, asset needs, and competition.

## Worked Example
For Microsoft in 2023, the authors report sustainable RNOA of 58.0%, from a 34.3% sustainable profit margin and 1.69 asset turnover. The decline from 2022 reflects both margin pressure and weaker turnover. A scenario that cuts margin to 30% should therefore flow through RNOA and ReOI rather than being appended to a separate price multiple. The chapter also strips Amazon's gain on Rivian shares from a quarter's operating-income comparison because it did not come from the business and later reversed.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define growth in value-added operating earnings, then unpack its causes.
2. Build a sustainable income base before extrapolating.
3. Test market-implied growth using sales, margin, and investment requirements together.
