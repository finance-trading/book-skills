# Chapter 4: Accounting for the Market Price

## Core Idea
Instead of pretending to know a precise intrinsic value, calculate what growth the market price implies and decide whether business evidence supports it. The valuation model becomes a tool for questioning price.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Accounting value versus speculative value**: Start with book value and near-term forecasts you trust. Set speculative long-run residual earnings growth to zero for a no-growth anchor, then subtract that anchor from market price to see what the market pays for growth.
- **Reverse engineer implied growth**: Hold price, current book value, forecast earnings, and the chosen hurdle rate fixed; solve the residual-income model for the long-run growth rate `g` implicit in price. Investigate unusually high or low `g` rather than mechanically ranking P/E or P/B.
- **Declining growth path**: Model residual earnings growth fading from near-term forecasts toward a long-run economy-wide rate. The author's example uses `g_(t+1) = w × g_t + (1−w) × 4%` as a scenario, not a universal forecast.
- **Dynamic tracking**: Recalculate the market's price of growth and implied `g` as quotes and evidence change.

## Key Concepts
- **No-growth value**: Value from book value and near-term earnings if residual earnings do not grow beyond the explicit forecast.
- **Market's price of growth**: Market price minus the no-growth accounting value.
- **Implied g**: A market expectation extracted from price, contingent on the chosen hurdle rate and forecasts.
- **Competitive advantage period**: The time over which excess profitability can persist before competition erodes it.

## Mental Models
- Treat a valuation model as an interrogation of price: “What must be true?”
- Change the hurdle rate and near-term evidence explicitly; do not disguise their effects as a single objective `g`.
- Separate a low market-implied `g` screen from a buy conclusion; fundamental analysis must establish whether the market is too pessimistic.

## Anti-patterns
- **Point-value worship**: Precision in a terminal value does not eliminate uncertainty.
- **Unexamined growth assumption**: Plugging an analyst's speculative `g` into a model merely converts speculation into a decimal answer.
- **Price-contaminated valuation**: Letting the quote inform an allegedly independent estimate produces circular reasoning.

## Worked Example
For Netflix on March 15, 2022, the chapter uses an 8% hurdle rate and a $343.75 share price. Its near-term accounting yields a $166.66 no-growth value; the difference, $177.09, is the market's price of longer-run growth. The question becomes whether Netflix's operating economics justify paying $177.09 for that uncertain component. The chapter then tracks how the implied growth changes as Netflix's quote falls and later recovers.

## Key Takeaways
1. Separate what can be accounted for from the price of unproven growth.
2. State the hurdle rate before interpreting an implied growth figure.
3. Use implied growth to focus investigation, then test it with operating drivers.
