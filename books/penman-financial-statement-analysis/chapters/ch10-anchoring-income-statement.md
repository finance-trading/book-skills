# Chapter 10: Keeping the Book: The Anchoring Income Statement

## Core Idea
Forecast from a sustainable, predictive operating income statement. Reported earnings can include transitory items or accounting distortions; those leave clues in the balance sheet and cash flows.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three-step predictive income statement**: Separate operating from financing income; remove unsustainable gains and losses; investigate the accounting and estimates behind recurring-looking sales, costs, taxes, and assets.
- **Follow the trail**: Use `Operating Income = Free Cash Flow + Change in NOA` as a reconciliation. An unexplained increase in operating earnings often comes with asset build-up or liability reduction; then investigate receivables, inventory, deferred revenue, capitalization, and cash conversion.
- **Revenue-quality diagnostic**: Reconcile `Sales Revenue = Cash Sales + Change in Gross Receivables − Change in Deferred Revenue`. Test whether reported growth is accompanied by collectability, acceptance, and durable customer obligations.
- **Accounting arbitrage**: When low-quality earnings inflate market expectations, adjust the accounting and challenge the resulting price before the reversal becomes obvious.

## Key Concepts
- **Sustainable operating income**: Recurring earnings from the business after removing nonrepeatable items and correctly allocating tax.
- **Deferred revenue**: Cash received before an obligation is performed; not yet earned revenue.
- **Inventory-to-sales**: Rising stock relative to sales can signal margin pressure or future markdowns.
- **Pension income**: Returns on pension assets that should not be mixed with profit from customer sales.
- **S-score**: The authors' quality-diagnostic score built from accounting signals, used as a prompt for investigation.

## Mental Models
- Earnings manipulation must balance somewhere in double-entry accounting.
- A one-time write-down can depress current earnings while flattering later margins through a lower asset base.
- Reclassify equity-method income, lease interest, pension returns, and investment gains before measuring operating margins.

## Anti-patterns
- **Extrapolating headline earnings**: Restructuring, grant income, asset sales, and tax benefits may not recur.
- **Ignoring receivable or inventory build**: The balance sheet may contradict reported sales and margin strength.
- **Inventing maintenance CapEx**: A precise replacement number can be more speculative than the accounting depreciation it replaces.

## Worked Example
Delta's 2020 reported loss mixes pandemic operating collapse, government grants, impairments, tax effects, and net financing cost. The authors reformulate it to a sustainable operating loss after tax of $6.587 billion, a different base from the headline $12.385 billion net loss. This does not make the business healthy; it gives a clearer starting point for recovery and default scenarios.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reconcile the income statement to the balance sheet and cash flows.
2. Normalize only with evidence from notes and business economics.
3. Model the reversal of accounting choices that artificially raise current earnings.
