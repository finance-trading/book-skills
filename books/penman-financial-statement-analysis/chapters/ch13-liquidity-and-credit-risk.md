# Chapter 13: Keeping a Book on Liquidity and the Analysis of Credit Risk

## Core Idea
Free cash flow is a poor standalone measure of going-concern value creation, but a crucial measure of cash available to service debt. A liquidity book projects when obligations exceed available cash and financing options.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Cash-flow identity**: `FCF = Cash from Operations (C) − Net Cash Investment (I) = Net Dividends to Shareholders (d) + Payments to Net Debtholders (F)`. Reconcile the cash flow statement before interpreting financing needs.
- **Default point**: Compare cash needed for interest and principal with `C − I − d` and available debt assets; if obligations exceed resources, evaluate cash, debt rollovers, asset sales, equity issuance, lower investment, or suspended distributions.
- **Treasurer's Rule**: Forecast net debt through `Net Debt_t = Net Debt_(t−1) + Net Interest_t − (FCF_t − Net Dividends_t)`. Use a pro forma balance sheet and cash-flow statement to identify when debt financing or asset liquidation becomes necessary.
- **Reformulated cash-flow statement**: Separate operating investment from financing flows and align them with reformulated balance sheets and income statements.

## Key Concepts
- **Liquidity**: Ability to meet near-term obligations with cash and financing access.
- **Cash burn**: Persistent cash outflow that consumes debt assets or requires external funding.
- **Debt service**: Scheduled interest and principal payments.
- **Default point**: Forecast state in which obligations cannot be met without an unavailable financing or operating adjustment.
- **FCF**: Cash remaining after operating investment, relevant to debt coverage even if negative during value-creating growth.

## Mental Models
- A growing firm can have negative FCF and positive value; it still needs a credible financing path.
- Model timing, not just totals: cash arriving after a maturity cannot service that maturity.
- Treat refinancing as a contingent option, not certain cash.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling negative FCF value destruction**: It may fund profitable investment.
- **Ignoring negative FCF in credit analysis**: Even good projects can fail if liquidity runs out first.
- **Using an unreconciled cash-flow forecast**: It can silently assume money appears or liabilities disappear.

## Worked Example
The authors examine Bed Bath & Beyond's cash-flow statement and falling liquidity. In a pro forma book, operating cash and investments are projected alongside dividends and debt service. If the shortfall persists, debt assets decline and net debt rises; the investor can identify the period in which debt service depends on new borrowing or asset sales. The analysis distinguishes a poor valuation claim from the more immediate question of whether the firm can survive to realize any value.

## Key Takeaways
1. Put FCF in a cash-availability and debt-service context.
2. Forecast cash, net debt, and obligations jointly.
3. Stress refinancing and operating assumptions to locate default points.
