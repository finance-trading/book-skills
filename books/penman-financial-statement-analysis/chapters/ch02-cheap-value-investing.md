# Chapter 2: Cheap Value Investing

## Core Idea
Low P/E or P/B is not sufficient evidence of cheapness. Multiples jointly reflect growth, profitability, and the risk of those outcomes, so cheap-looking stocks can be value traps.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Decompose the multiple**: Use when a stock screens as cheap. Examine the earnings or book value in the denominator, expected growth, and risk before comparing the multiple with history or peers.
- **Value-added earnings**: Earnings add value only after a charge for the opening book value: `Value-added Earnings_t = Earnings_t − r × Book Value_(t−1) = (ROE_t − r) × Book Value_(t−1)`. Use this to distinguish mere accounting earnings from return above the required return.
- **Growth-risk reading of P/E and P/B**: A high P/E can reflect expected growth or low risk; a low P/E can reflect weak or risky growth. P/B depends on expected ROE and risk to ROE.

## Key Concepts
- **P/E**: Price relative to reported or forecast earnings, never an independent value estimate.
- **P/B**: Price relative to equity book value, affected by expected profitability and risk.
- **E/P**: Earnings yield, the inverse of P/E.
- **Value trap**: A low multiple that compensates for deterioration or risky earnings rather than mispricing.
- **Required return (r)**: Risk-free rate plus a premium for risk borne; the hurdle for value-added earnings.

## Mental Models
- Ask what the market is charging for growth and whether the growth is likely to survive competition.
- Compare operating economics, accounting methods, and risk across peers before applying peer multiples.
- Treat price/sales as especially incomplete because it omits margins, investment requirements, and risk.

## Anti-patterns
- **Mechanical low-multiple buying**: It confuses a low quote relative to current accounting with a discount to value.
- **PEG as a universal shortcut**: It treats forecast growth as certain and ignores risk and the quality of earnings.
- **Value versus growth labels**: They hide that both expected growth and risk help set every multiple.

## Worked Example
The chapter contrasts forward P/E ratios for automobile firms. Toyota, GM, and Volkswagen can show low ratios while Ferrari shows a high one; the ratio alone does not say which is cheap. The investor must test whether profitability, growth durability, capital requirements, and uncertainty explain the differences. Likewise, a low P/B could mean that future ROE is expected to disappoint.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reconstruct the economics behind a multiple before ranking stocks.
2. Charge earnings for the capital that produced them.
3. A bargain requires evidence that the market is too pessimistic, not just a small denominator ratio.
