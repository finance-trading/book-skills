## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Keep an accounting book, then negotiate with Mr. Market

The authors treat value investing as an accounting discipline. A quoted stock price is a proposition about a business, not an answer. Start by understanding the business and reformulating its statements; build an accounting value anchor from book value and sustainable earnings; then identify the part of price that depends on uncertain growth. Do not use the market price inside an allegedly independent value calculation. A price decline alone does not establish cheapness. See [Ch 1](chapters/ch01-opening-the-book.md) and [Ch 4](chapters/ch04-accounting-for-the-market-price.md).

### Charge earnings for the capital that produced them

`Residual Earnings_t = Earnings_t − r × Opening Equity Book Value`, or `(ROE_t − r) × Opening Equity Book Value`. A firm does not add value merely by reporting earnings or earnings growth. For the operating business, use `Residual Operating Income (ReOI)_t = (RNOA_t − r_firm) × Opening NOA`. Pair the income statement with the relevant balance-sheet investment. If forecast ROE only covers the required return, equity book value is the value anchor. See [Ch 2](chapters/ch02-cheap-value-investing.md), [Ch 3](chapters/ch03-keeping-a-book-for-value-investing.md), and [Ch 6](chapters/ch06-accounting-for-profitability.md).

### Separate operations from financing

Reformulate the statements as `Common Equity = Net Operating Assets (NOA) − Net Financing Debt`, with `NOA = Operating Assets − Operating Liabilities` and `Net Debt = Debt Liabilities − Debt Assets`. Use RNOA for business performance; inspect ROE through `ROE = RNOA + LEV × (RNOA − Net Borrowing Cost)`. Debt and repurchases can amplify EPS, ROE, and risk without improving the business. Operating liabilities can reduce the capital needed to operate and may add value, but their terms and obligations need examination. See [Ch 5](chapters/ch05-accounting-for-leverage.md) and [Ch 6](chapters/ch06-accounting-for-profitability.md).

### Reverse engineer the growth already priced in

Calculate a no-growth value from book value and credible near-term residual earnings. `Market's price of growth = Market price − No-growth accounting value`. Solve for the long-run residual-income growth `g` that reconciles the quote to the accounting model under an explicit required return. A low implied `g` is a research candidate, not a buy signal; a high implied `g` is a claim to test. The implied rate moves with the hurdle and near-term forecasts. Track it as information and price change. See [Ch 4](chapters/ch04-accounting-for-the-market-price.md).

### Test growth through business drivers

Growth that adds value is growth in residual operating income, not merely sales or EPS. Normalize the operating income base, then decompose `RNOA = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover`; analyze gross margin, operating costs, tax, sales, receivables, inventory, fixed assets, and operating liabilities. Propagate alternative driver scenarios into ReOI and value. Require a credible account of competitive advantage and fade before paying for distant growth. See [Ch 7](chapters/ch07-accounting-for-growth.md).

### Protect the anchor and inspect the downside

Read footnotes for commitments, contingent liabilities, pensions, options, convertibles, and fragile fair values. Reconcile earnings to balance-sheet changes and cash flows: `Operating Income = FCF + Change in NOA`. Earnings manipulation or temporary gains can leave a trail in receivables, inventory, deferred revenue, write-downs, and tax. Construct growth-return and value-at-risk profiles rather than claiming a single exact intrinsic value. Required return is an investor-specific hurdle; show sensitivity instead of false precision. See [Ch 8](chapters/ch08-accounting-for-risk-to-value.md), [Ch 9](chapters/ch09-anchoring-balance-sheet.md), and [Ch 10](chapters/ch10-anchoring-income-statement.md).

### Put free cash flow in the right job

FCF is not a standalone measure of value creation because valuable investment consumes cash. It is essential for liquidity and default analysis. Reconcile `FCF = Cash from Operations − Net Cash Investment = Net Dividends + Payments to Net Debtholders`, then forecast debt service and available funding by period. A profitable project can still fail if financing runs out before payoffs arrive. See [Ch 3](chapters/ch03-keeping-a-book-for-value-investing.md) and [Ch 13](chapters/ch13-liquidity-and-credit-risk.md).
