# Decision Cheatsheet

| If you observe… | Do this | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| Low P/E or P/B | Test growth, ROE, accounting quality, and risk together | A low multiple may be a value trap (Ch 2, 12). |
| Strong EPS or ROE after debt issuance or buybacks | Reformulate operations and financing; calculate RNOA and ReOI | Leverage can raise shareholder ratios without creating operating value (Ch 5–6). |
| High reported RNOA | Pair income with NOA and check missing/expensed investment | Accounting profitability need not equal economic profitability (Ch 6). |
| Negative FCF during expansion | Ask whether incremental operating returns exceed the capital charge, then test financing runway | Growth investment uses cash; default risk is separate (Ch 3, 13). |
| Sales or margin growth with growing receivables/inventory | Reconcile income to change in NOA and cash; read notes | Earnings quality problems leave a balance-sheet trail (Ch 10). |
| An unusually low market-implied `g` | Test whether operating drivers justify a higher path | Only fundamental evidence turns a screen into a bargain (Ch 4, 7). |
| A high speculative share of price | Stress margin, asset turnover, fade, and tail outcomes | The investor pays today for uncertain future ReOI (Ch 4, 7–8). |
| Commitments, guarantees, options, or Level 3 assets | Stress or adjust the safe balance sheet | Hidden claims and fragile values can erase the anchor (Ch 9). |
| Acquisition EPS accretion or vague synergy claims | Value the combined firm and each shareholder group's consideration | Accretion need not add value to original owners (Ch 11). |

## Calculation chain

1. `Equity = NOA − Net Debt`; `NOA = Operating Assets − Operating Liabilities` (Ch 5).
2. `RNOA = Sustainable Operating Income / Opening NOA` (Ch 6–7).
3. `ReOI = (RNOA − r_firm) × Opening NOA` (Ch 6).
4. `RNOA = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover`; `Asset Turnover = Sales / NOA` (Ch 7).
5. `Market's price of growth = Price − No-growth accounting value`; solve for implied `g` under a stated hurdle (Ch 4).
6. `FCF = C − I = Dividends to shareholders + Payments to net debtholders`; compare with debt service by period (Ch 13).

## Rules of thumb from the authors

- Use a conservative, quality-checked balance sheet and sustainable earnings as the anchor; never put the price you are testing into that anchor (Ch 1, 9–10).
- Do not adopt an exact universal cost of capital; state the investor's required return and show sensitivity (Ch 8).
- Treat growth above a stable long-run rate as a finite competitive-advantage claim, not a perpetuity by default (Ch 4, 7).
- A red flag triggers investigation, not an automatic accusation or trade (Ch 10).
