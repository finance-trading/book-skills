# Patterns and Techniques

## Reformulate financial statements
**When to use**: Before comparing business profitability or applying an enterprise valuation.  
**How**: Classify operating assets/liabilities and debt assets/liabilities; reconcile `Equity = NOA − ND`; split after-tax operating income from net financing expense. Trace ambiguous lines to footnotes.  
**Trade-offs**: Classification can require judgment, especially for cash, leases, and investment portfolios; document it.

## Build a residual-earnings anchor
**When to use**: When a DCF forecast depends heavily on distant cash flow.  
**How**: Start with book value, forecast earnings, subtract `r × opening book value`, and add supported residual earnings. Leave uncertain growth visible rather than burying it in a terminal value.  
**Trade-offs**: Book value and earnings must be quality checked; the hurdle rate remains an assumption.

## Reverse engineer market growth
**When to use**: When deciding which quoted stock deserves research or whether a quote demands too much growth.  
**How**: Fix current book value, credible short-term earnings, and investor hurdle; calculate no-growth value; solve for the residual-income growth path that matches price. Repeat as price or evidence changes.  
**Trade-offs**: Implied growth varies with the hurdle and near-term forecasts; it is a market-implied hypothesis, not the company's actual outlook.

## Decompose operating growth
**When to use**: When a quote implies persistent ReOI growth.  
**How**: Normalize earnings; compute `RNOA = PM × ATO`; break PM into gross margin, expenses, and tax; break ATO into NOA components relative to sales. Vary sales, margins, investment, and operating liabilities in scenarios.  
**Trade-offs**: Segment and cost disclosures can be incomplete; avoid false precision.

## Test accounting quality by following the trail
**When to use**: When earnings growth looks stronger than cash conversion or business facts.  
**How**: Reconcile `Operating Income = FCF + Change in NOA`; inspect receivables, deferred revenue, inventory, capitalized costs, write-downs, tax, pensions, and notes. Model likely reversals.  
**Trade-offs**: A red flag is not proof of manipulation; growth investment can also enlarge NOA.

## Make the balance sheet safe
**When to use**: Before trusting book value, leverage, or market-implied growth.  
**How**: Read commitments, contingencies, option/convertible terms, pensions, and fair-value disclosures. Include plausible claims or stress them separately; inspect Level 3 values and assets exposed to impairment.  
**Trade-offs**: Booking a full contingent liability can be overconservative; show probability and asset offsets.

## Profile value at risk
**When to use**: When upside depends on uncertain growth or when tail loss matters.  
**How**: Start from no-growth value; propagate low, central, and high operating-driver scenarios into ReOI and value; compare each with price and liquidity constraints.  
**Trade-offs**: Scenario probabilities are judgments; inspect sensitivity rather than presenting a single target.

## Analyze insurer float and reserves
**When to use**: For property-and-casualty insurers.  
**How**: Reformulate underwriting, claims reserves, float, investment securities, and ventures; test combined ratio, reserve adequacy, and investment fair values under stress.  
**Trade-offs**: Reserve estimation and catastrophe tails can overwhelm apparent float benefits.

## Evaluate acquisition value by shareholder group
**When to use**: For a proposed merger, especially one paid with shares.  
**How**: Build combined pro forma statements, specify measurable synergies, value the combined firm, allocate shares and consideration, then compare value received and given up by each side.  
**Trade-offs**: Synergy realization and division are uncertain; EPS accretion is not a substitute.

## Locate default points with a liquidity book
**When to use**: When negative FCF, debt maturities, or refinancing risk could interrupt a going concern.  
**How**: Reconcile FCF to dividends and debt payments; forecast cash, investment, net debt, interest, and obligations by period; stress financing access and identify the first shortfall.  
**Trade-offs**: A business can be economically valuable yet fail before the payoff arrives.
