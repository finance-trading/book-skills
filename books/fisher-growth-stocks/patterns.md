# Patterns

## 15-Point Triage
**When to use**: Before spending time on a full model or management meeting.
**How**: Score market runway, innovation, R&D, sales, margins, people, accounting, advantage, earnings, financing, disclosure, and integrity. Investigate every weak answer; reject clusters of weakness.
**Trade-offs**: Qualitative judgment is slower than a screen but catches governance and execution risks that ratios miss.

## Scuttlebutt Loop
**When to use**: Facts about customers, competitors, product quality, or management are incomplete.
**How**: Form a hypothesis; interview several independent channels; ask follow-ups triggered by answers; record convergent and conflicting evidence; then test it with management.
**Trade-offs**: Time-intensive and vulnerable to sampling bias; require source diversity and contradiction checks.

## Margin Bridge
**When to use**: Sales are growing but the investment case depends on earnings growth.
**How**: Compare multi-year operating margins with peers; identify price, mix, scale, cost, R&D, and sales investments; ask what management is doing to protect or improve margins.
**Trade-offs**: Current margin can be depressed by productive investment; distinguish investment from structural deterioration.

## Temporary-Problem Entry
**When to use**: A high-quality company is disliked because a new plant, product, or sales ramp temporarily hurts earnings.
**How**: Verify the underlying 15-point profile; confirm the problem is finite and fixable; estimate the post-ramp earnings curve; buy only when evidence, price, and risk fit.
**Trade-offs**: “Temporary” is often rationalization; demand operational proof, not management optimism.

## Thesis-Based Exit
**When to use**: A holding has risen, lagged, or attracted market criticism.
**How**: Re-test the original facts. Exit for deteriorating management/integrity, exhausted market runway, broken margins, inadequate depth, excessive dilution, or a clearly superior opportunity.
**Trade-offs**: Opportunity-cost switches can improve returns but create tax, timing, and replacement-risk costs.
