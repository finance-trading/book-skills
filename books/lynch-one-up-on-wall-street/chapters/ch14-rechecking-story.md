# Chapter 14: Rechecking the Story

## Core Idea
A purchase begins a monitoring process. Every few months, Lynch compares new results and observations with the original reason for owning the stock.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Periodic recheck**: Use quarterly reports, updated research, earnings, and field observation on a regular cadence. The goal is detection of change, not constant trading.
- **Three growth phases**: Use start-up, rapid expansion, and maturity/saturation to locate a growth company. The expansion phase is often the most attractive because the company repeats a proven formula.
- **Card-turning test**: Use it when new facts appear. Ask whether the new card strengthens, weakens, or ends the story.

## Key Concepts
- **Start-up phase**: The period when the model is still being proven and risk is highest.
- **Rapid expansion**: Growth through repeated entry into new markets or locations.
- **Saturation**: The point at which easy expansion ends and a new source of growth is required.
- **Monitoring**: Scheduled comparison of results with expectations.
- **Story drift**: A gradual change in business model, returns, or category.

## Mental Models
- Think of each earnings report as a checkpoint, not a referendum on the market.
- Use phase changes to anticipate valuation and growth changes before they become obvious.
- Separate temporary noise from evidence that the replication engine is weakening.

## Anti-patterns
- **Set-and-forget ownership**: Holding for years without reading new reports is not long-term investing.
- **Overreacting to one quarter**: A single noisy period does not automatically break a multiyear story.
- **Ignoring saturation**: Assuming yesterday's expansion rate continues after markets are fully served.

## Worked Example
A fast grower expands a detection system from store to store and the stock rises sharply. On recheck, the investor finds few suitable new customers and no credible second product. That is a phase change from expansion toward saturation. The original story no longer supports the same growth multiple, so the investor revalues or sells rather than relying on the old trend.

## Key Takeaways
1. Recheck the story every few months.
2. Growth companies pass through materially different phases.
3. The expansion phase deserves the closest attention.
4. New facts should confirm, revise, or end the thesis.
