# Chapter 15: The Final Checklist

## Core Idea
The final review is category-specific. Lynch checks general financial health first, then applies the questions appropriate to slow growers, stalwarts, cyclicals, fast growers, turnarounds, or asset plays.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **General checklist**: Use it for every stock. Confirm the business story, recent sales and earnings trend, valuation relative to growth, cash and debt, insider behavior, share count, and the main competitive risk.
- **Category checklists**: Use the relevant subset before buying. Slow growers emphasize dividends; stalwarts emphasize price and resilience; cyclicals emphasize cycle position; fast growers emphasize runway and balance sheet; turnarounds emphasize survival; asset plays emphasize realizable asset value.
- **Final pass**: Use it after enthusiasm peaks. If a required answer is unknown, the research is not finished.

## Key Concepts
- **General screen**: The common facts checked regardless of category.
- **Payout discipline**: Dividend affordability and usefulness for mature companies.
- **Cycle position**: Whether profits are recovering, peaking, or collapsing.
- **Runway**: Room to continue expanding a proven model.
- **Realizable value**: Asset value that can actually be recognized by owners, net of liabilities and frictions.

## Mental Models
- Think of the checklist as a gate, not a scoreboard to be gamed.
- Use missing information as a reason to wait.
- Keep the final decision tied to one primary category even when a company has secondary traits.

## Anti-patterns
- **Averaging incompatible categories**: Mixing cyclical and fast-growth rules produces a thesis that cannot be monitored.
- **Checklist after purchase**: Using the list to rationalize a decision already made reverses its purpose.
- **Asset-value gross-up**: Counting gross assets while ignoring debt, taxes, costs, or the inability to realize value.

## Worked Example
A depressed manufacturer looks cheap. The final pass first rejects the vague label “cheap” and classifies it. If it is a cyclical, the investor checks inventories, pricing, capacity, and balance-sheet stamina. If it is a turnaround, the investor checks cash, debt maturities, and whether a specific operational fix is working. The purchase requires the correct classification and complete answers, not merely a fallen price.

## Key Takeaways
1. Finish with a written checklist.
2. General financial questions apply to all stocks.
3. Each category has distinct decisive questions.
4. Unknown answers mean the work is incomplete.
