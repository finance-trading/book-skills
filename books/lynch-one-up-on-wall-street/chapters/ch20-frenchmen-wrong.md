# Chapter 20: 50,000 Frenchmen Can Be Wrong

## Core Idea
A large crowd can share the same mistaken investment belief. Lynch argues for independent verification even when sophisticated people, institutions, or entire markets agree.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Consensus audit**: Use this when “everyone knows” a stock, industry, or economic conclusion. Identify the evidence behind the consensus and the incentive each group has to repeat it.
- **Independent verification**: Use primary business facts to confirm or reject popular opinion. Agreement is relevant only to the extent its participants independently know something.
- **Social-proof resistance**: Use this at extremes of optimism or pessimism. The number of believers does not change earnings, cash flow, debt, or competitive position.

## Key Concepts
- **Consensus**: A widely shared market opinion.
- **Social proof**: Treating popularity as evidence of correctness.
- **Independent judgment**: A conclusion supported by facts the investor personally understands.
- **Crowded idea**: A thesis whose recognition may already be reflected in price.
- **Contrarian discipline**: Disagreeing only when evidence supports the disagreement.

## Mental Models
- Think of consensus as a hypothesis generator, then audit its source.
- Use crowds to identify what is priced in, not what is true.
- Prefer a documented minority view to an undocumented majority view.

## Anti-patterns
- **Appeal to numbers**: “Thousands of investors own it” does not establish value.
- **Reflexive rebellion**: Being contrary without evidence is another form of following a rule blindly.
- **Delegated conviction**: Owning a stock only because a respected group owns it leaves you unable to act rationally when the group changes its mind.

## Worked Example
A widely owned growth favorite falls after earnings miss expectations. Many investors repeat that so many professionals cannot be wrong and hold for that reason. The better test is independent: Did the company lose its expansion runway, or did a temporary issue leave long-term earnings intact? The crowd's size does not answer the question.

## Key Takeaways
1. Large groups can be collectively wrong.
2. Consensus must be traced back to evidence.
3. Popularity can mean the opportunity is already priced.
4. Independent judgment requires the ability to explain the facts alone.
