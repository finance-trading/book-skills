# Chapter 17: The Best Time to Buy and Sell

## Core Idea
The best transactions are driven by company facts and valuation, not by predicting short-term market movements. Sell rules differ across Lynch's six categories.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Buy on fundamentals**: Use this during market scares and ordinary conditions alike. Buy when the story is sound and the price offers a margin relative to expected earnings or assets.
- **Category sell rules**: Use the rule matching the original thesis. Slow growers are sold when dividends, market position, or fundamentals weaken; stalwarts when valuation normalizes or fundamentals deteriorate; cyclicals near evidence of a cycle peak; fast growers when expansion ends, debt rises, or valuation detaches from growth; turnarounds when recovery is complete or fails; asset plays when the value is recognized or shown to be unrealizable.
- **Drumbeat effect**: Use it to resist repeated alarming headlines. Distinguish persistent news noise from a genuine change in earnings power or balance-sheet strength.

## Key Concepts
- **Fundamental buy**: A purchase justified by business value rather than market timing.
- **False alarm**: A price decline or headline that does not alter the thesis.
- **Genuine sell**: A sale caused by broken fundamentals, excessive valuation, or completed recognition.
- **Cycle peak**: The point where cyclical margins, prices, or capacity signal unsustainably strong earnings.
- **Recovery completion**: The stage when a turnaround has become an ordinary business and should be revalued as such.

## Mental Models
- Think of selling as thesis maintenance, not emotional relief.
- Use the same evidence standard for buying, holding, and selling.
- Separate market-wide panic from company-specific deterioration.

## Anti-patterns
- **Selling only because price rose**: A gain does not establish that value has been exhausted.
- **Holding only because price fell**: Loss aversion cannot repair a broken story.
- **One universal sell rule**: A cyclical peak and a fast grower's temporary correction require different responses.

## Worked Example
A fast grower's shares fall during a broad market break, but new stores continue to earn target returns, debt remains manageable, and the valuation becomes more attractive. That supports buying or holding. If instead same-store returns decline, expansion opportunities shrink, and management begins unrelated acquisitions, the category rule says to sell even if the broader market recovers.

## Key Takeaways
1. Buy from a company story and price, not a market forecast.
2. Headlines require a fundamental filter.
3. Each category has its own sell trigger.
4. Completed, failed, or overpriced stories should be sold.
