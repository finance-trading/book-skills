# Chapter 7: I've Got It, I've Got It—What Is It?

## Core Idea
Before judging a stock, classify the company. Lynch's six categories determine which facts matter, what counts as success, and which risks deserve attention.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Six stock categories**: Use this immediately after a lead. Label the company as a slow grower, stalwart, fast grower, cyclical, turnaround, or asset play. Revisit the label when the business changes.
- **Bottom-line first**: Use it to move from “interesting company” to “how earnings or assets create value.”
- **Category migration**: Use it when a fast grower matures, a cyclical weakens, or an asset play becomes an operating story. The old thesis and sell rule may no longer fit.

## Key Concepts
- **Slow grower**: A mature company, often with a dividend and limited expansion.
- **Stalwart**: A large, relatively dependable company with moderate growth.
- **Fast grower**: A company with a long runway for above-average expansion.
- **Cyclical**: A business whose profits fluctuate strongly with economic or industry cycles.
- **Turnaround**: A troubled company where survival and repair drive the return.
- **Asset play**: A company whose underappreciated assets may be worth more than the market price implies.

## Mental Models
- Think of category as the lens that selects the checklist.
- Use size, industry growth, earnings pattern, debt, and assets to classify; do not rely on the company's self-description.
- Expect the best long-term upside from a fast grower that can replicate a proven model, but demand evidence that the runway remains open.

## Anti-patterns
- **One checklist for every stock**: A low P/E may help a stalwart but mislead badly in a cyclical downturn or a failing turnaround.
- **Confusing company quality with category**: A beloved brand can still be a slow grower or an overpriced stalwart.
- **Ignoring distress risk**: A cyclical that deteriorates too far can become a bankruptcy candidate rather than a normal rebound.

## Worked Example
A retailer grows quickly in a mature industry by taking share and expanding a proven format. It is not automatically a cyclical merely because retail is familiar, nor a stalwart merely because the industry is old. If growth is driven by replication and the balance sheet supports expansion, Lynch would analyze it primarily as a fast grower—until saturation changes the story.

## Key Takeaways
1. Classification comes before valuation and portfolio weight.
2. The six categories have different evidence, expectations, and failure modes.
3. Companies can move between categories.
4. The category label must be rechecked against current results.
