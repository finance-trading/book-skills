# Chapter 12: Getting the Facts

## Core Idea
The facts an individual investor needs are mostly available through reports, direct questions, and observation. Lynch treats research as a practical collection process, not a privileged Wall Street ritual.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Public-evidence ladder**: Use filings and reports first, then the broker, company contacts, and personal observation. Give primary documents more weight than rumors.
- **Broker as researcher**: Use a full-service broker for reports, data, and access, not merely order entry. The investor remains responsible for the conclusion.
- **Kick the tires**: Use store visits, product use, supplier checks, and local observation to test whether reported strength is visible in operations.

## Key Concepts
- **Annual report**: The baseline source for business description, financials, and management discussion.
- **Quarterly report**: The update used to detect change in the story.
- **Investor relations**: A source for factual clarification, not a substitute for independent judgment.
- **Grassroots research**: Evidence gathered from customers, stores, products, competitors, or workplaces.
- **Headquarters visit**: A way to assess candor, plans, and whether results match presentation.

## Mental Models
- Think of research as triangulating documents, people, and physical evidence.
- Use management statements as claims requiring verification.
- Prefer one independently checked fact over ten repeated opinions.

## Anti-patterns
- **Rumor premium**: Giving a vague overheard comment more weight than audited disclosures.
- **Passive full-service relationship**: Paying for advice or access but asking only for hot ideas.
- **Tour blindness**: Letting a polished headquarters visit override weak numbers.

## Worked Example
An investor interested in a retailer reads the annual report, notes expansion plans and debt, asks the broker for the latest filing and competitor comparison, calls investor relations with specific questions about sales per store, and visits several locations to compare traffic, pricing, and service. The purchase decision uses the convergence—or contradiction—among these sources.

## Key Takeaways
1. Individual investors can obtain most relevant facts from public sources.
2. Brokers and companies should be questioned, not obeyed.
3. Operational observation tests the paper story.
4. Rumors rank below documents and direct evidence.
