# Chapter 11: The Two-Minute Drill

## Core Idea
A stock thesis should be explainable in roughly two minutes. The short version exposes missing facts and provides the baseline for later monitoring.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two-Minute Drill**: Use this before deep work or immediately after a first pass. State the category, what the company does, how it makes money, why earnings can change, the key valuation fact, the principal risk, and what will prove you wrong.
- **Story card**: Use it to keep the thesis stable as prices fluctuate. Update the card only when business facts change.
- **La Quinta-style expansion check**: Use a lodging or retail expansion story as a model: identify the profitable unit, the number of suitable locations left, competition, and whether replication still earns the original return.

## Key Concepts
- **Story**: A compact causal account linking operations to future earnings or asset value.
- **Triage**: A short decision to continue, wait, or reject.
- **Replication**: Growth by repeating a proven unit rather than inventing a new business.
- **Falsifier**: A specific fact that would break the thesis.
- **Baseline**: The original expectation against which later reports are compared.

## Mental Models
- Think of the drill as an elevator pitch you must be able to defend, not marketing language.
- Use inability to explain the company as evidence that research is incomplete.
- Reduce every long report to the few variables that control the outcome.

## Anti-patterns
- **Storyless ownership**: Buying first and inventing the rationale later.
- **Essay without numbers**: A narrative that omits earnings, valuation, debt, or runway is not complete.
- **Changing the story after losses**: Reframing the reason for ownership merely to justify holding adds bias.

## Worked Example
“La Quinta is a lodging expansion story. It builds a standardized inn format in markets where occupancy and room economics are attractive, grows by repeating the format, and can keep growing while suitable sites remain and returns on new inns exceed the cost of capital. The thesis fails if new units earn materially less, expansion sites run out, leverage rises unsafely, or the price assumes perfection.” This is the required level of specificity before a full investigation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Every holding needs a short, testable story.
2. The story must include category, economics, valuation, and a falsifier.
3. The drill screens out vague enthusiasm quickly.
4. Later reviews compare results with the original story.
