# Patterns

## Lead → Story → Numbers → Price
**When to use**: Any time a product, store, employer, supplier, or tip attracts attention.
**How**: Record the lead; classify the company; write a two-minute story; verify earnings, assets, cash, debt, and growth; compare value with price; define what would falsify the thesis.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than buying on enthusiasm, but converts familiarity into a monitorable decision.

## Six-Category Routing
**When to use**: Before applying a valuation or sell rule.
**How**: Route the company to slow grower, stalwart, fast grower, cyclical, turnaround, or asset play. Use the category-specific checklist and revise the label when the business changes.
**Trade-offs**: Simplifies analysis but can obscure mixed businesses; choose the dominant value driver.

## Two-Minute Drill
**When to use**: For first-pass triage and before adding to a holding.
**How**: State what the company does, category, earnings mechanism, runway/catalyst, valuation fact, key risk, and falsifier in a few sentences.
**Trade-offs**: Fast and revealing, but it is not a substitute for full research.

## Public-Evidence Ladder
**When to use**: When a thesis relies on rumors or a broker's idea.
**How**: Start with annual and quarterly reports; then use industry material, broker data, investor relations, management calls, site visits, and grassroots observation to triangulate.
**Trade-offs**: Public evidence is slower than a tip but more auditable and repeatable.

## Growth-Phase Check
**When to use**: When monitoring a fast grower.
**How**: Identify start-up, rapid expansion, or maturity/saturation. Check whether the proven model still has room to replicate and whether a new growth engine exists after saturation.
**Trade-offs**: Avoids extrapolation but may sell too early if a temporary slowdown is mistaken for maturity.

## Category-Specific Sell Rule
**When to use**: When deciding whether a price decline, gain, or headline requires action.
**How**: Return to the original category and story. Sell when the story breaks, valuation detaches from credible results, recovery is complete, or an asset is recognized/unrealizable according to the category.
**Trade-offs**: More precise than a universal percentage stop, but requires honest monitoring.

## Portfolio Gardening
**When to use**: During rebalancing or when a loser tempts an additional purchase.
**How**: Compare each holding's facts, valuation, and monitoring priority. Water strong, still-attractive stories; remove weeds; diversify underlying risks rather than ticker count.
**Trade-offs**: Concentrates attention and may increase single-name risk; excessive diversification can make every holding irrelevant.

## Price-Fallacy Filter
**When to use**: When the argument is “it has fallen,” “it is only $X,” or “it cannot rise further.”
**How**: Replace the price claim with earnings, assets, debt, cash, growth, and expectations. Decide from business value, not nominal price or a guessed bottom.
**Trade-offs**: Reduces anchoring, but still depends on uncertain forecasts.
