# Lynch Decision Cheatsheet

## Fast route

| If you see… | Do this | Because |
|---|---|---|
| A product/store/business you like | Record it as a lead; do not buy | Familiarity is a research edge, not proof |
| A broker tip or market rumor | Ask for the public, testable story | Mystery is not evidence |
| “Is this a good market?” | Return to company, earnings, price | Market forecasts distract from business facts |
| “It has fallen a lot” | Recalculate value and survival | A stock can fall to zero |
| “It is only $3” | Check market cap, shares, earnings, debt | Nominal price is arbitrary |
| A new holding | Write a two-minute story and falsifier | You cannot monitor a thesis you cannot state |

## Classify before you value

- **Slow grower** → focus on dividend support, modest growth, and whether the mature thesis still holds.
- **Stalwart** → demand resilience and a sensible price; do not pay a fast-grower multiple for moderate growth.
- **Fast grower** → verify runway, repeatable unit economics, per-share earnings, and balance-sheet funding.
- **Cyclical** → identify cycle position, inventory, pricing, capacity, and peak-versus-normal earnings.
- **Turnaround** → check survival first: cash, debt maturities, operational fix, and recovery evidence.
- **Asset play** → discount liabilities and realization friction; identify how value reaches owners.

## Research gate

1. Can I explain the business in plain language?
2. What category is it, and what is the specific value driver?
3. Are sales, earnings, and cash moving as the story requires?
4. What do P/E, growth, debt, margins, share count, and assets imply together?
5. Why might the market be missing it?
6. What precise fact would make me sell?

If any answer is unknown, wait or keep researching.

## Monitoring and selling

- Recheck reports and operating evidence every few months.
- **Hold/add** when the original story remains true and price remains reasonable.
- **Revalue** when the company moves from expansion toward saturation or changes category.
- **Sell** when the story breaks, balance-sheet risk rises, valuation assumes unrealistic growth, recovery completes, or asset value is recognized/unrealizable.
- Do not sell solely because the price rose; do not hold solely because it fell.

## Portfolio defaults

- Own only as many stories as you can follow.
- Diversify underlying business risks, not just tickers.
- Water strong, understandable ideas; do not automatically average down weeds.
- Treat options, futures, and shorts as a complexity and timing penalty unless you have a specific, durable edge.

## Hard guardrails

- Historical examples and source-era ratios are not current recommendations.
- Verify current filings, price, debt, share count, taxes, liquidity, and risk capacity.
- Never use material nonpublic information from employment or industry access.
