# Glossary

**Asset play** — A company whose underappreciated assets may be worth more than the market value (Ch 7, Ch 15).

**Balance sheet** — Statement of assets, liabilities, and equity used to assess resilience and funding (Ch 10, Ch 13).

**Cyclical** — A company whose earnings follow an industry or economic cycle; timing and peak earnings matter (Ch 7, Ch 15).

**Diworsification** — Diversification that weakens a business or misallocates shareholder capital (Ch 9).

**Earnings** — Business profit attributable to owners; the primary long-term driver of stock value (Ch 10).

**Fast grower** — A company with a credible runway for above-average earnings growth (Ch 7, Ch 14).

**Insider buying** — Open-market purchases by company insiders; a supporting alignment signal, not proof (Ch 8).

**P/E ratio** — Price per share divided by earnings per share; meaningful only in growth, quality, and cycle context (Ch 10).

**PEG-style comparison** — A rough comparison of P/E with expected earnings growth; a screen, not a valuation law (Ch 10).

**Rechecking the story** — Periodic comparison of operating facts with the original investment thesis (Ch 14).

**Slow grower** — A mature company with limited expansion and often a meaningful dividend (Ch 7).

**Stalwart** — A large, relatively resilient company with moderate growth (Ch 7).

**Street lag** — Delayed professional recognition of a visible but underfollowed business trend (Ch 2).

**Tenbagger** — A stock that multiplies in value manyfold; an outcome sought through research, not a forecast (Ch 6).

**The Cocktail Theory** — A qualitative sentiment gauge based on how much party conversation centers on stocks and tips (Ch 5).

**The Mirror Test** — Questions about financial readiness, liquidity, and temperament before owning stocks (Ch 4).

**The Two-Minute Drill** — A compact explanation of category, business, earnings path, valuation, risk, and falsifier (Ch 11).

**Turnaround** — A troubled company whose return depends on survival and operational recovery (Ch 7).

**Watering the weeds** — Adding capital to weak holdings while neglecting stronger ideas (Ch 16).
