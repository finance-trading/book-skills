# Patterns — The Outsiders

## Rank Every Use of Capital
**When to use**: Cash is available or a large financing decision approaches.

**How**: Put internal reinvestment, acquisitions, own shares, debt repayment, and distributions in one comparison. Estimate after-tax return, capital required, downside, and effect on value per share with conservative assumptions. Keep cash when none clears the hurdle.

**Trade-offs**: Estimates are uncertain; a short model focuses judgment but cannot eliminate operational or regulatory risk (Ch 9; Epilogue).

## Buyback as an Investment
**When to use**: Shares may trade below conservative intrinsic value.

**How**: Estimate value per share; compare expected return with acquisitions and internal uses; test cash and debt capacity; buy in meaningful size only while the price remains attractive. Singleton used large tenders; Graham and Stiritz used opportunistic purchases.

**Trade-offs**: Price and balance-sheet risk matter. A buyback at an exorbitant price destroys owner value (Ch 2, 5–6).

## Acquisition with an Operating Thesis
**When to use**: A target fits a business the operator understands and an identifiable improvement justifies the price.

**How**: Name the specific margin, cost, or growth change; identify managers who can deliver it; set a purchase ceiling and integration capacity; finance conservatively; measure realized cash flow after closing.

**Trade-offs**: High leverage magnifies integration mistakes. Murphy's ABC deal depended on a tested operating team, not merely a low purchase multiple (Ch 1, 4).

## Shrink to Create Value
**When to use**: Demand or asset economics have changed structurally.

**How**: Recalculate prospective returns for each unit; stop low-return projects; sell or close assets; return proceeds unless a better investment exists. Anders's post-Cold War General Dynamics is the principal case.

**Trade-offs**: Premature sales may sacrifice future recovery; selling price and tax effects must be compared with holding value (Ch 3, 6, 8).

## Decentralize Operations, Centralize Allocation
**When to use**: Many local businesses need fast operating decisions while capital remains scarce at the parent level.

**How**: Hire accountable general managers; set economic targets; keep headquarters lean; retain CEO authority over major cash uses, financing, and portfolio changes.

**Trade-offs**: Weak reporting or incentives can hide poor unit performance; too much central control can remove local initiative (Ch 1–2, 4–5, 8–9).

## Tax-Aware Deal Structure
**When to use**: Debt, asset sales, stock consideration, or spinoffs can materially change after-tax returns.

**How**: Calculate the operating return first; model taxes, interest, NOLs, and liabilities; review the structure with tax counsel; compare after-tax outcomes per share.

**Trade-offs**: Tax rules change and transaction complexity can obscure weak economics (Ch 4, 6–7; Epilogue).

## Test Management in Context
**When to use**: Comparing CEOs or assessing a historical record.

**How**: Use compound shareholder return over identical dates, then compare with a broad market index and a relevant peer basket; inspect starting valuations, industry conditions, and the share count. Apply the appendix's Buffett Test as one additional check.

**Trade-offs**: Peer selection and start/end dates can change the result; market price is an imperfect measure of management quality (Preface; Ch 9; Appendix).
