## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The allocation toolkit
The CEO has five main destinations for capital: reinvest in existing operations, acquire businesses, pay dividends, reduce debt, or repurchase shares. There are three principal funding sources: operating cash, borrowing, or issuing equity. Put all feasible uses into one risk-adjusted, after-tax comparison. The correct action changes with price and circumstance; Thorndike's CEOs did not follow a standing rule to always buy back stock or always acquire.

### Always Do the Math
Start with the expected return, a defensible hurdle, and the few assumptions that determine the outcome. Use a short investment case to make the alternatives visible. Conservative assumptions matter more than an elaborate spreadsheet. Where uncertainty is greater, require more return or less capital at risk. If no project clears the test, wait rather than manufacture growth. [Ch 9](chapters/ch09-radical-rationality.md) and the book's Epilogue give the process.

### The Denominator Matters
Judge value **per share** as well as total company value. Issuing richly valued shares can be a favorable acquisition currency; buying undervalued shares can be a superior investment. Reverse the choice when valuations reverse. Singleton's high-priced issuance and later large tenders make the point concrete. A repurchase at an inflated price or with unsafe debt can reduce per-share value. [Ch 2](chapters/ch02-singleton-teledyne.md), [Ch 9](chapters/ch09-radical-rationality.md).

### Decentralized operations, centralized allocation
Give capable unit managers authority over customers, costs, and daily operations. Keep the CEO accountable for company-wide capital deployment, financing, and portfolio shape. A lean center can avoid overhead and preserve local information, but only with clear economic reporting and incentives. Murphy's partnership with Burke, Singleton's Teledyne, and Buffett's Berkshire illustrate different forms. [Ch 1](chapters/ch01-murphy-capital-cities.md), [Ch 2](chapters/ch02-singleton-teledyne.md), [Ch 8](chapters/ch08-buffett-berkshire.md).

### Crocodile-like patience, occasional bold action
Wait for a high-probability opportunity that clears the hurdle. When one appears, size the decision to its economics and financing capacity rather than to what peers expect. Graham skipped much of the newspaper acquisition frenzy; Murphy made a transformative ABC purchase only with an operational thesis; Anders shrank a defense company when the industry changed. [Ch 5](chapters/ch05-graham-washington-post.md), [Ch 1](chapters/ch01-murphy-capital-cities.md), [Ch 3](chapters/ch03-anders-general-dynamics.md).

### Judge the CEO's record in context
Compare the same tenure's compound shareholder return with a broad market benchmark and a relevant peer basket. Check starting and ending valuations, share issuance and repurchases, and industry conditions. The book's appendix offers the **Buffett Test**: did each dollar of retained earnings create at least one dollar of owner value over the tenure? This is a retrospective clue, not a substitute for a forward return model. [Ch 9](chapters/ch09-radical-rationality.md), [patterns](patterns.md).
