# Glossary — The Outsiders

**Acquisition hurdle** — Minimum risk-adjusted return required before buying a business; should be compared with other uses of capital (Ch 1, 5, 9).

**Asset pruning** — Selling or closing operations with inferior prospective returns, regardless of their historical status (Ch 3, 6, 8).

**Buffett Test** — The appendix's retrospective question: did each dollar of retained earnings create at least a dollar of shareholder value over a CEO's tenure? Market prices and timing require care when applying it (Appendix; Ch 8–9).

**Capital allocation** — Choosing among reinvestment, acquisitions, dividends, debt reduction, and buybacks, and funding with operating cash, debt, or equity (Preface; Ch 1–9).

**Cash-flow multiple** — Asset price divided by a consistently defined measure of operating cash generation (Ch 1, 4, 7).

**Cost of float** — Underwriting economics associated with using premiums before insurance claims are paid (Ch 8).

**Cross-collateralization** — Debt structure that pledges multiple assets together and can spread trouble from one unit to another (Ch 4).

**Decentralized operations** — Routine operating authority held near local units, with managers accountable for results (Ch 1–2, 4–5, 8–9).

**Denominator** — The share count beneath value per share; repurchases and issuances alter it (Ch 2, 9).

**Equity currency** — Stock used to fund an acquisition; its valuation changes the real cost to existing owners (Ch 2–3).

**Free cash flow** — Cash available after operations and necessary investment, subject to the definitions used by each company (Ch 1–8).

**Hurdle rate** — Minimum acceptable prospective return; set with available alternatives, risk, and cost of capital in view (Ch 5, 9; Epilogue).

**Institutional imperative** — Pressure to follow peer behavior or preserve scale despite poor return arithmetic (Ch 3, 9).

**Insurance float** — Premium funds held before claims are paid; an investable funding source that carries liabilities (Ch 8).

**Internal rate of return (IRR)** — Discount rate equating an investment's dated cash flows to its cost; used in the book's historical deal and repurchase comparisons (Ch 6–7).

**Margin of safety** — Difference between conservative assessed value and the price paid (Ch 8).

**NOL** — Net operating loss tax carryforward, potentially useful for sheltering taxable gains under applicable tax rules (Ch 4).

**One-pager** — Short investment case exposing key return assumptions, alternatives, and risks (Ch 9).

**Opportunity cost** — Return forgone by choosing one capital use over the best other option (Ch 1–9).

**Per-share value** — Value attributable to each remaining share, distinct from total company size (Ch 2, 9).

**Public LBO** — Thorndike's term for Stiritz's use of leverage, portfolio pruning, and repurchases inside a public company (Ch 6).

**Repurchase return** — Return to remaining owners from buying shares at a given price instead of the next-best capital use (Ch 2, 5–6, 9).

**Special dividend** — One-off distribution of excess capital when repeatable reinvestment returns are insufficient (Ch 3).

**Spinoff** — Distribution of a subsidiary to parent shareholders, creating separate ownership and capital decision-making (Ch 6).

**Tender offer** — Offer to buy a substantial share block on stated terms within a limited period (Ch 2–3).
