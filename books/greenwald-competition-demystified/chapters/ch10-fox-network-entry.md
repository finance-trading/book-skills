# Chapter 10: Into the Henhouse — Fox Becomes a Network

## Core Idea
Entry into a protected market is possible when an entrant finds a feasible niche and incumbents choose not to fight destructively. The entrant's prospects still depend on local affiliates, content, advertisers, and changing technology.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Network Industry Map**: Separate program production, national network, local stations, advertisers, and viewers.
  - When to use: Evaluating media distribution or two-sided network entry.
  - How: Test each link's incumbents, switching costs, scale, and contractual bottlenecks.
- **Entry-Response Analysis**: Compare incumbent accommodation and retaliation with the entrant's persistence and scale path.
  - When to use: A new competitor challenges a small incumbent club.
  - How: Identify the entrant's minimum affiliate and programming base; estimate the incumbent's cost of resistance; monitor which barriers erode as rules or viewing habits change.

## Key Concepts
- **Affiliate**: A local station carrying network programming.
- **Network scale**: Fixed programming and national advertising costs spread over audience reach.
- **Viewer captivity**: Habit around shows and network schedules.
- **Barriers erosion**: Technology or regulation that makes old distribution bottlenecks less binding.

## Mental Models
- Think **several links must align**: Buying a studio alone does not confer the network's customer and affiliate position.
- Use **dynamic barriers**: Cable, VCRs, remote controls, and regulatory change can alter the incumbent advantage.

## Anti-patterns
- **Invoking vague studio-network synergy**: Trace a specific saving or customer benefit.
- **Assuming old share stability survives a technology shift**: Re-test captivity and distribution after new channels emerge.
- **Underestimating launch financing**: Entry requires enough stations, programs, and advertisers to reach viability.

## Worked Example
Rupert Murdoch assembled Fox in the 1980s through a group of local stations and a film studio, then built a fourth U.S. network. ABC, CBS, and NBC had entrenched audience, advertiser, and affiliate positions, but they did not launch a ruinous advertising price war against Fox. The case asks whether an entrant can assemble a viable minimum network and whether incumbents' rational responses leave room for it. It also shows that later technology can weaken all networks' original barriers.

## Key Takeaways
1. Decompose distribution and content before claiming synergy.
2. Analyze incumbent response as part of entry economics.
3. Budget the minimum system needed to make entry credible.
4. Refresh barrier tests when technology and regulation change.
