# Decision Cheatsheet

| If you see… | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| High returns plus stable share in a precisely bounded market | Test supply, demand, and scale barriers | Persistent profits may signal protection, but require a mechanism. |
| Many competitors can enter with similar resources | Prioritize operating efficiency | Entry erodes excess returns. |
| A few protected rivals repeatedly change prices | Map best responses in a payoff matrix | Each firm's result depends on the other's choice. |
| A durable entry or capacity commitment | Draw a decision tree and work backward | Later incentives determine whether threats are credible. |
| A niche entrant plans rapid expansion | Recalculate incumbent loss at each scale step | The original tolerance may disappear. |
| Corporate strength is offered as proof of adjacent-market entry | Rebuild the moat test for the target market | Advantage is local to customers, product, and geography. |
| A platform extracts more from complementors | Model the next technology reset | The installed base and partner loyalty may not survive it. |
| EPV exceeds asset reproduction value | Name the exact entry barrier | The excess requires protection to persist. |
| EPV is below asset value | Challenge expansion; seek operational fixes | More capital at weak returns destroys value. |
| A growth forecast earns below the capital cost | Reject growth value | Higher earnings can still mean lower shareholder value. |
| An acquisition claims “synergy” | Itemize cost savings; compare with premium and contracts | Vague revenue gains rarely justify ownership cost. |

## Fast sequence

1. **Bound the market**: product, customer, and geography.
2. **Test entry**: can a capable rival reproduce the position?
3. **Locate the barrier**: supply, customer captivity, or local scale.
4. **Choose the game**: matrix for repeated reversible moves; tree for sequential durable moves.
5. **Value separately**: assets → normalized EPV → protected growth.
6. **Recheck after expansion**: crossing a market or scale boundary changes the answer.

For cooperation, verify each party beats its outside option and keep proposed conduct within competition law. Historical descriptions in the book are cases to analyze, not instructions to coordinate prices or markets.
