# Glossary

**Asset value** — Reproduction value of a firm's present resources, net of obligations (Ch 16).

**Barrier to entry** — A condition that prevents entrants or expanding rivals from reproducing an incumbent's economic position (Ch 1–4).

**BATNA / threat point** — A party's best feasible outcome without an agreement; the floor for sustainable cooperation (Ch 14).

**Brand asset** — Recognition or customer association that may yield income but needs maintenance and does not by itself block entry (Ch 17).

**Complementor** — A supplier whose product or service makes another firm's system more valuable (Ch 15).

**Customer captivity** — Repeat buying protected by habit, switching costs, search costs, or installed equipment (Ch 2–3, 13).

**Economies of scale** — Lower cost per unit as fixed costs are spread over larger relevant-market volume (Ch 3, 5).

**Earnings power value (EPV)** — Value of normalized maintainable earnings without assigning value to growth (Ch 16).

**Entry/preemption game** — Sequential choice model for capacity, entry, and incumbent response (Ch 11–13).

**Franchise value** — EPV above asset reproduction value, sustainable only where a barrier protects the excess return (Ch 16).

**Individual rationality** — Every participant receives at least its noncooperative payoff (Ch 14).

**Installed base** — Existing users or complementary equipment that can attract follow-on purchases and suppliers (Ch 13, 15).

**Linear invariance** — Nash fairness condition allocating cooperative gains in proportion to legitimate relative economic positions (Ch 14).

**Local economies of scale** — Scale advantage confined to a geographic or product market, such as store density or distribution reach (Ch 3, 5).

**Market-space locality** — An advantage protects a particular product, customer, or geography rather than the entire corporation (Ch 5–6, 13).

**Normal-form game** — Payoff matrix representing simultaneous choices and best responses (Ch 8–9).

**Operational effectiveness** — Performing available activities better, which can raise earnings without an exclusive moat (Ch 1, 18).

**Preemption** — Durable investment intended to change another firm's entry incentives (Ch 11).

**Prisoner's dilemma** — Game in which individually rational moves produce an inferior joint outcome (Ch 8–9, 15).

**Reproduction cost** — Cost for a rival to assemble assets capable of matching an incumbent's output (Ch 16).

**Scale trigger** — Expansion point at which an entrant becomes material enough to provoke incumbent response (Ch 12).

**Strategic decision** — A choice whose payoff depends materially on other actors' reactions, regardless of its budget (Ch 1).

**Symmetry** — Nash fairness condition that essentially equal claimants divide a joint surplus equally (Ch 14).

**Takeover premium** — Acquisition price paid above the target's stand-alone value, requiring incremental gains to justify it (Ch 17).

**Virtuous circle** — Reinforcement among platform adoption, complementor participation, and customer demand (Ch 15).
