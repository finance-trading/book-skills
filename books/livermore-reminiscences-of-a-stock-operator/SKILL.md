## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. Trade with the general market, not individual stocks
First determine whether the general market is in a bull or bear phase, then choose direction and stocks within it. The big money is made in big movements driven by underlying conditions, not by constantly scalping small fluctuations. Use `ch05`, `ch08`.

### 2. Line of least resistance
Prices tend to continue along the path of least resistance. Judge the direction by comparing the speed, breadth, and quality of reactions rather than by opinion or news. If a stock behaves badly, leave it alone rather than force an explanation. Use `ch10`, `ch05`.

### 3. Timing before action
Do not trade every day. Wait for the market to prove your view, then act quickly. When you are right, the trade should show a paper profit almost immediately; a trade that goes against you from the start is a warning. Use `ch08`, `ch03`, `ch07`.

### 4. Pyramiding into strength, never averaging down
Add to a position only at successively higher prices (long) or lower prices (short), after the market has confirmed your judgment. Never add to a losing position to lower your average cost. Use `ch07`, `ch02`.

### 5. Independent judgment: facts, not opinions or tips
Own your facts: only what you have observed and can restate should drive a trade. Be skeptical of experts, friends, insiders, and tips — especially people you respect. Convert any suggestion into observable market conditions and wait for price confirmation. Use `ch08`, `ch12`, `ch16`.

### 6. Cut losses, let winners run
A small realized loss is the tuition; a wrong position that you refuse to exit is the disaster. Protect capital and the ability to act. Do not throw good money after bad, and do not hold losers in the hope of getting even. Use `ch03`, `ch10`, `ch17`.

### 7. Protect action freedom: avoid debt and controlling relationships
Debt, credit, and obligations to others can take away your ability to act on your own judgment even when you are right. Capital is a claim on future options. Use `ch13`, `ch14`.

### 8. Trade the leaders, not the cheapest stocks
In the same direction, choose the strongest, clearest, most liquid stocks. Price alone is not value; low price is not low risk. Use `ch21`, `ch07`.

### 9. Distribution and public psychology
Rising prices, active volume, tips, and favorable stories can be the machinery of distribution: insiders and marketers use the public as the exit liquidity. Ask who benefits from your belief, and let price and supply-demand facts outweigh narrative. Use `ch20`, `ch22`, `ch23`, `ch24`.

### 10. Human nature does not change
"Wall Street has no new things." Speculation is as old as the hills; greed, fear, and crowd behavior repeat under new names and new packaging. Study the mechanism, not the headline. Use `ch18`, `ch01`.

## Scope & Limits

This skill covers the content of the Chinese revised edition of *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* (Edwin Lefevre, translated by 丁圣元). It is a study of Livermore's trading *frameworks* — trend, timing, pyramiding, discipline, and market psychology — not a biography and not investment advice.

Historical events, prices, and figures are source-era illustrations from the book and its translator's annotations; they are not current market data. The appendices are the translator's compilation of Livermore's trading rules and timeline.

For the author's own perspective and voice, see the related `jesse-livermore-perspective` skill. For hands-on trading implementation, combine with your own risk and position-sizing rules — this skill extracts judgment, not a mechanical system.
