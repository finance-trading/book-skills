## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Find the right hunting ground, then do the work
Look for corporate events and securities that are too small, awkward, complex, or transient for many institutional investors. Structural neglect can cause forced selling, limited coverage, or an overlooked security. It is a reason to investigate, not proof of undervaluation. The edge is research that establishes both why the market may be wrong and what the risk-adjusted value actually is. See [Ch 1](chapters/ch01-follow-the-yellow-brick-road.md) and [Ch 2](chapters/ch02-some-basics.md).

### Choose spots; protect the downside
Do your own work, distrust both promoters and reflexive skeptics, and focus on what can go wrong before counting upside. Cash is a valid holding when available opportunities are poor. A narrow group of well-understood ideas is better than broad exposure to weak theses, but concentration raises the cost of an analytical error. See [Ch 2](chapters/ch02-some-basics.md).

### Corporate events create researchable mispricings
- **Spinoffs / rights**: Read the Form 10 or proxy, identify mandate-driven sellers and insider incentives, map debt and standalone costs, and value parent, child, and rights separately. See [Ch 3](chapters/ch03-spinoffs-and-rights-offerings.md).
- **Merger arbitrage / merger securities**: A spread compensates for break and timing risk. Model the break price, deal conditions, hedge unwind, and holding period. Unusual consideration may be forced-sold, but its contract and credit must be valued. See [Ch 4](chapters/ch04-risk-arbitrage-and-merger-securities.md).
- **Bankruptcy / restructuring**: Avoid assuming old equity has value. Map claim priority and recovery, distinguish an overleveraged good business from a broken one, and require a credible catalyst and downside support. Treat an event trade differently from a long-term investment. See [Ch 5](chapters/ch05-bankruptcy-and-restructuring.md).
- **Recaps / options**: Analyze the underlying business and capital structure before using leverage. A stub can be impaired by debt; an option can expire worthless even when the thesis is eventually right. See [Ch 6](chapters/ch06-recaps-stubs-and-options.md).

### Research from primary sources; price cash economics
Use articles, fund portfolios, and newsletters as leads, not conclusions. Verify transaction terms in filings and proxies. Compare reported earnings with free cash flow over time, adjusting for capital expenditure and working capital. A simple book screening measure is net income plus depreciation and amortization less capital expenditures; understand whether investment is maintenance or growth before drawing conclusions. See [Ch 7](chapters/ch07-seeing-the-trees-through-the-forest.md).

### Temperament and portfolio discipline are part of the method
Build gradually, diversify across situations and industries, avoid excessive margin, and limit option exposure. Historical allocations in the book are heuristics, not universal recommendations. If market declines would force panic selling or the research burden is unwelcome, a passive strategy may fit better. Patiently wait for a sound thesis to be recognized, while changing course when the facts do. See [Ch 7](chapters/ch07-seeing-the-trees-through-the-forest.md) and [Ch 8](chapters/ch08-all-the-fun-in-getting-there.md).
