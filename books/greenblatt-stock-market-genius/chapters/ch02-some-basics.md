# Chapter 2: Some Basics—Don’t Leave Home Without Them

## Core Idea
Special situations reward independent analysis and selectivity, not activity. Build a personal process that protects against both overconfidence and blind trust, and choose a small number of understandable opportunities over a large portfolio of weak ideas.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Do your own work**: Uncovered opportunities rarely come with analysis tailored to the source of mispricing. Read the filings and test the thesis yourself.
- **Trust neither insiders nor outsiders automatically**: Management, analysts, promoters, and critics all have incentives or blind spots. Verify claims against primary evidence and economics.
- **Pick your spots**: Favor a few unusually attractive, understandable situations over diversification into marginal ideas; reserve cash when no compelling opportunity exists.
- **Look down, not up**: Focus on downside protection and what can go wrong before fixating on the upside.
- **More than one road to investment heaven**: The book's special situations are examples, not a single formula; select a method suited to the facts and security.

## Key Concepts
- **Circle of competence**: Situations whose economics and risks can be understood well enough to evaluate.
- **Downside analysis**: Estimate plausible loss and asset or claim recovery before projecting gains.
- **Opportunity cost**: Return forgone by tying money up in a lower-quality position or leaving it unavailable.
- **Conviction**: A conclusion earned by evidence and valuation, not confidence or a popular narrative.

## Mental Models
Use cash as a valid position when available alternatives are poor. Treat a portfolio as a set of risk-bearing claims, not a list of stock symbols. Complexity raises the required research effort and expected return; it does not excuse skipping either.

## Anti-patterns
- **Taking advice as a substitute for analysis**: A recommendation does not transfer the risk to its source.
- **Buying merely to stay invested**: Activity can force capital into inferior opportunities.
- **Focusing on upside alone**: Event investing can have discontinuous losses when a deal breaks or a restructuring reallocates value.

## Worked Example
Before buying a newly separated company, identify what the market may dislike, inspect the separation documents, estimate sustainable cash generation and debt obligations, then compare likely downside with an independently estimated value. If the thesis depends on a promotional forecast or a single favorable event, keep the position small or pass.

## Key Takeaways
1. Make independent work the price of admission.
2. Base position choice on both opportunity and downside.
3. Keep cash available when no attractive risk-adjusted investment is available.
4. Use different analytical tools for different corporate situations.
