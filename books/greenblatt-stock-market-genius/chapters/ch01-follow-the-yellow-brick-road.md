# Chapter 1: Follow the Yellow Brick Road—Then Hang a Right

## Core Idea
A public market can be inefficient at the edges: institutions avoid small, odd, illiquid or operationally inconvenient securities even when the underlying economics are attractive. The individual investor's advantage is not superior speed or credentials but willingness to find and study neglected situations.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Look where others cannot or will not**: Search for situations outside standard institutional mandates and broad analyst attention.
  - When to use: A security is small, newly created, complex, or otherwise inconvenient to own.
  - How: Identify the structural reason attention or demand is impaired; then do independent valuation rather than assuming neglect means cheapness.
- **Homework, not risk-taking**: Seek compensation for analysis and judgment, not simply for accepting large uncertainty.
  - How: Compare downside and upside explicitly; pass if the expected reward does not outweigh the risks.

## Key Concepts
- **Efficient-market theory**: Public information is rapidly reflected in prices; the book argues that institutional and structural constraints leave exceptions.
- **Neglected security**: An asset overlooked for reasons unrelated to its long-term economic value.
- **Special situation**: Corporate event or security structure that creates a temporary or forced pricing anomaly.

## Mental Models
Treat investor attention as constrained capital: mandates, liquidity requirements, and benchmarking can create openings. But “unpopular” is a place to search, not a thesis. Discomfort can signal either a bargain or a genuine hazard.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming every obscure stock is a bargain**: Low visibility can coexist with poor economics or permanent impairment.
- **Believing credentials create an edge**: Expertise helps only when it improves the specific analysis.

## Worked Example
The book contrasts a professional investor who must own liquid, benchmark-relevant securities with an individual who can examine a small or awkward issue. The individual advantage exists only if the latter investigates the underlying documents and prices the actual risks rather than relying on the label “special situation.”

## Key Takeaways
1. Search where institutional constraints limit competition.
2. Treat complexity as a research lead, not evidence of mispricing.
3. The edge comes from work that others have little incentive to do.
