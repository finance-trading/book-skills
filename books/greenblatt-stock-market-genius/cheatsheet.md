# Cheatsheet

## Decision Rules
1. When an announced deal offers a spread, compare the dollar loss on a broken deal to the dollar gain on close before judging the annualized return.
2. When a spinoff appears cheap, look for the second security: the parent may be the stronger opportunity.
3. When an option is on a company with a pending event, price the event separately from historical volatility.
4. When a distressed security looks promising, estimate claim priority first; a good business is not a good equity claim if senior debt absorbs value.
5. When the market is down, search for newly attractive special situations rather than assuming the market will turn in time.
6. When a thesis can break a portfolio, scale the position down and preserve holding power; avoid margin that forces a sale.

## Situation Triage
- **Spinoff** → check forced sellers, insider alignment, distribution documents; value parent and child separately.
- **Announced merger** → model close/break scenarios and time; read consideration and conditions.
- **Bankruptcy/restructuring** → estimate enterprise value and walk down the claim waterfall.
- **Recap or stub** → rebuild post-transaction balance sheet and residual value.
- **Options/warrants** → align catalyst timing with expiry; consider dilution and premium.

## Thresholds and Defaults
- Start with a small portion of the portfolio in unfamiliar special situations; grow size with demonstrated competence.
- Prefer a small number of high-conviction situations over a wide basket of marginal ones.
- Use cash as a position when available opportunities are weak.
- Concentration in one industry or a set of correlated event risks is generally unwise.

## Tells and Smells
- A large spread with high break risk is a compensation clue, not a signal of certainty.
- Forced sellers can indicate supply, but a low price can also reflect a genuinely bad business.
- Management projections are starting points, not conclusions; test assumptions.
- If you cannot follow the claim or event to its payoff, pass.
