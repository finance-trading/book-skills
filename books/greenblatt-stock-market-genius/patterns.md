# Patterns

## Spinoff Screening
**When to use**: A parent announces a distribution of a subsidiary or division.
**How**: Identify why recipients may sell (mandate, size, complexity, or indifference); check insider ownership and compensation; read the distribution documents and separate valuation. Look for a newly revealed business or a hidden value in the parent.
**Trade-offs**: Requires reading filings and valuing two entities; event supply can persist for months.

## Risk Arbitrage
**When to use**: An announced cash, stock, or security merger offers a spread.
**How**: Model close value, break value, probability, and time to completion; annualize only after modeling delay. Adjust thesis as conditions change.
**Trade-offs**: Small gains on close versus much larger losses on failure; capital locked up.

## Bankruptcy Recovery Waterfall
**When to use**: Analyzing distressed debt, equity, or claims in a reorganization.
**How**: Estimate enterprise value; rank claims by priority and legal terms; allocate value down the waterfall; validate the plan and dilution.
**Trade-offs**: Value estimates and legal outcomes are uncertain; timing can be extended.

## Recapitalization / Stub Valuation
**When to use**: A company distributes cash or securities, leaving residual equity.
**How**: Rebuild the post-transaction balance sheet; subtract distributed value and debt; estimate the continuing business's implied value.
**Trade-offs**: Leverage magnifies both upside and downside; debt service can strain cash flow.

## Event Option Analysis
**When to use**: Considering options on companies with pending spinoffs, restructurings, or mergers.
**How**: Analyze the event and its timing independently of historical volatility; compare expiry and strike to the catalyst; model payoff at multiple prices.
**Trade-offs**: Premium decay and short windows create high uncertainty; standard pricing models may be misleading.

## Downside-First Position Sizing
**When to use**: Any special situation, especially leveraged or complex securities.
**How**: Estimate plausible loss before gains; size to evidence and experience; diversify across independent failure modes and limit margin.
**Trade-offs**: More conservative sizing may reduce long-run returns but improves survival.
