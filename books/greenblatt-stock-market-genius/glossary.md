# Glossary

- **Absolute priority** — General rule that senior claims are paid before junior claims in restructuring (Ch 5).
- **Break risk** — Risk that an announced merger fails, often causing the target to fall toward its unaffected value (Ch 4).
- **Catalyst** — Event that may expose or unlock value (Ch 3, 6, 8).
- **Deal spread** — Difference between a target security’s price and announced merger consideration (Ch 4).
- **Distressed debt** — Debt trading at a discount because payment or recovery is uncertain (Ch 5).
- **Forced seller** — Investor compelled or inclined to sell regardless of intrinsic value (Ch 3).
- **LEAPS** — Long-dated listed options (Ch 6).
- **Merger consideration** — Cash, stock, or other securities offered to target holders (Ch 4).
- **Parent-company discount** — Potential undervaluation of remaining operations when a complex division obscures their economics (Ch 3).
- **Recapitalization** — Change to a company’s capital structure, often involving debt, repurchases, dividends, or preferred securities (Ch 6).
- **Recovery value** — Estimated amount a particular claim receives in liquidation or reorganization (Ch 5).
- **Rights offering** — Offer allowing existing shareholders to buy additional shares under specified terms (Ch 3).
- **Risk arbitrage** — Buying securities after a merger announcement to earn the contingent spread if the transaction closes (Ch 4).
- **Spinoff** — Distribution of a subsidiary or division as a separately traded company (Ch 3).
- **Stub stock** — Residual equity after a company distributes value or restructures, often with significant leverage (Ch 6).
- **Timing risk** — Uncertainty and capital commitment while an event awaits resolution (Ch 4).
- **Unaffected price** — Target price before merger speculation or announcement; a reference point, not a guaranteed floor (Ch 4).
- **Warrant** — Right, often company-issued, to buy shares at a specified price (Ch 6).
