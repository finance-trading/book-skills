# Valuation Patterns

## Match claimant, cash flow, and rate
**When to use**: Before every DCF.  
**How**: FCFF → operating/firm value → WACC; FCFE or dividends → equity value → Ke. Match currency, nominal/real basis, and forecast horizon.  
**Trade-offs**: FCFF avoids detailed debt forecasting; FCFE can be natural for equity distributions but requires borrowing assumptions. See Ch 2, 7, 10, 14–15.

## Rebuild reported earnings
**When to use**: Financial statements are stale, contain one-offs, or expense long-lived investments.  
**How**: Construct current TTM, normalize recurring operations, capitalize R&D or financing-like leases consistently in earnings and capital, then reconcile to reported statements.  
**Trade-offs**: Useful adjustments require amortization life and lease assumptions; one-sided add-backs bias value. See Ch 3, 9.

## Convert growth into cash
**When to use**: A forecast shows high earnings or revenue growth.  
**How**: Estimate market/share/margin, incremental capital or sales-to-capital, taxes, and financing. Check `growth ≈ reinvestment rate × marginal ROIC` when returns are stable.  
**Trade-offs**: Bottom-up forecasts are richer but sensitive to market and competition assumptions. See Ch 10–11, 23.

## Close a DCF in stable growth
**When to use**: Ending an explicit forecast.  
**How**: Set mature growth below the matching discount rate, fade risk/returns, use `reinvestment rate = g/ROIC`, and compute next-period FCFF or FCFE terminal value. Run `r−g` and CAP sensitivities.  
**Trade-offs**: A terminal value can dominate the DCF; report its sensitivity. See Ch 12.

## Story-to-numbers 3P test
**When to use**: A firm's future differs from its financial history.  
**How**: Write the business story, test possible/plausible/probable, map it to market, share, margins, reinvestment, risk and duration, and maintain a feedback loop.  
**Trade-offs**: Stories expose assumptions but may be persuasive without being probable; demand disconfirming evidence. See Ch 13.

## Four-step multiple audit
**When to use**: Calling an asset cheap or expensive against peers.  
**How**: Define multiple consistently, inspect sector/market distribution and excluded cases, derive fundamental drivers, then select peers and adjust differences.  
**Trade-offs**: Pricing is faster than DCF but can inherit sector-wide overpricing. See Ch 17–20.

## Equity bridge after enterprise DCF
**When to use**: Reporting a per-share value from operating value.  
**How**: Add excess cash and nonoperating holdings, subtract debt/minority and other senior claims, account for employee equity claims, divide by a consistent diluted count.  
**Trade-offs**: Cross-holdings and stock compensation require careful double-count checks. See Ch 15–16.

## Financial-firm excess-return model
**When to use**: Bank or insurer with meaningful book equity and capital requirements.  
**How**: Forecast equity capital, ROE, Ke, and fade; value current equity capital plus PV of `(ROE−Ke)×equity capital`.  
**Trade-offs**: Book equity and credit quality can be impaired; do not apply industrial FCFF mechanically. See Ch 21.

## Acquirer value bridge
**When to use**: Evaluating a takeover.  
**How**: Separate target stand-alone value, feasible control improvements, combination-only synergy, integration costs, and price paid. Calculate acquirer benefit after the seller's premium.  
**Trade-offs**: Synergy probability and timing are uncertain; overbidding can transfer all benefits. See Ch 25.

## Real-option test
**When to use**: Proposed delay, expansion, abandonment, or distressed-equity optionality.  
**How**: Establish a genuine right, underlying asset value, exercise cost, time, volatility, and costs of waiting. Check whether base DCF already includes the same decision.  
**Trade-offs**: Competition, finance, and contract constraints can make theoretical option values unattainable. See Ch 5, 28–30.

## Probability method selection
**When to use**: Uncertainty is material to valuation.  
**How**: Use coherent scenarios for discrete worlds, decision trees for sequential choices, Monte Carlo for many correlated continuous inputs.  
**Trade-offs**: More simulated outcomes do not fix unsupported distributions. See Ch 33.
