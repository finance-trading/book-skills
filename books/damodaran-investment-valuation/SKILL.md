## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### Decide whether you are valuing or pricing

**Intrinsic valuation** estimates risk-adjusted cash-flow value. **Relative valuation** infers a price from comparable assets after controlling for growth, risk, margins, and capital needs. **Contingent-claim valuation** is for a genuine right with an asymmetric exercise payoff. Name the question before choosing a method; an asset may look cheap to its peers yet expensive against its own forecast cash flow. See [Ch 1](chapters/ch01-introduction-to-valuation.md), [Ch 2](chapters/ch02-approaches-to-valuation.md), and [Ch 34](chapters/ch34-overview-and-conclusion.md).

### Match claim, cash flow, and discount rate

For operations, forecast **FCFF** after taxes and reinvestment and discount at the **cost of capital**; then bridge to equity. For shareholders, forecast dividends or **FCFE** after net debt financing and discount at the **cost of equity**. Match nominal versus real and currency throughout. A sovereign yield containing default risk is not a risk-free base. See [Ch 7](chapters/ch07-riskless-rates-and-risk-premiums.md), [Ch 8](chapters/ch08-costs-of-financing.md), [Ch 10](chapters/ch10-from-earnings-to-cash-flows.md), [Ch 14](chapters/ch14-equity-intrinsic-value-models.md), and [Ch 15](chapters/ch15-firm-valuation.md).

### Fund the growth story

A growth forecast needs revenue opportunity, sustainable operating margin, incremental invested capital, and returns on new capital. With stable returns, operating-income growth follows **reinvestment rate × ROIC**. High growth with no funding requirement is an inconsistent story. Test a narrative for **possible, plausible, and probable** before turning it into model inputs, and revisit it as evidence changes. See [Ch 9](chapters/ch09-measuring-earnings.md), [Ch 11](chapters/ch11-estimating-growth.md), [Ch 13](chapters/ch13-narrative-and-numbers.md), and [Ch 23](chapters/ch23-young-and-startup-firms.md).

### Close the valuation without a terminal plug

Stable growth must fit the economy/currency and remain below the matching discount rate. Mature returns, risk, and reinvestment must change with growth; for firm cash flow, stable reinvestment is **g/ROIC**. The duration of excess returns—the competitive advantage period—often matters more than a single-year growth rate. Treat meaningful business failure separately rather than assuming every firm survives to terminal value. See [Ch 12](chapters/ch12-estimating-terminal-value.md).

### Audit every multiple in four steps

(1) Define and measure it consistently; (2) inspect its distribution and exclusions; (3) derive its drivers; (4) choose comparable firms and control differences. Equity price must pair with equity earnings/book; enterprise value with operating earnings or sales. A low P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, or EV/Sales can reflect weak economics. See [Ch 17](chapters/ch17-relative-valuation-principles.md) through [Ch 20](chapters/ch20-revenue-and-sector-multiples.md).

### Change model when the asset changes

Banks use equity distributions or excess equity returns because debt is an operating input and regulatory capital constrains payout. Private firms require owner-pay, control, and liquidity analysis. Acquisitions separate status-quo value, control improvements, combination-specific synergy, and price paid. Patents, expansion, delay, abandonment, and distressed equity can have option-like payoffs only when the exercise right is real and has not already been included in the DCF. See [Ch 21](chapters/ch21-financial-service-firms.md), [Ch 24](chapters/ch24-private-firms.md), [Ch 25](chapters/ch25-acquisitions-and-takeovers.md), and [Ch 28](chapters/ch28-option-to-delay.md) through [Ch 30](chapters/ch30-distressed-equity.md).
