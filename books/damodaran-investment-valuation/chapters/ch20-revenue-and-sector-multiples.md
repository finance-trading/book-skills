# Chapter 20: Revenue Multiples and Sector-Specific Multiples

## Core Idea
Revenue multiples can be used before earnings stabilize, but they are driven by eventual margins, growth, reinvestment, and risk. Sector-specific units require an explicit bridge to future cash flow.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Price-to-sales and EV-to-sales**: `P/S = equity market value / revenue`; `EV/Sales = enterprise value / revenue`. Both need sustainable margin, growth, and reinvestment analysis; EV/Sales is less mechanically distorted by different debt mixes because revenue is generated by the whole operating business.
  - When to use: Earnings are negative or distorted while revenue has economic meaning.
- **Sector-specific unit valuation**: Price users, subscribers, stores, or production capacity only after estimating the cash flow generated by one additional unit.
  - Why it works: A user count without retention, monetization, or acquisition cost is not an economic asset measure.
- **Subscriber cohort valuation**: Separate existing users, newly acquired users, retention, revenue per user, servicing cost, and corporate drag.

## Key Concepts
- **P/S**: Equity value divided by revenue.
- **EV/Sales**: Enterprise value divided by revenue.
- **Contribution margin**: Incremental revenue less direct cost of serving it.
- **Retention**: Proportion of users/subscribers who remain over time.
- **Customer acquisition cost**: Spending to add a new customer or subscriber.

## Mental Models
- Sales become valuable through future operating profit and cash flow, not merely scale.
- A high-growth low-margin firm and a high-margin stable firm can deserve very different revenue multiples.

## Anti-patterns
- **Value per website hit or user without unit economics**: The count may never become profitable cash flow.
- **Ignoring corporate overhead**: Cohort economics can look attractive before platform-wide costs.
- **Mixing price/sales and EV/sales**: Debt and cash make the numerator materially different.

## Worked Example
The chapter deconstructs Netflix into existing subscribers, the value of new subscribers, and corporate drag. For the existing base, forecast monthly revenue, churn, content/service costs, and the life of the cohort. For new users, subtract acquisition and onboarding costs before adding their expected contribution. A high per-subscriber market price can then be tested against retention and margins rather than asserted to be reasonable merely because peer platforms have many users.

## Key Takeaways
1. Translate sales or users into margin and cash flow.
2. Keep equity and enterprise numerators consistent.
3. Subtract acquisition, reinvestment, and overhead costs from unit value.
