# Chapter 15: Firm Valuation—Cost of Capital and Adjusted Present Value Approaches

## Core Idea
Firm valuation discounts cash flows available to all capital providers and then allocates operating value among debt and equity claims. The cost-of-capital and adjusted-present-value approaches frame leverage differently but should reconcile under consistent assumptions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **FCFF/WACC valuation**: Discount FCFF at a market-value-weighted cost of capital, with a stable terminal phase and reinvestment consistent with growth.
  - When to use: Valuing operations without having to forecast each debt issue for an FCFE model.
- **Adjusted present value (APV)**: Value an all-equity-financed business, add financing tax benefits, and subtract expected financing distress costs.
  - When to use: Leverage changes materially or the financing effects deserve explicit treatment.
- **Firm-to-equity bridge**: From operating asset value, add nonoperating assets and subtract financial debt and other senior claims; handle minority interests and cross-holdings consistently.

## Key Concepts
- **FCFF**: After-tax operating income plus noncash charges less reinvestment.
- **WACC**: Market-value weighted debt and equity required returns, with an appropriate tax adjustment to debt.
- **Unlevered firm value**: Operating value before financing tax shields and distress effects.
- **Tax shield**: Tax saving generated by deductible interest, if the firm can use it.
- **Financial distress cost**: Expected direct and indirect value loss from debt pressure or default.

## Mental Models
- Debt can create a tax advantage and a rising distress cost; a lower WACC is not a free lunch.
- The firm-value estimate is not yet a per-share equity value.

## Reference Formulas
- `Operating firm value = Σ[FCFF_t / (1 + WACC)^t] + TV_firm,n / (1 + WACC)^n` when WACC is held constant for this presentation; if leverage or risk changes, discount each period with its period-appropriate rate rather than forcing one fixed WACC.
- `WACC = Ke × E/(D+E) + Kd × (1−tax rate) × D/(D+E)`, with **market** values of equity `E` and debt `D` and a usable tax shield.
- `APV = unlevered firm value + PV(interest tax shields) − PV(expected distress costs)`. APV makes financing effects explicit; do not also embed the same tax shield in the unlevered discount rate.

## Anti-patterns
- **FCFF discounted at cost of equity**: Mismatches claim and rate.
- **Adding tax shields twice**: APV treats them explicitly; WACC usually embeds them in after-tax debt cost.
- **Subtracting debt from enterprise value without nonoperating assets**: It can omit cash, cross-holdings, or other claims.

## Reference Table
| Method | Base | Financing treatment | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| WACC | FCFF discounted at cost of capital | Embedded in weighted discount rate | Circular or inconsistent capital weights |
| APV | Unlevered operating value | Tax benefits and distress separately | Overstating usable tax shields |

## Worked Example
The chapter values Telesp using a stable-growth FCFF model. It starts with operating earnings after tax, subtracts capital needs implied by expected growth, discounts at WACC, and then bridges from operating value to the equity claim. Target's lease illustration shows why recognizing lease debt must be paired with corresponding operating adjustments. Amgen's R&D example shows the same principle: changing the accounting treatment of research affects invested capital and earnings together; it should not be booked as a one-sided value increase.

## Key Takeaways
1. Forecast FCFF and discount it at WACC, or use APV with financing effects separately.
2. Align debt, leases, tax shields, and capital weights across all steps.
3. Reconcile operating value to the equity claim before quoting a share value.
