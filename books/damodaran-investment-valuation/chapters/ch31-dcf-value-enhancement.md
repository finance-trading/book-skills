# Chapter 31: Value Enhancement—A Discounted Cash Flow Valuation Framework

## Core Idea
Value enhancement changes expected cash flows, growth, the duration of excess returns, or risk. It must be evaluated against the investment and disruption required to achieve it, rather than asserted from higher accounting earnings alone.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Value enhancement chain**: Diagnose the current business, identify operating or financing changes, translate them into DCF drivers, and compare improved value with implementation costs.
  - When to use: Activist proposals, restructuring, management change, or capital-allocation review.
- **Existing-assets improvement**: Raise after-tax operating income by using assets better, selling underperforming units, changing margins, or managing working capital.
- **Growth quality improvement**: Direct reinvestment toward projects with returns above cost of capital; growth below that hurdle destroys value.
- **Risk and duration improvement**: Reduce avoidable financing/default risk or extend the competitive advantage period through defensible capabilities.

## Key Concepts
- **Return spread**: ROIC less cost of capital.
- **Value-neutral action**: Accounting or financing change that does not change risk-adjusted cash flow under consistent assumptions.
- **Divestiture**: Sale or exit of an asset whose proceeds and foregone cash flows both need valuing.
- **Working-capital release**: Cash created by reducing operating capital tied in receivables or inventory without damaging sales.
- **Competitive advantage period**: Span of above-normal returns.

## Mental Models
- More growth is valuable only if incremental ROIC exceeds WACC; otherwise less growth can be worth more.
- A management-change premium is a scenario about real operating actions, not an automatic percentage addition.

## Anti-patterns
- **Cost cuts without revenue or reinvestment effects**: Some cuts impair future margins and growth.
- **Selling an asset solely to book a gain**: Compare sale proceeds with the cash-flow value surrendered.
- **Targeting EPS rather than value**: Leverage and accounting changes can raise EPS without creating cash value.

## Worked Example
The chapter evaluates operating-margin changes at Marks & Spencer and shows how working-capital management can release cash. It also contrasts high-growth reinvestment with lower-growth but higher-return investment: a firm can enhance value by declining projects that earn below its cost of capital. In the Blockbuster management-change example, a control-value claim must identify specific improvements and costs; the improvement is not created by replacing a nameplate at the top of the company.

## Key Takeaways
1. Translate each proposed action into dated cash-flow or risk changes.
2. Test incremental investment against cost of capital.
3. Deduct transition costs and avoid double-counting control improvements.
