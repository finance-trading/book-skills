# Chapter 13: Narrative and Numbers—Story to Value

## Core Idea
A valuation is a bridge between a business story and numbers. Each major numerical assumption should follow from a claim about the market, the firm, and competition, and the story should change when evidence changes.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Story-to-numbers sequence**: Develop the business narrative, test it, translate it into revenue growth, margins, reinvestment, risk, and duration, then value and revisit it.
  - When to use: Especially for young firms and businesses where recent financial statements do not describe the future.
  - How: Write the story before tuning the spreadsheet; tie each major model input to a specific claim; seek contradictory evidence.
- **3P test**: Evaluate a story for possibility, plausibility, and probability before allowing its numbers into a base case.
  - Why it works: It separates a merely conceivable outcome from a realistic one backed by market size and execution evidence.
- **Feedback loop**: Update the story as new facts arrive instead of defending a stale point valuation.

## Key Concepts
- **Narrative**: Causal account of how the business creates and keeps value.
- **Addressable market**: Total relevant demand the firm can plausibly reach.
- **Operating margin path**: Route from current profitability to a sustainable margin.
- **Reinvestment**: Capital needed to create the projected sales and earnings.
- **Story break**: Evidence that invalidates a central causal assumption.

## Mental Models
- If a growth-rate input has no story behind it, it is a spreadsheet wish.
- If a story has no impact on a model input, it has not yet been valued.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reverse engineering a preferred price**: Adjusting growth or discount rate without changing the story conceals bias.
- **Narrative without economic constraints**: A large market does not guarantee share, margin, or returns.
- **Number-only projection**: Historical trends cannot explain structural shifts or competitive erosion.

## Worked Example
The chapter builds a story for Zomato, the Indian food-delivery platform. It first asks how large online food ordering can become, what share Zomato can gain, how margins might change as scale develops, and what reinvestment the platform needs. Those claims become a revenue path, operating-margin trajectory, sales-to-capital assumptions, and risk inputs. A bullish alternative must pass the same 3P test rather than simply raising the terminal growth rate. The valuation is then reopened when market or execution evidence contradicts the original story.

## Key Takeaways
1. Make the investment story explicit before fitting inputs.
2. Test whether each driver is possible, plausible, and probable.
3. Keep a documented feedback loop so new evidence changes the valuation.
