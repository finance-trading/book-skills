# Chapter 2: Approaches to Valuation

## Core Idea
Three approaches answer different questions: intrinsic valuation discounts expected cash flows, relative valuation prices against comparable assets, and contingent-claim valuation prices option-like rights.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Intrinsic valuation**: Forecast cash flows after investment and financing claims, then discount using a rate matched to those cash flows.
  - When to use: When the business or asset has an estimable cash-flow path.
  - How: Choose equity or firm value; match nominal cash flows to nominal rates and currency to currency; add a defensible terminal value; reconcile to the quoted security.
- **Pricing or relative valuation**: Compare standardized market prices with peers, and control for fundamental differences.
  - When to use: When peer transactions or traded comparables are informative.
  - How: Define numerator and denominator consistently; examine growth, risk, margins, and reinvestment; use a range rather than a peer average alone.
- **Contingent claim valuation**: Model a right whose payoff depends on the value of another asset and a decision or threshold.
  - When to use: For financial options, patents, reserves, and projects with meaningful flexibility.
  - How: Specify underlying value, exercise cost, time, volatility, and whether the right can be exercised or abandoned.

## Key Concepts
- **Firm value**: Value of claims on operations before allocating between debt and equity.
- **Equity value**: Value remaining for equity holders after senior claims.
- **FCFF**: Cash flow available to all capital providers.
- **FCFE**: Cash flow available to equity investors after debt-related flows.
- **Comparable**: Asset whose pricing can inform another asset only after relevant differences are accounted for.
- **Real option**: Flexibility in a real investment with an option-like contingent payoff.

## Mental Models
- Ask whether the output is a cash-flow value, a peer-derived price, or an option value before comparing results.
- A multiple is a compressed forecast: growth, risk, and margins drive what counts as a fair multiple.

## Anti-patterns
- **Cash-flow/discount-rate mismatch**: Discounting FCFF at a cost of equity, or FCFE at a cost of capital, gives a category error.
- **Comparable by label only**: Two firms in the same industry can deserve different multiples because their economics differ.
- **Double-counting optionality**: Do not add option value if the cash-flow forecast already includes the same expansion benefit.

## Key Takeaways
1. Keep the claim and discount rate consistent.
2. Explain why a peer deserves the same or a different multiple.
3. Use option models only when an actual contingent right exists.
