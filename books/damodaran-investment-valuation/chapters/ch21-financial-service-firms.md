# Chapter 21: Valuing Financial Service Firms

## Core Idea
Banks and other financial firms require an equity-focused valuation: debt is part of their operating raw material, regulatory capital constrains distributions, and conventional FCFF/WACC bridges can obscure their economics.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Financial-firm equity valuation**: Forecast dividends or FCFE after the capital needed to support regulated growth, and discount at the cost of equity.
  - When to use: Banks, insurers, investment banks, and other balance-sheet intermediaries.
- **Excess-return model**: Begin with book equity and add the present value of future equity returns above the cost of equity on that capital.
  - How: Estimate sustainable ROE, equity capital required for future assets, and the period of excess returns; test credit quality and funding stability.
- **Bank P/B and ROE bridge**: Judge price-to-book through expected ROE relative to cost of equity, growth, and risk, not by a sector average alone.

## Key Concepts
- **Regulatory capital**: Equity buffer demanded by regulators against risk-weighted exposures.
- **Net interest margin**: Spread between interest earned on assets and cost of funding.
- **Credit loss**: Expected loss from borrowers failing to pay.
- **Deposit franchise**: Funding advantage created by stable, lower-cost deposits.
- **Book equity**: Especially relevant for regulated firms, but still subject to asset-quality and accounting checks.

## Mental Models
- A bank's borrowings and deposits are operating inputs, so “subtract debt” as if it were an industrial company's financing can distort value.
- Reported earnings are not wholly distributable if asset growth requires more regulatory capital.

## Reference Formula
`Equity value = current equity capital + PV(expected future excess equity returns)`, where each period's `excess equity return = (ROE − Ke) × equity capital invested`. Forecast the book equity that supports future growth and the fade of excess ROE. This is an equity model; discount excess equity returns at the cost of equity, and do not add the same returns again through a separate DDM.

## Anti-patterns
- **Industrial-company FCFF formula on a bank**: Debt and working capital have different roles.
- **High ROE without asset-quality review**: Under-provisioning can inflate earnings and book value.
- **Comparing bank P/B without capital and risk differences**: Leverage and credit mix matter.

## Worked Example
The chapter values HSBC with a stable-growth dividend model: forecast earnings and payouts while keeping capital adequate for growth, then discount the equity distributions. Goldman Sachs is examined with an excess-return model, making the value of ROE above the required equity return explicit. The Silicon Valley Bank case later shows how a seemingly valuable deposit franchise can erode when duration mismatch and deposit-flight risk become binding; a historical P/B or ROE cannot stand in for current funding risk.

## Key Takeaways
1. Value financial firms through equity distributions or excess equity returns.
2. Treat regulatory capital and credit losses as cash-flow constraints.
3. Reassess funding and asset risk when past profitability ceases to represent the franchise.
