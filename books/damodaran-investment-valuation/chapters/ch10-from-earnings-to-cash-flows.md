# Chapter 10: From Earnings to Cash Flows

## Core Idea
Valuation needs cash available after taxes and reinvestment. Accounting earnings cannot be discounted as though capital spending, working capital, and debt flows were optional.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **FCFF bridge**: Begin with after-tax operating income, add noncash charges, subtract capital expenditure and increases in noncash working capital.
  - When to use: Valuing the whole operating business at the cost of capital.
  - How: Normalize taxes, include acquisition spending when it supports growth, and ensure working capital excludes cash and financing debt.
- **FCFE bridge**: Begin with net income, add noncash charges, subtract reinvestment and add net debt financing.
  - When to use: Valuing equity directly at the cost of equity.
- **Tax-rate transition**: Distinguish the current effective tax rate, marginal statutory rate, loss carryforwards, and temporary tax subsidies. Model the period during which a tax advantage lasts.

## Key Concepts
- **Net capital expenditure**: Capital expenditure less depreciation; a rough measure of investment above replacement.
- **Noncash working capital**: Operating current assets minus operating current liabilities, excluding financing cash/debt.
- **Net operating loss (NOL)**: Past tax loss that can shield future taxable income subject to tax rules.
- **Effective tax rate**: Reported tax expense relative to reported pretax income; it can be temporary or distorted.
- **Marginal tax rate**: Rate applicable to an additional dollar of taxable profit.

## Mental Models
- Growth consumes cash when it requires incremental plant, acquisitions, inventory, or receivables.
- A tax shield has value only to the extent the firm can use it and while the legal benefit lasts.

## Reference Formulas
- `FCFF = EBIT × (1 − tax rate) + depreciation/amortization − capital expenditures − Δ(non-cash working capital)`. For a serial acquirer, acquisition outlays supporting projected growth must be captured in reinvestment; for capitalized R&D, include R&D investment and related amortization consistently.
- `FCFE = net income + depreciation/amortization − capital expenditures − Δ(non-cash working capital) + net debt issued`. These are equity cash flows, so debt issuance and repayment belong here, unlike in FCFF.
- `Non-cash working capital = (non-cash current operating assets) − (non-debt current operating liabilities)`. Cash and financing debt are excluded from the operating requirement.

## Anti-patterns
- **EBITDA as free cash flow**: It omits taxes and the reinvestment needed to maintain or grow operations.
- **Zero reinvestment with high perpetual growth**: The forecast assumes growth without financing its assets.
- **Ignoring acquisitions**: A serial acquirer may need continuing deal spending to deliver the projected growth.

## Reference Table
| Cash flow | Starting earnings | Reinvestment | Financing flows | Discount rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FCFF | After-tax operating income | Subtract | Exclude | Cost of capital |
| FCFE | Net income | Subtract | Include net debt issuance | Cost of equity |

## Worked Example
The Tesla loss-carryforward illustration shows that a company with accumulated tax losses may pay little cash tax while it uses them, but cannot use a zero tax rate forever if it becomes profitable. Forecast taxable income and NOL usage year by year, then transition to a sustainable marginal tax rate. The Gap working-capital illustration asks the analyst to exclude cash and short-term borrowing from operating working capital; otherwise financing choices are mistaken for operating reinvestment.

## Key Takeaways
1. Match the cash-flow claimant to the discount rate.
2. Forecast taxes and reinvestment explicitly through the growth period.
3. Treat acquisition and working-capital needs as part of growth economics.
