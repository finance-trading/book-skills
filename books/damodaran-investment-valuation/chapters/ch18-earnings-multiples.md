# Chapter 18: Earnings Multiples

## Core Idea
Earnings multiples depend on payout and cash conversion, growth, and risk. A low P/E or EV/EBITDA is a prompt to investigate those drivers, not a standalone bargain signal.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **P/E decomposition**: Compare equity price with the same period and scope of equity earnings, then ask what payout, expected growth, and cost of equity justify the ratio.
  - When to use: A firm has meaningful sustainable net earnings.
- **PEG check**: Divide P/E by expected growth only after checking the growth horizon, risk, payout, and units; equal PEG does not guarantee equal value.
- **EV/EBITDA check**: Pair enterprise value with EBITDA and adjust comparisons for tax rate, depreciation, capital intensity, working capital, and return on capital.
  - Why it works: EBITDA can be high while cash reinvestment leaves little value for owners.

## Key Concepts
- **Trailing P/E**: Price divided by recently reported earnings.
- **Forward P/E**: Price divided by forecast earnings for a future period.
- **PEG**: P/E scaled by an expected earnings-growth rate.
- **EV/EBITDA**: Enterprise value divided by earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization.
- **Earnings yield**: Earnings divided by price, the reciprocal of P/E when earnings are positive.

## Mental Models
- A multiple cannot be judged independently of the duration and quality of growth.
- EBITDA is before the cash cost of replacing assets; high capex can make an apparently cheap EV/EBITDA misleading.

## Reference Formulas
- For a stable dividend-paying firm, `trailing P/E = payout ratio × (1 + g) / (Ke − g)` using current earnings, while `forward P/E = payout ratio / (Ke − g)` using next-period earnings. `g < Ke`, payout and growth must be sustainable, and earnings must be positive and comparable.
- Stable equity growth can be linked to retained earnings: `g = (1 − payout ratio) × sustainable ROE`. Raising `g` while holding payout fixed may violate this relationship.

## Anti-patterns
- **P/E on temporarily depressed or inflated earnings**: Normalize the base before reading the multiple.
- **PEG as a universal equalizer**: It omits risk, dividends, and capital needs, and may compare incompatible growth windows.
- **P/E on negative earnings**: The ratio loses conventional meaning; use another model or a normalized future period.

## Reference Table
| Multiple | Claim | Main drivers to inspect |
|---|---|
| P/E | Equity | Payout, growth, cost of equity, earnings quality |
| PEG | Equity | Growth horizon, risk, payout, units |
| EV/EBITDA | Firm | Tax, reinvestment, depreciation, ROIC |

## Key Takeaways
1. Align the earnings denominator with a dated, normalized claim.
2. Explain a multiple through growth, risk, payout, and reinvestment.
3. Use PEG and EV/EBITDA as diagnostics, not automatic fair-price formulas.
