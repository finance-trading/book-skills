# Chapter 29: The Options to Expand and to Abandon—Valuation Implications

## Core Idea
A pilot investment can be worth more than its immediate cash flows if it grants a right to scale up later; an exit or abandonment right can limit losses. Both require contractual and operational ability to act.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Option to expand**: Value the initial project plus the contingent right to invest more if early evidence supports a larger market.
  - When to use: New markets, products, platforms, or sequential capacity buildouts.
  - How: Specify the expansion threshold, required investment, potential market value, time window, and competitive restrictions.
- **Option to abandon**: Compare continuation value with salvage/sale value at future decision points; exit when the latter is higher.
- **Financial flexibility**: Cash and borrowing headroom can preserve the ability to fund positive-NPV opportunities during adverse markets.

## Key Concepts
- **Pilot project**: Small initial investment that yields information and access to later expansion.
- **Exercise threshold**: State in which expanding or abandoning beats the alternative.
- **Salvage value**: Recoverable value when a project is stopped.
- **Sequential option**: Later decisions depend on outcomes of earlier investments.
- **Platform option**: Existing users or data may lower the cost of entering adjacent products, if rights and economics support it.

## Mental Models
- An option to expand is strongest when uncertainty is high, market upside is large, and the firm has defensible access.
- Cash reserves can have option value beyond interest income if they prevent forced abandonment or enable investment at a favorable moment.

## Anti-patterns
- **Free expansion premium**: Future growth still requires exercise capital and may attract competitors.
- **Adding option value on top of a growth forecast that includes expansion**: Count the same opportunity once.
- **Assuming abandonment is frictionless**: Contracts, employees, reputation, and liquidation costs can make exit expensive.

## Worked Example
Ambev's Guaraná example treats a local launch as a pilot that might justify expansion into a larger market. The initial market can have modest value while the opportunity to expand later is attractive if demand proves strong; the exercise cost and probability of succeeding must still be modeled. Secure Mail illustrates a software platform's potential expansion, but a user base is valuable only to the extent it lowers the cost of monetizable new products. The Home Depot case frames spare borrowing capacity as flexibility to invest or weather stress.

## Key Takeaways
1. Specify the decision right and cost at each future branch.
2. Distinguish growth already forecast from separate expansion optionality.
3. Include competitive, contractual, and exit frictions.
