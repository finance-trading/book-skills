# Chapter 25: Acquisitions and Takeovers

## Core Idea
The buyer should pay for stand-alone target value plus only the control improvements and synergies it can actually capture, after integration cost and execution risk. A deal can create combined value yet destroy value for the acquirer's shareholders if the price transfers it to the seller.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three-layer target valuation**: Estimate (1) status-quo value, (2) value of control from feasible operating changes, and (3) merger-specific synergy from combining the firms.
  - When to use: Bid design, M&A review, or takeover-defense analysis.
- **Synergy valuation**: Change explicit cash-flow drivers—revenue, cost, taxes, investment, or risk—then subtract implementation cost and discount the incremental flows.
- **Deal structure check**: Analyze cash versus stock, financing, taxes, dilution, and who receives the benefits.

## Key Concepts
- **Status-quo value**: Value under current management and policies.
- **Control value**: Increase attainable by changing operations or financing.
- **Synergy**: Additional value unique to the combination, beyond two stand-alone firms.
- **Acquisition premium**: Price paid above the target's undisturbed market price.
- **Winner's curse**: Competitive bidding that forces the buyer to overpay for uncertain benefits.

## Mental Models
- `Value(A+B) > Value(A) + Value(B)` is necessary for synergy, but shareholder gain also depends on purchase price and division of that value.
- The more specific the synergy cash flow, the more testable the claim.

## Reference Formula
`Gross synergy value = V(A+B with combination-specific benefits) − V(A stand-alone) − V(B stand-alone)`. Expected buyer benefit then subtracts integration spending, execution risk, and the acquisition premium transferred to the seller. Keep management improvements available to any owner in control value, not merger-specific synergy.

## Anti-patterns
- **Adding a generic percentage for synergy**: It hides the source and timing of incremental cash flow.
- **Counting target turnaround and merger synergy twice**: Control improvements may be available to any buyer.
- **EPS accretion as value creation**: Accounting EPS can rise even if the deal's NPV is negative.

## Worked Example
For SABMiller, the chapter first values current operations, then asks what better management or financing could change, and only then models AB InBev-specific combination benefits. An acquirer should estimate integration costs and the probability that savings materialize. The maximum rational bid is below the buyer's expected combined incremental value if the buyer is to retain any benefit; simply paying up to the full modeled synergy transfers that benefit to the seller and leaves no cushion for error.

## Key Takeaways
1. Separate stand-alone, control, and synergy value.
2. Trace synergy into dated cash-flow changes and costs.
3. Evaluate the deal for the buyer after the price and financing structure.
