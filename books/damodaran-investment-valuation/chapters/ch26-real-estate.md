# Chapter 26: Valuing Real Estate

## Core Idea
Real estate can be valued from property-level cash flows or comparable sales, but rent, vacancy, maintenance, financing, and location must be modeled explicitly. A building and a real-estate business are different assets.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Property DCF**: Forecast rental income, vacancy, operating expenses, maintenance and capital improvements, taxes, and sale proceeds; discount at a rate consistent with property risk and claim.
  - When to use: Income-producing property with estimable rent and upkeep.
- **Comparable property pricing**: Compare price per square foot, capitalization rate, or other unit only after adjusting for tenant, lease term, location, condition, and growth.
- **Levered equity bridge**: Value the property first, then subtract mortgage claims or forecast equity cash flows after debt service.

## Key Concepts
- **Net operating income (NOI)**: Property income after ordinary operating expenses but before financing and some capital needs.
- **Capitalization rate**: NOI divided by property price; a compressed statement of growth and risk.
- **Vacancy rate**: Expected unoccupied share that reduces rent collections.
- **Maintenance capex**: Spending needed to preserve the property's earning power.
- **Real versus nominal cash flow**: Inflation-adjusted versus currency-unit forecasts, each requiring a matching discount rate.

## Mental Models
- A cap rate is not a substitute for assumptions about rent growth, lease rollover, and capital expenditure.
- The identity of the marginal investor affects how much diversifiable local property risk is priced.

## Anti-patterns
- **Discounting gross rent**: Vacancy, operating costs, and maintenance are real claims on cash.
- **Comparing properties by address or square footage alone**: Lease and tenant quality can dominate the difference.
- **Mixing property value with equity value**: Mortgage financing must be handled consistently.

## Key Takeaways
1. Forecast property cash after vacancy, upkeep, and operating costs.
2. Match nominal/real and property/equity cash flows to their rates.
3. Use comparable sales only after property-level adjustments.
