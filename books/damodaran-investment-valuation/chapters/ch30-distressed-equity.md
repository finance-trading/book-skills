# Chapter 30: Valuing Equity in Distressed Firms

## Core Idea
Limited liability makes equity in a highly levered distressed firm resemble a call option on the firm's assets. This optionality can give equity positive value even when conventional asset value is below debt, but financing and legal realities constrain the payoff.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Equity-as-call mapping**: Treat firm asset value as the underlying, promised debt payment as the exercise threshold, debt maturity as option life, and asset-value uncertainty as volatility.
  - When to use: Deeply distressed firms with substantial debt and limited liability.
- **Debt/equity reconciliation**: Compare the option-based equity result with a DCF that includes default, restructuring, and future financing; the models should describe the same claims.
- **Distress decision test**: Recognize that equity holders' incentive to take risk can diverge from lenders' incentives when downside is capped for equity.

## Key Concepts
- **Limited liability**: Equity holders cannot lose more than their invested capital under ordinary corporate form.
- **Asset value**: Value of the business available to all claimants.
- **Debt face value**: Contractual repayment amount, not necessarily market value of debt.
- **Volatility effect**: Greater asset-value uncertainty can increase option-like equity value while making debt riskier.
- **Debt overhang**: Existing debt can discourage equity-funded positive-NPV investment.

## Mental Models
- A distressed stock can have positive option value without being a good investment at its market price.
- Risk shifting can raise equity's option value while lowering total firm value and creditor recovery.

## Reference Formula
At a single debt maturity with face repayment `D`, a simplified limited-liability equity payoff is `max(V_assets at maturity − D, 0)`. The option analogy requires an asset-value estimate and asset volatility, not just equity volatility. Multiple debt maturities, covenants, rescue funding, and bankruptcy bargaining can break this simple mapping.

## Anti-patterns
- **Setting equity to zero whenever DCF firm value is below face debt**: It ignores limited-liability upside.
- **Treating option value as guaranteed recovery**: The firm may need new capital or lose control before maturity.
- **Using stock volatility as asset volatility directly**: The option model's underlying is the firm asset value.

## Worked Example
The chapter's simple example compares a firm whose asset value is below the promised debt repayment with two possible future states. Equity receives nothing in the low state but can retain upside in a high state, so the current equity claim may be positive. Eurotunnel and Jet India are then used to map firm assets, debt maturity, and uncertainty into an option-style value. The analyst must still check covenant, refinancing, dilution, and restructuring outcomes before comparing the estimate with stock price.

## Key Takeaways
1. Map the actual debt contract and limited-liability payoff before option pricing.
2. Model dilution and restructuring alongside upside.
3. Distinguish a positive equity option value from attractive total firm value.
