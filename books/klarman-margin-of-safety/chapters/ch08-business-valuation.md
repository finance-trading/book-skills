# Chapter 8: The Art of Business Valuation

## Core Idea
Valuation is a range, not a precise output. Several conservative methods should converge, and the buyer must assume that analysis contains mistakes.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Triangulated Valuation**: Use net asset value, earnings or cash-flow power, and, where relevant, liquidation or private-market transactions.
  - When to use: Establishing value for any operating business or asset-heavy security.
  - How: Calculate each applicable method independently. Investigate wide disagreements instead of averaging them mechanically.
- **Present Value of Cash Flow**: Value is the cash an owner can remove, discounted for time and uncertainty.
  - When to use: Businesses with estimable continuing free cash flow.
  - How: Normalize earnings, subtract maintenance investment, exclude growth that consumes the whole return, and test adverse cases.
- **Conservative Bias**: Choose plausible assumptions that reduce value when precision is impossible.
  - When to use: Forecasting margins, growth, liquidation proceeds, or discount rates.
  - How: Prefer demonstrated results over promotional projections. Treat unverifiable precision as a warning.

## Key Concepts
- **Net asset value (NAV)**: Conservative value of assets minus liabilities.
- **Earnings power**: Sustainable earnings available to owners.
- **Liquidation value**: Proceeds if assets are sold and obligations settled.
- **Private-market value**: Price informed buyers pay for a whole business.
- **Valuation range**: The span of reasonable outcomes rather than a false point estimate.

## Mental Models
Use valuation as a bounded map. The useful question is not whether value equals one number, but whether price remains attractive at the low end of a defensible range.

## Anti-patterns
- **False precision**: Multiplying an optimistic figure by an exact multiple.
- **Single-method dependence**: One model can conceal one bad assumption.
- **Valuing growth twice**: Assuming high growth while also using a peak multiple.
- **Ignoring claims and conversion**: Enterprise value must reach the specific security correctly.
- **Using peak or trough results without normalization**: Cyclical accounting can invert apparent cheapness.

## Worked Example
Klarman presents valuation as art constrained by evidence, not as a spreadsheet ceremony. Reconstruct a business using three cases. In the asset case, haircut receivables, inventory, and fixed assets according to realizability. In the earnings case, remove one-time gains and maintenance underinvestment. In the transaction case, use comparable sales only after adjusting for leverage and cycle. Buy only when even the overlap of conservative cases exceeds market price by a substantial margin.

## Why It Works / Failure Modes
Triangulation exposes assumptions that one method hides. It fails when all methods reuse the same optimistic input, such as management forecasts, or when the investor adjusts the model until it blesses a decision already made.

## Key Takeaways
1. Demand a value range and know what drives its low end.
2. Cash flow, assets, and relevant transactions should corroborate one another.
3. Conservative assumptions are part of the method, not an optional mood.
4. Precision is not accuracy.
