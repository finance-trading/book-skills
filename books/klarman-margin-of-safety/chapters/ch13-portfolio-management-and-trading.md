# Chapter 13: Portfolio Management and Trading

## Core Idea
Portfolio management turns individual bargains into a survivable result. Diversification, cash, position size, and trading discipline must protect capital without diluting the best ideas.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Balanced Diversification**: Own enough independent risks to survive error, but not so many that the best discounts become irrelevant.
  - When to use: Adding a new position or reviewing concentration.
  - How: Group exposures by business, industry, event, legal process, and liquidity. Add positions only when their risks are genuinely different.
- **Position Sizing by Downside and Certainty**: The largest positions combine a large discount, understandable risk, and acceptable liquidity.
  - When to use: Every purchase or add.
  - How: Size from loss potential and correlation, not expected upside alone. Reduce size as complexity, leverage, or correlation rises.
- **Trading as Price Discipline**: Buy weakness in a qualified idea and sell strength when price approaches value.
  - When to use: Volatile or illiquid markets.
  - How: Use limits and predetermined value ranges. Do not trade merely to justify activity or respond to quotations.

## Key Concepts
- **Correlation**: The tendency of positions to suffer from the same event.
- **Cash balance**: Unallocated capital that reduces forced selling and funds bargains.
- **Liquidity**: The ability to enter or exit without moving price excessively.
- **Rebalancing**: Restoring position weights after price changes.
- **Opportunity cost**: What capital cannot buy because it is already deployed.

## Mental Models
Think of the portfolio as one claim on capital, not a collection of isolated successes. A new position must improve the whole portfolio's downside, not merely look attractive alone.

## Anti-patterns
- **Diversifying into inferior ideas**: Breadth is harmful when it imports fully priced risk.
- **Averaging down mechanically**: A lower price helps only if value and the thesis remain intact.
- **Concentrating correlated special situations**: Several different events can share one credit or liquidity cycle.
- **Using leverage to repair scarcity of ideas**: Leverage converts temporary mispricing into forced-sale risk.
- **Trading on every quotation**: Mr. Market should not set the agenda.

## Worked Example
Klarman treats portfolio construction as risk control rather than a search for maximum excitement. A practical allocation ranks qualified ideas by discount, downside clarity, liquidity, and correlation. The top idea may warrant a large position, while several distressed claims exposed to the same refinancing cycle count as one risk. Cash remains when the next candidate contributes only activity. This is a management rule, not a promise of higher return.

## Why It Works / Failure Modes
The framework preserves the margin of safety at portfolio level. It fails when investors underestimate hidden correlation, cannot distinguish a broken thesis from a lower price, or allow trading costs and impatience to consume the discount.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diversify risks, not ticker symbols.
2. Size positions by downside, understanding, and portfolio fit.
3. Cash is a legitimate holding when standards are unmet.
4. Trade against price extremes; do not let price dictate judgment.
5. Leverage can nullify careful security selection.
