# Chapter 9: Investment Research: The Challenge of Finding Attractive Investments

## Core Idea
Research is a search for mispricing, not an effort to know everything. The analyst must find where effort is rewarded and which facts determine downside and value.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Information Priority**: Spend time on facts that can change value, downside, or the reason for mispricing.
  - When to use: Planning research on a new security.
  - How: List the few questions whose answers would prevent purchase. Answer those from primary evidence before gathering background.
- **Primary-Source Discipline**: Prefer filings, financial statements, contracts, and direct industry evidence to summarized opinions.
  - When to use: Any recommendation originating from management, media, or brokers.
  - How: Verify claims in source documents and note conflicts and incentives.
- **Idea-Flow Management**: A repeating search process beats waiting for inspiration.
  - When to use: Maintaining a pipeline of bargains.
  - How: Scan neglected areas, forced transactions, spinoffs, distress, and unusual filings. Record why each candidate qualifies or fails.

## Key Concepts
- **Variant perception**: A supported view that differs from the market's embedded assumptions.
- **Primary evidence**: Original documents and data rather than commentary.
- **Neglected security**: A security with limited sponsorship or coverage.
- **Research funnel**: The progression from broad screen to full underwriting.
- **Disconfirming evidence**: Information that argues against the investment.

## Mental Models
Think of research as narrowing uncertainty at the points that affect the decision. Information that cannot change the action is not a priority, however interesting it is.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reading more opinions instead of evidence**: Volume of commentary can imitate knowledge.
- **Starting with management's story**: The narrative can frame later fact gathering.
- **Boiling the ocean**: Exhaustive collection delays action and obscures the decisive variables.
- **Seeking only confirmation**: A good process reserves early attention for what would make the idea fail.

## Worked Example
Klarman describes the practical difficulty of repeatedly finding bargains rather than valuing a business in the abstract. A working funnel might begin with a spinoff or distressed filing, then immediately ask: What is the security worth under conservative assumptions, why is the seller uneconomic, and what hidden liability could erase the discount? Only survivors receive full historical and industry work. This keeps effort proportional to opportunity.

## Why It Works / Failure Modes
Prioritization works because markets reward correctly identifying value gaps, not producing complete encyclopedias. It fails when analysts exclude unfamiliar but decisive information, especially liabilities, contracts, and customer concentration, merely to keep the checklist short.

## Key Takeaways
1. Research begins with the questions that could kill the thesis.
2. Primary documents outrank recommendations and narratives.
3. A repeatable funnel is necessary because bargains are rare.
4. Record failures so the same trap is recognizable next time.
