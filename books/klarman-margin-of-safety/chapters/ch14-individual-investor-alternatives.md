# Chapter 14: Investment Alternatives for the Individual Investor

## Core Idea
Individuals should choose the simplest vehicle that preserves their real edge. Delegation is useful only when the outside manager demonstrably follows a compatible, disciplined process.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Vehicle Selection by Edge**: Decide whether the investor's advantage is research time, small size, patience, or access to a manager.
  - When to use: Choosing among direct holdings, funds, and passive vehicles.
  - How: Match the vehicle to the advantage. Do not pay active fees where no active edge exists.
- **Manager Due Diligence**: Judge philosophy, constraints, incentives, downside history, and behavior in difficult markets.
  - When to use: Hiring a professional value investor.
  - How: Look for conservative valuation, willingness to hold cash, and resistance to fashion. Recent outperformance alone is insufficient.
- **Circle-of-Competence Allocation**: Own directly only what the individual can research and monitor.
  - When to use: Moving from principles to implementation.
  - How: Limit direct positions to understandable businesses and special situations. Delegate or avoid the rest.

## Key Concepts
- **Direct investing**: Owning securities selected by the individual.
- **Delegation**: Entrusting capital to a manager or fund.
- **Fee drag**: The return consumed by management and transaction costs.
- **Compatibility**: Alignment between a vehicle's process and value discipline.
- **Simplicity**: A structure whose risks the owner can actually monitor.

## Mental Models
Think of implementation as a risk that can undo the philosophy. The best security purchased through an unsuitable fund structure, fee arrangement, or leverage profile may not remain the intended investment.

## Anti-patterns
- **Hiring recent performance**: A hot record may reflect risk that has not surfaced.
- **Using a fund to bypass understanding**: Delegation transfers execution, not responsibility for suitability.
- **Collecting complex vehicles**: Options, margin, and fashionable alternatives can add risks outside the investor's competence.
- **Paying active prices for index behavior**: Fees require genuine discipline and opportunity.
- **Overestimating available time**: A sound direct-investment process requires continuing research.

## Worked Example
Klarman closes by translating institutional practice into choices available to individuals. Reconstruct the decision in two gates. First, can the investor independently perform research, valuation, and portfolio control? If not, direct special-situation investing is inappropriate. Second, if hiring help, compare managers on cash discipline, downside behavior, concentration, fees, and evidence of value investing during speculative periods. Choose the documented process, not the most flattering recent number.

## Why It Works / Failure Modes
Vehicle fit prevents a correct philosophy from being implemented incorrectly. It fails when investors use simplicity as an excuse to skip manager or security analysis, or treat a famous value label as proof of current discipline.

## Key Takeaways
1. Choose direct investing only where research capacity is real.
2. Manager selection is process due diligence, not return chasing.
3. Fees, leverage, and complexity can consume the margin of safety.
4. Small, patient capital has opportunities unavailable to large institutions.
5. The owner retains responsibility for suitability.
