# Klarman Decision Cheatsheet

## Purchase Gate

1. Can the business or claim be valued from evidence? If no, pass.
2. Does conservative value substantially exceed price after adverse assumptions? If no, pass.
3. Is the return paid by cash flow, value growth, or gap closure? If it needs a future buyer, treat as speculation.
4. What constraint or error explains the price? If impairment explains it, pass.
5. Does the position improve portfolio downside and correlation? If no, pass or resize.

## Decision Rules

- When price equals optimistic value, do not buy; apparent quality does not replace a discount.
- When methods disagree widely, investigate the disagreement rather than average it away.
- When a seller chooses the timing, as in a new issue, require extra discount and primary-document proof.
- When only historical yield supports a credit, reject the pitch and inspect structure, collateral, and recovery.
- When a catalyst can fail, underwrite the failed-catalyst case before giving the event any value.
- When several positions depend on one credit, legal, or liquidity outcome, count them as one risk.
- When no qualified bargain exists, hold cash; do not weaken the required discount.
- When a lower price follows a broken thesis, do not average down.
- When leverage is required to make the result attractive, the margin of safety is not real.
- When hiring a manager, buy the documented process, not its recent ranking.

## Valuation Choices

| Situation | Primary evidence | Reject when |
|---|---|---|
| Stable operating business | Normalized free cash flow plus asset check | Growth consumes all cash or peak margins are treated as permanent |
| Asset-heavy business | Realizable assets minus claims | Accounting book value ignores bad assets |
| Cyclical business | Mid-cycle results and balance-sheet survival | Trough earnings make a peak-cycle business look expensive, or the reverse |
| Corporate event | Value with and without catalyst | Event success is the only source of return |
| Distressed claim | Estate value and priority waterfall | Recovery depends on par value or junior optimism |
| Conversion or issuance | Pro forma tangible value and incentives | Historical deal returns replace current mechanics |

## Portfolio Defaults

- Size up only where discount, understanding, liquidity, and independence overlap.
- Size down for complexity, doubtful collateral, long legal timing, or correlation.
- Cash is suitable for near-term obligations and for capital awaiting qualified bargains.
- Direct ownership requires time to read filings and monitor disconfirming evidence.
- Delegation requires compatible incentives and evidence of discipline in speculative markets.

## Warning Smells

- "The asset class has always worked."
- "The yield compensates for the risk," without recovery analysis.
- "It is liquid, so it is safe."
- "The benchmark owns it."
- "The rating says it is suitable."
- "Management forecasts justify the precision."
- "The event will make the market notice."
- "The equity is cheap because it fell."
- "A lower price automatically improves the bargain."
- "Recent outperformance proves the manager is disciplined."
