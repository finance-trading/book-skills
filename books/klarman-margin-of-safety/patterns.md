# Patterns

## Investment-Speculation Gate
**When to use**: Before any purchase motivated by momentum, narrative, or urgency.
**How**: Write the cash-flow source, conservative value, and price gap. If return requires another buyer at a higher quotation, label it speculation and size or reject it accordingly.
**Trade-offs**: Misses profitable momentum trades; prevents speculative positions from being disguised as investments.

## Incentive Audit
**When to use**: A broker, underwriter, consultant, or manager introduces an idea.
**How**: Identify compensation, timing, and whether a transaction benefits the introducer. Read primary filings before accepting the supplied narrative.
**Trade-offs**: Slows response to genuinely useful introductions; removes a major source of hidden bias.

## Historical-Average Challenge
**When to use**: A security is justified by the past performance of its asset class.
**How**: Compare today's structure, covenants, leverage, collateral, and buyer base with those in the cited history. Underwrite the individual security.
**Trade-offs**: Can reject a fairly priced innovation; avoids false comfort from changed samples.

## Three-Method Valuation
**When to use**: A business appears cheap on one metric.
**How**: Prepare asset, normalized-cash-flow, and relevant transaction or liquidation cases. Use conservative inputs and reconcile major disagreements.
**Trade-offs**: Less precise than a single model; much harder for one hidden assumption to bless a purchase.

## Thesis-Killer Research
**When to use**: A new idea enters the research funnel.
**How**: List the few facts that would invalidate value or downside protection. Investigate those in primary sources before background reading.
**Trade-offs**: May miss interesting context at the start; concentrates effort where it changes action.

## Constraint-Source Search
**When to use**: Searching for bargains or explaining a low price.
**How**: Identify mandate, size, rating, liquidity, index, distress, or complexity constraints. Confirm the absent buyers and test whether impairment explains the price.
**Trade-offs**: Neglected areas can contain poor businesses; the explicit constraint test reduces blind contrarianism.

## Catalyst Separation
**When to use**: Spinoffs, tenders, reorganizations, conversions, or other corporate events.
**How**: Value the outcome without the event, then value the accelerated outcome. Buy only when the no-event case still offers protection.
**Trade-offs**: Rejects attractive event trades that lack a discount; avoids paying away the margin of safety.

## Priority Waterfall
**When to use**: Distressed debt, bankruptcy, or any layered capital structure.
**How**: Estimate estate value, then allocate it through secured claims, senior claims, and junior claims. Include fees, financing, time, and dilution.
**Trade-offs**: Requires legal and process understanding; prevents junior securities from being priced in isolation.

## Conversion Worksheet
**When to use**: Mutual conversions or similar first-time ownership transactions.
**How**: Calculate proceeds, shares, pro forma tangible value, reserves, restrictions, and insider participation. Compare price with stress-tested value.
**Trade-offs**: Historical deal patterns may not recur; mechanics reveal whether any discount remains.

## Portfolio Risk Budget
**When to use**: Adding, enlarging, or replacing a position.
**How**: Group correlated risks, rank ideas by downside and understanding, and leave cash when candidates do not improve the portfolio. Avoid leverage that creates forced selling.
**Trade-offs**: Will not maximize exposure during strong markets; preserves capital and decision flexibility.

## Manager Process Screen
**When to use**: Delegating capital to a professional or fund.
**How**: Examine valuation discipline, cash holding, downside history, incentives, concentration, and behavior during fashionable markets. Do not select on recent rank alone.
**Trade-offs**: May reject a temporarily successful manager; improves alignment with the intended philosophy.
