## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

Use **Investment Versus Speculation** first. Investors buy fractional business ownership or loans after comparing conservative value with price. Speculators bet on where another buyer will take the quotation. A qualifying investment is paid by free cash flow, growth in underlying value, or closure of a price-value gap—not by the mere hope that a quotation rises.

Apply **Margin of Safety** to every purchase. Estimate value conservatively, assume the estimate contains errors, and buy only at a discount large enough to absorb bad luck and business deterioration. A good business, famous manager, or persuasive forecast does not replace that discount. When qualifying discounts are scarce, hold cash rather than lower the standard.

Use **Business Value Before Security Price**. Start bottom-up with competitive position, sustainable free cash flow, balance sheet, capital allocation, and claims priority. Translate those facts into a valuation range using net assets, normalized cash flow, liquidation value, and relevant private-market evidence. Buy against the low end of the defensible range; investigate methods that disagree instead of averaging away the conflict.

Ask **Whose Interest Does This Serve?** whenever Wall Street, a manager, or an institution supplies the idea. Brokers and underwriters benefit from activity; institutions may minimize career and tracking-error risk rather than beneficiary risk. Prefer primary documents to recommendations. Treat new issues, fashionable products, and historical-return marketing with extra skepticism.

Use the **Junk-Bond Lesson** as the template for financial fashion. Yield, ratings, and historical asset-class returns are not underwriting. Inspect issuance purpose, leverage, covenants, seniority, collateral, and loss severity. Distinguish fallen angels from securities that were weak when issued, and follow risky layers as they are relabeled for new buyers.

Search with the **Inefficiency Map**. Look for forced sellers, spinoffs, neglect, mandate exclusions, complexity, distress, and small structures such as thrift conversions. Identify the constraint that explains why an institution is absent. Then verify that the price is below conservative value rather than correctly reflecting deterioration or dilution.

Treat a **Catalyst** as an accelerator, never as the bargain itself. Prefer dated, contractual, or cash-realizing events—reorganizations, tenders, asset sales, conversions—over a hope that the market notices. The position must remain acceptable if recognition is delayed or the event fails.

For distress, use **Claims-Priority Valuation**. Value the estate, then allocate that value through collateral and seniority until it is exhausted. Identify the fulcrum security, fees, financing, dilution, and timing. Never anchor to pre-distress par or buy residual equity merely because its price looks low.

Build the portfolio with **Downside-First Sizing**. Diversify independent risks without adding inferior ideas. Size positions by discount, understanding, liquidity, and correlation; count several economically linked situations as one risk. Avoid leverage that can force sale before convergence. Trade against price extremes and recheck the thesis before averaging down.

Select an implementation vehicle through **Edge and Suitability**. Invest directly only within a real research capacity. If delegating, inspect process, incentives, cash discipline, downside behavior, and fees; do not hire a trailing return. Match illiquid or volatile bargains solely to patient capital.
