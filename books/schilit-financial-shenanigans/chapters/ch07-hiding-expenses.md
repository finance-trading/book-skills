# Chapter 7: Earnings Manipulation Shenanigan No. 5 — Employing Other Techniques to Hide Expenses or Losses

## Core Idea
Expenses can disappear without becoming an asset: they can be omitted, understated through assumptions, or reduced by releasing earlier reserves.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three expense-suppression tests**: look for omitted current invoices or obligations, assumptions that drive unusually low accruals, and reserve releases that offset operating costs.
- **Assumption roll-forward**: compare lease, self-insurance, pension, and other estimates with prior periods; separate actual economic improvement from a changed estimate.
- **Reserve bridge**: calculate opening balance + new charges − usage − releases = closing balance, then trace releases into income.

## Key Concepts
- **Accrual**: expense recorded when incurred, not when cash is paid.
- **Reserve release**: reversal of a prior liability estimate, reducing current expense.
- **Pension assumption**: actuarial input that changes recognized pension cost.
- **Self-insurance reserve**: estimate of future claims borne by the company.
- **Late invoice**: an obligation that still belongs to the current period if services were received.

## Mental Models
- Ask **what happened to the obligation?** when a cost ratio improves abruptly.
- Treat a reserve as a **stored earnings lever** until the roll-forward proves its release was warranted.

## Anti-patterns
- **Mistaking a reversed accrual for operating improvement**: a one-time release can flatter the quarter.
- **Accepting benign-sounding estimate changes without quantified effects**: assumptions can dominate the reported result.

## Worked Example
The book describes Under Armour's 2016 fourth quarter: after missing performance targets, it reversed previously accrued employee bonuses, lowering reported expense. The reversal can be appropriate if the bonus truly will not be paid, but it does not mean the apparel business improved. An analyst would separate that benefit from recurring operating margin and ask why the underlying target was missed.

## Detection Matrix
| Expense lever | Reconciliation | Decision point |
|---|---|---|
| Late invoice omitted | Match receipt of service to invoice and period | Was the obligation incurred before year-end? |
| Lease or pension assumption revised | Compute cost under old and new assumption | Is the economic evidence consistent with the new input? |
| Self-insurance reserve reduced | Compare claims history, cash settlements, and reserve | Is loss experience actually improving? |
| Prior reserve released | Roll forward balance and trace income effect | Did a real liability expire, or was profit supported by old accruals? |

## Key Takeaways
1. Reconcile expenses to obligations, not just cash paid.
2. Quantify estimate changes and reserve releases in a margin bridge.
3. Distinguish an accounting correction from improved unit economics.
