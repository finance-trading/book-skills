# Chapter 15: Acquisition Accounting Shenanigan No. 1 — Artificially Boosting Revenue and Earnings

## Core Idea
Deals create multiple windows for moving costs or sales across the close. Compare target, buyer, and combined company before and after the acquisition on a consistent basis.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four deal-earnings tests**: inspect pre-close target actions, losses hidden at closing, dubious new revenue streams after closing, and reserve releases around closing.
- **Stub-period bridge**: compare target sales, inventory, reserves, and expenses in the period immediately before close with the first consolidated quarters. Look for a drop then rebound that flatters the acquirer.

## Key Concepts
- **Stub period**: short interval between reporting dates and deal completion.
- **Purchase accounting**: recognition and valuation of acquired assets and liabilities.
- **Deal reserve**: acquisition-related liability whose release can later raise earnings.
- **Organic growth**: change excluding acquired businesses, requiring careful scope definitions.
- **One-time revenue stream**: unusual deal-related proceeds presented as recurring sales.

## Mental Models
- Think of a deal as a **cutoff event**, then trace what was shifted to either side.
- Treat the acquirer's post-close jump as **unproven organic improvement** until target history is included.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring target pre-close weakness** once its results are consolidated.
- **Assuming all purchase-accounting changes are economically neutral** to future profit.

## Worked Example
The book examines Salix before and after Valeant's purchase. Salix sales accelerated while a sale was sought, fell sharply during the closing window, then rebounded after Valeant consolidated the business. The authors use the pattern to motivate deeper work, not to let a chart alone prove intent. An analyst would examine channel inventory, returns, end-customer demand, and purchase-accounting disclosures to reconstruct comparable sales.

## Deal-Close Checklist
| Window | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Before close | Did target sales or expenses change unusually? | Quarterly target filings, channel inventory, returns |
| Stub period | Were sales delayed or costs accelerated? | Close date, purchase agreement, short-period results |
| At close | Were losses hidden in opening acquired balances? | Purchase-price allocation and liability notes |
| After close | Did reserves release or “new” revenue appear? | Reserve roll-forward and related transaction terms |

Show the target's independent history beside the buyer's consolidated numbers; the closing date otherwise becomes an artificial comparison boundary.

## Key Takeaways
1. Collect target results before close, including the stub period.
2. Reconcile reserve and purchase-accounting changes to later income.
3. Normalize the combined company's revenue base before judging growth.
