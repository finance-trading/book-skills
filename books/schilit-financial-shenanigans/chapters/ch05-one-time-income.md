# Chapter 5: Earnings Manipulation Shenanigan No. 3 — Boosting Income Using One-Time or Unsustainable Activities

## Core Idea
Even correctly recorded gains can mislead when they are framed as recurring operating performance. Rebuild sustainable income by separating one-off transactions and classification choices.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Recurring-profit bridge**: start with reported operating income, identify asset sales, unusual gains, reserve releases, and shifted expenses, then calculate a repeatable operating measure with explicit adjustments.
- **Above-/below-the-line review**: compare classification of normal costs and nonoperating gains across periods; inspect whether a change flatters operating profit.

## Key Concepts
- **One-time event**: a transaction that does not recur through normal operations.
- **Operating income**: profit attributed to core operations, highly sensitive to classification.
- **Below the line**: presentation outside the operating measure investors emphasize.
- **Unsustainable gain**: real current income with no dependable future stream.
- **Balance-sheet classification**: placing a cost in assets or liabilities rather than current profit.

## Mental Models
- Use a **repeatability test**: can the same profit source plausibly recur without selling another asset or changing presentation?
- Separate **accounting legitimacy from investment relevance**: a GAAP-compliant item may still be unhelpful for forecasting.

## Anti-patterns
- **Annualizing an asset-sale gain**: this inflates a core earnings run rate.
- **Ignoring margin mismatches**: revenue and gross profit may weaken while reported operating income surges.

## Worked Example
In the book's IBM discussion, 1999 revenue rose 7.2% and cost of goods and services rose 9.5%, yet operating and pretax profits jumped about 30%. The discrepancy is the starting question, not proof of wrongdoing. An analyst should reconcile the income-statement lines, identify gains and reclassifications, and calculate a core margin excluding items that cannot repeat.

## Reference Table
| Item in reported profit | Analytical treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Asset or business sale gain | Show separately from operating trend | A second sale is needed to recur |
| Normal cost moved below operating income | Restore to the operating cost base | Presentation changed, economics did not |
| Nonoperating gain moved above operating income | Remove from core operating margin | It does not arise from customer service |
| Cost placed on balance sheet | Assess asset benefit and future amortization | Current profit is higher at later-period expense |

Keep the GAAP subtotal alongside the adjusted bridge so readers can audit each judgment.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reconcile reported profit growth to revenue and gross-margin trends.
2. Show one-off and reclassification adjustments explicitly.
3. Forecast the business, not the most flattering reported subtotal.
