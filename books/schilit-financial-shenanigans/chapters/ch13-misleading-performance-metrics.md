# Chapter 13: Key Metric Shenanigan No. 1 — Showcasing Misleading Metrics That Overstate Performance

## Core Idea
Management-selected substitutes for revenue, earnings, or cash flow can steer attention away from deteriorating GAAP economics. Reconcile every favored metric to its components and incentives.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three metric-substitution tests**: identify a surrogate for revenue (same-store sales, ARPU, subscribers, bookings), for earnings (EBITDA, adjusted earnings), or for cash flow (“cash earnings” or custom cash measures). Reconcile the surrogate to the statutory statements.
- **Definition consistency test**: track changes in numerator, denominator, exclusions, acquisition scope, and period; restate history before comparing.

## Key Concepts
- **Same-store sales**: sales growth at a defined set of existing stores; the store set matters.
- **ARPU**: average revenue per user; user definitions and mix affect it.
- **Bookings/backlog**: orders or committed work, not automatically recognized revenue.
- **EBITDA**: earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization; not cash flow.
- **Adjusted earnings**: non-GAAP income after management-selected exclusions.

## Mental Models
- Ask **what must be excluded for this metric to look good?**
- Separate **leading indicators** from **realized economics**: bookings can signal demand but do not settle revenue quality or profitability.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling EBITDA cash flow**: it ignores capital spending and working capital.
- **Accepting recurring “one-time” exclusions**: repeated costs remain economically relevant.

## Worked Example
Groupon's adjusted consolidated segment operating income excluded online marketing, stock compensation, and acquisition costs. The book contrasts the flattering adjusted measure with large GAAP operating losses, and notes SEC objections to the metric. The useful analyst artifact is a bridge from GAAP loss to management's adjusted number, with each add-back assessed for recurrence and business necessity.

## Metric Audit Table
| Management highlights | Required bridge | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Same-store sales or ARPU | Definition of store/user cohort and total revenue | Selected cohort hides weaker consolidated demand |
| Bookings and backlog | Conversion to revenue and cancellation history | Demand proxy is mistaken for earned sales |
| EBITDA or adjusted profit | GAAP profit plus each excluded cost | Repeated necessary spending disappears |
| “Cash earnings” | CFFO, capex, working capital, and deal cash | Earnings proxy is mislabeled as cash generation |

A useful non-GAAP metric should clarify economics while retaining a reproducible bridge to the audited statements.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reconcile non-GAAP metrics to GAAP and cash flow.
2. Rebuild historical series when definitions change.
3. Decide whether an excluded cost can truly be avoided.
