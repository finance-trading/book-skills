# Chapter 3: Earnings Manipulation Shenanigan No. 1 — Recording Revenue Too Soon

## Core Idea
A signed order, shipment, or management forecast does not necessarily mean revenue has been earned. Test whether obligations, acceptance, and collectability support the recognition date.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four premature-revenue tests**: check (1) material performance obligations, (2) percentage-of-completion assumptions and progress, (3) final customer acceptance, and (4) ability and obligation to pay. Compare contract terms with the reporting cutoff.
- **Policy and estimate change check**: if revenue accelerates after a new policy, milestone definition, or project estimate, recompute prior and current periods on a common basis.

## Key Concepts
- **Cutoff**: the period in which a sale is recorded.
- **Percentage of completion (POC)**: recognition based on estimated work performed; estimates can be advanced aggressively.
- **Final acceptance**: customer sign-off that may be required before a sale is earned.
- **Bill-and-hold or third-party shipment**: arrangements that require close scrutiny of control and delivery.
- **Extended payment terms**: concessions that can undermine collectability or economic substance.

## Mental Models
- Use a **contract-to-cash trace**: contract → performance → acceptance → billing → collection.
- Treat quarter-end concentration as a **cutoff risk**, then inspect subsequent returns, concessions, and receivable aging.

## Anti-patterns
- **Contract-signing equals revenue**: unfinished service obligations invalidate the shortcut.
- **Blindly trusting POC**: changing cost-to-complete estimates can pull future revenue forward.

## Worked Example
MicroStrategy urged customers to sign contracts just before quarter-end and treated signing as the decisive event. The book also flags sales to parties in which it had invested. An analyst would obtain the contract terms, identify remaining services, test whether the buyer could reject the product, and compare recognized revenue with subsequent cash collection. The case illustrates why a plausible sale can still be recognized too early.

## Detection Matrix
| Trigger | Test | Contrary evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue at signature | Identify undelivered services and acceptance clauses | Completed performance and enforceable acceptance before cutoff |
| POC revenue accelerates | Compare estimated total project cost and percent complete with prior estimates | Independent progress documentation supports the revision |
| Revenue before delivery | Trace shipping destination and customer control | Customer controlled the product by period-end |
| Receivables stretch | Read payment concessions and collectability evidence | Ordinary terms and prompt collection without side concessions |

Use the matrix to frame questions. The applicable revenue standard and contract facts decide recognition; an unusual ratio alone does not.

## Key Takeaways
1. Verify the earning event, not merely the invoice or signed contract.
2. Normalize policy and estimate changes before comparing growth.
3. Follow late-period sales into receivables, returns, and collections.
