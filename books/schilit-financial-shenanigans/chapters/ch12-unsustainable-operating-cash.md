# Chapter 12: Cash Flow Shenanigan No. 3 — Boosting Operating Cash Flow Using Unsustainable Activities

## Core Idea
Better CFFO can result from timing cash receipts and payments rather than stronger sales or margins. Quantify the working-capital contribution and its limits.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four timing tests**: check slower vendor payment, faster customer collection or prepayments, reduced inventory purchases, and one-time benefits.
- **Working-capital bridge**: break the change in CFFO into earnings, receivables, payables, inventory, deferred revenue, and other items; assess whether each source can repeat.

## Key Concepts
- **Days payable outstanding (DPO)**: average time taken to pay suppliers.
- **Customer prepayment**: cash received before the future product or service is delivered.
- **Inventory drawdown**: cash conserved by buying less inventory, possibly at the cost of future sales or replenishment.
- **CFFO**: cash flow from operations; genuine cash that may still be temporary.
- **Working capital**: operating assets and liabilities whose timing affects CFFO.

## Mental Models
- Ask **can the company stretch this source again next year?** An improvement that reaches a ceiling is not a durable growth rate.
- Distinguish **cash conversion** from **cash extraction**: squeezing suppliers can temporarily accelerate cash but strain relationships.

## Anti-patterns
- **Equating a CFFO jump with better demand** without a line-item bridge.
- **Treating customer advances as free cash** while ignoring the obligation to deliver.

## Worked Example
The book describes Home Depot's first year under Bob Nardelli. CFFO more than doubled, but payable days increased from 22 to 34 as vendors were paid later. An analyst would quantify how much of the CFFO gain came from payables, then ask what payment terms could sustainably remain. The cash was real; the inference of improved operating productivity was the problem.

## Reference Table
| CFFO source | How to test duration | Likely limit |
|---|---|---|
| Slower vendor payment | Track DPO and contract terms | Suppliers may demand payment or prices may rise |
| Faster customer collection | Track DSO and discounts | Receivables cannot fall indefinitely |
| Customer prepayment | Match future delivery obligation | Cash is borrowed from future service periods |
| Less inventory purchasing | Compare turnover, stockouts, replenishment | Inventory must eventually support sales |
| One-off tax or settlement receipt | Identify transaction and repeatability | No recurring operating source |

## Key Takeaways
1. Build a working-capital bridge whenever CFFO diverges from sales or profit.
2. Assess the reversibility and capacity of each cash source.
3. Show a normalized CFFO alongside the reported figure.
