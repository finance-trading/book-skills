# Chapter 16: Acquisition Accounting Shenanigan No. 2 — Inflating Reported Cash Flow

## Core Idea
A serial acquirer can report strong operating cash flow while repeatedly paying for the assets and customers that produce it in investing cash flow. Evaluate cash after acquisitions and distinguish inherited cash from organically generated cash.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three acquisition-CFFO channels**: inherited operating inflows, purchased contracts or customers whose cash cost is classified as investing, and creatively structured disposals.
- **Free cash flow after acquisitions**: begin with CFFO, subtract capital expenditure and cash acquisition spending, then explain what part of the spending is maintenance of the business model versus optional expansion.

## Key Concepts
- **Inherited CFFO**: acquired operating assets or contracts that generate post-close operating cash.
- **Acquisition cash outflow**: purchase price, normally presented in investing activities.
- **Purchased customer base**: customer relationships bought rather than developed through operating spending.
- **Roll-up**: strategy that relies on frequent acquisitions.
- **Restricted cash**: cash with limitations that can obscure available liquidity.

## Mental Models
- Ask **what cash did the company spend to acquire the stream now called operating cash?**
- Model serial acquisitions as a **recurring input** if the company cannot maintain growth without them.

## Anti-patterns
- **Relying on CFFO alone for a roll-up**: it omits acquisition spending.
- **Classifying the acquisition program as wholly optional** when targets supply continuing customer relationships.

## Worked Example
Tyco bought more than 700 companies for about $29 billion during 1999–2002, according to the book. Reported CFFO looked strong, yet the authors show negative free cash flow after acquisitions in those years. An analyst would present both reported CFFO and cash after deal spending, identify which acquired assets produced the cash inflows, and ask how much deal spending was needed to sustain the operating base.

## Cash-Flow Bridge
| Layer | Question |
|---|---|
| Reported CFFO | How much came from the legacy business versus acquired receivables/contracts? |
| Less capital expenditure | Which assets require regular replacement? |
| Less acquisition cash spending | How much was needed to buy recurring customers, contracts, or capacity? |
| Plus/minus disposal proceeds | Are sales of businesses being confused with operations? |
| Cash after acquisitions | Can the strategy fund itself without fresh borrowing or equity? |

Do not mechanically treat every deal dollar as maintenance. State a range or classification when organic capacity and strategic expansion cannot be separated.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pair CFFO with the cost of acquiring the cash-generating assets.
2. Separate inherited working-capital inflows from ongoing unit economics.
3. Make the acquisition-spending assumption explicit when comparing firms.
