# Chapter 6: Earnings Manipulation Shenanigan No. 4 — Shifting Current Expenses to a Later Period

## Core Idea
Capitalizing an operating cost, stretching asset lives, or withholding write-downs moves current expense into future periods and overstates current profit.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four deferral mechanisms**: test excessive capitalization, slow depreciation/amortization, unrecognized impairment or obsolete inventory, and inadequate allowances for receivables or investments.
- **Capex-to-FCF check**: when profit rises with capital expenditure, ask whether normal operating costs have migrated to investing cash outflow; compare FCF rather than CFFO alone.
- **Useful-life challenge**: benchmark asset lives and reserve assumptions against prior years and peers; quantify the earnings effect of changes.

## Key Concepts
- **Capitalization**: recording a cost as an asset rather than an immediate expense.
- **Amortization period**: the schedule that allocates an asset's cost to later periods.
- **Impairment**: loss of asset value that should be recognized when required.
- **Allowance**: estimate of receivables or loans that will not be collected.
- **Free cash flow**: CFFO less capex; a cross-check when capitalization moves cash outflows.

## Mental Models
- Follow a cost through **expense → asset → future depreciation**.
- Compare **income and cash economics**: capitalization can lift both earnings and CFFO while FCF weakens.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling all capex growth investment**: a policy change can conceal routine operating spend.
- **Accepting stable reserves despite worsening collections**: delayed loss recognition can mask credit deterioration.

## Worked Example
WorldCom capitalized line costs that belonged in current operations. The book notes free cash flow deteriorating from positive $2.3 billion in 1999 to negative $3.8 billion as reported capital spending jumped. An analyst would compare line-cost ratios and capex guidance, rebuild FCF, and examine whether the new assets produced genuine future benefits. The mismatch was a warning even before the full fraud became public.

## Detection Matrix
| Mechanism | Early clue | Rebuild |
|---|---|---|
| Operating cost capitalized | New asset category or capex surge without clear productive assets | Expense the questionable cost for an analytical sensitivity |
| Asset life extended | Depreciation rate falls while assets age | Estimate expense under prior useful life |
| Impairment delayed | Weak asset cash flow but stable carrying value | Compare recoverable value assumptions and later write-downs |
| Bad-debt allowance suppressed | Receivables age while reserve ratio falls | Re-estimate expected loss with collection evidence |

None of these indicators settles whether accounting was improper. The aim is to expose the earnings sensitivity to assumptions and policy choices.

## Key Takeaways
1. Investigate cost capitalization that departs from the company's economics or history.
2. Stress-test lives, impairments, and bad-debt reserves.
3. Reconcile CFFO with capex and FCF before concluding cash generation improved.
