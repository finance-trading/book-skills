# Chapter 10: Cash Flow Shenanigan No. 1 — Shifting Financing Cash Inflows to the Operating Section

## Core Idea
CFFO can rise when the company monetizes future collections or disguises financing, even if customer-generated cash has not improved.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three financing-to-CFFO channels**: inspect bank borrowing disguised as operations, real receivable sales that accelerate future collections, and sham receivable sales that remain financing in substance.
- **Receivable-sale bridge**: identify gross receivables sold, cash received, fees, recourse, and subsequent-period effects; rebuild cash flow without the sale.

## Key Concepts
- **CFFO**: cash flow from operations, a classification-sensitive measure.
- **CFFF**: cash flow from financing, where true borrowing belongs.
- **Factoring/securitization**: transferring receivables for earlier cash.
- **Recourse**: seller obligation to absorb losses if sold receivables do not pay.
- **Off-balance-sheet structure**: entity or arrangement that can obscure financing economics.

## Mental Models
- Ask **who ultimately owes the cash and who bears collection risk?**
- Treat a receivable sale as **future cash pulled forward** when evaluating a growth rate.

## Anti-patterns
- **Annualizing factoring-driven CFFO**: the next period starts with fewer receivables to collect.
- **Taking legal sale form as economic substance**: retained risk may mean financing.

## Worked Example
Cardinal Health sold $800 million of receivables by its December 2004 quarter. The book attributes much of the period's strong CFFO growth to that acceleration. A cash-flow bridge would subtract the one-time factoring proceeds, compare customer collection trends, and check whether the next quarters inherited a collection shortfall. The transaction produced cash but did not demonstrate a stronger underlying business.

## Detection Matrix
| Cash source | Economic classification question | Analyst adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Borrowing via special entity | Is repayment still an obligation of the company? | Present cash as financing in sensitivity |
| Genuine receivable sale | How much future customer cash was advanced? | Remove sale proceeds from sustainable CFFO growth |
| Purported sale with recourse | Who retains credit and collection risk? | Treat as borrowing if substance supports it |
| Bogus revenue collection | Who supplied the cash ultimately? | Trace payer and related financing before calling it CFFO |

## Key Takeaways
1. Separate customer cash from financing or asset monetization.
2. Read receivable-sale notes and recourse terms.
3. Recalculate CFFO growth without unusual factoring proceeds.
