# Forensic Patterns

## Three-Statement Triangulation
**When to use**: A headline profit, cash, or balance-sheet improvement seems unusually strong.  
**How**: Trace the same transaction through income, assets/liabilities, and cash; compare related ratios across periods.  
**Trade-offs**: Classification differences can have legitimate explanations; request the relevant contract and footnotes before assigning intent.

## Revenue Contract-to-Cash Trace
**When to use**: Late-quarter revenue accelerates or receivables rise faster than sales.  
**How**: Check contractual obligations, delivery, acceptance, payment duty, side letters, invoices, and later collections.  
**Trade-offs**: Collection delays do not alone prove bogus revenue; industry terms and customer mix matter.

## Recurring-Profit Bridge
**When to use**: Operating income outgrows sales or gross profit.  
**How**: Reconcile reported income to asset-sale gains, reserve releases, reclassifications, and changed assumptions; calculate a disclosed repeatable-profit estimate.  
**Trade-offs**: The bridge is an analyst estimate, not a substitute for GAAP.

## Expense and Reserve Roll-Forward
**When to use**: Expense ratios improve or reserves move sharply.  
**How**: Opening reserve + charges − actual use − releases = closing reserve; inspect the reason and income effect of each line.  
**Trade-offs**: A warranted reversal still changes comparability but is not automatically manipulation.

## Capitalization and Free-Cash-Flow Check
**When to use**: Profit and CFFO rise while capex also jumps.  
**How**: Examine new capitalized cost categories, useful lives, capex guidance, and FCF after recurring asset spending.  
**Trade-offs**: Genuine expansion also raises capex; compare operating need and asset benefits.

## CFFO Source Bridge
**When to use**: CFFO rises much faster than sales or profit.  
**How**: Separate earnings, receivable sales, payables, inventory, customer advances, acquisitions, and classification changes. Mark each source repeatable or temporary.  
**Trade-offs**: Temporary cash is real cash; the judgment concerns its growth sustainability.

## Receivable-Sale Adjustment
**When to use**: CFFO improves and DSO falls around factoring or securitization.  
**How**: Identify sold balance and recourse, then show CFFO and DSO with sold receivables added back for comparability.  
**Trade-offs**: Factoring may be sound cash management; the adjustment tests operating improvement.

## Non-GAAP Definition Audit
**When to use**: Management emphasizes EBITDA, adjusted earnings, bookings, or custom cash flow.  
**How**: Reconcile to GAAP and cash; list excluded costs and changes in definition; restate historical comparisons.  
**Trade-offs**: Some non-GAAP measures are useful when definitions are stable and costs are truly nonrecurring.

## Acquisition Cohort Bridge
**When to use**: A serial acquirer reports strong sales, profit, or CFFO.  
**How**: Gather target pre-close and stub-period history, pro forma combined sales, purchase-accounting reserves, deal cash spending, and post-close contribution.  
**Trade-offs**: Target disclosures may be incomplete; mark organic growth as unavailable rather than fabricating precision.

## Dated Red-Flag Log
**When to use**: Several individually small clues appear over time.  
**How**: Log each policy, metric, governance, compensation, auditor, and disclosure change; ask “why?” and “why now?”; separate evidence from hypothesis.  
**Trade-offs**: Red flags are not a forecast of fraud or an exact catalyst date.
