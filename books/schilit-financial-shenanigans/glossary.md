# Glossary

**Accrual** — expense or revenue recorded when economically incurred or earned rather than when cash moves (Ch 7).

**Acquisition accounting** — recognition and valuation of acquired assets and liabilities, with effects on later income and cash-flow presentation (Ch 15–17).

**Adjusted earnings** — non-GAAP profit after management-selected exclusions; test whether excluded costs recur (Ch 13, 17).

**Allowance** — estimate of receivables or loans unlikely to be collected (Ch 6).

**Arm's-length transaction** — deal negotiated by independent parties on commercial terms (Ch 4).

**Big bath** — exceptionally large current write-off or charge that can reduce future expense (Ch 9).

**Bookings/backlog** — orders or contracted work that do not by themselves equal earned revenue (Ch 13).

**Boomerang transaction** — linked cash payments and receipts whose classification can flatter CFFO (Ch 11).

**CFFO** — cash flow from operations; a useful but classification- and timing-sensitive measure (Ch 1, 10–12, 16).

**Channel stuffing** — pushing product into distribution ahead of end demand to accelerate sales (Ch 18).

**Days payable outstanding (DPO)** — approximate time a company takes to pay suppliers (Ch 12).

**Days sales outstanding (DSO)** — receivables relative to sales; factoring can lower it mechanically (Ch 14).

**Deferred revenue / unearned revenue** — payment or billings tied to unfulfilled obligations (Ch 8).

**Economic substance** — actual transfer of risks, benefits, and commercial value beyond legal form (Ch 4).

**EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization; not a cash-flow measure (Ch 13).

**Factoring** — sale of receivables to accelerate cash; assess recourse and future collection effects (Ch 10, 14).

**Free cash flow** — CFFO less capital expenditure in the book's basic cross-check; adjust for required deal spending when relevant (Ch 1, 6, 16).

**Gross versus net revenue** — whether an entity reports full transaction value or its net fee, based on its role (Ch 4).

**Organic growth** — growth attributed to existing businesses; requires a stable definition and comparable scope (Ch 15, 17).

**Percentage-of-completion accounting** — revenue recognized from estimated work performed, exposing results to estimate changes (Ch 3).

**Pro forma revenue** — hypothetical combined-company sales used to compare periods around an acquisition (Ch 17).

**Recourse** — retained obligation to absorb losses on transferred receivables (Ch 10).

**Reserve release** — reversal of a prior accrued liability, potentially lifting current income (Ch 7–9, 15).

**Restatement** — revision of previously issued financial statements (Ch 18).

**Same-store sales** — sales growth for a selected set of existing stores; store-selection rules matter (Ch 13).

**Side agreement** — separate term or concession that can undermine an apparent sale (Ch 4).

**Stub period** — short period immediately before deal close, important to post-close comparisons (Ch 8, 15).

**Working capital** — operating assets and liabilities whose timing changes CFFO (Ch 12).
