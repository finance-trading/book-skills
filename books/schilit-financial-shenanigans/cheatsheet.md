# Forensic Decision Sheet

| If you observe… | Rebuild or inspect… | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue accelerates near quarter-end | Contract obligations, acceptance, side letters, later cash | Signing and billing can precede earning (Ch 3–4). |
| Earnings rise faster than sales or gross profit | One-off gains, shifted costs, reserve releases | Presentation can mimic operating improvement (Ch 5–9). |
| Profit and CFFO rise while capex spikes | Capitalized cost categories and FCF | Operating costs may have moved into assets and investing cash flow (Ch 6, 11). |
| CFFO jumps while receivables are sold | CFFO excluding factoring; recourse; future collections | Future receipts may have been pulled into today (Ch 10). |
| CFFO jumps while payables rise | Days payable and supplier terms | Payment delay has a ceiling and may reverse (Ch 12). |
| DSO falls after factoring | DSO with sold receivables restored | The denominator changed; customers may not pay faster (Ch 14). |
| EBITDA or adjusted profit dominates discussion | GAAP-to-adjusted bridge and recurring exclusions | Neither metric automatically measures cash or sustainable income (Ch 13, 17). |
| Acquirer shows rapid growth | Same-business pro forma sales, target stub-period data | Scope and closing date can create apparent growth (Ch 15, 17). |
| Acquirer shows strong CFFO | CFFO less required capex and acquisition spending | Purchased customers and assets produce inherited operating cash (Ch 16). |

## Escalation Rule
1. **One anomaly** → record the date, size, and alternative explanations.
2. **Cross-statement corroboration** → rebuild the relevant metric and inspect footnotes or transaction terms.
3. **Repeated changes plus incentives** → raise concern, quantify uncertainty, and seek independent evidence.
4. **No supporting disclosure** → label the calculation unavailable; do not convert suspicion into a fraud finding.

## Fast Tells
- Ask **“why?” and “why now?”** whenever a policy, metric, reserve, or disclosure location changes.
- A real current cash inflow can still be **nonrepeatable**.
- A GAAP-compliant number can still be **poor for forecasting**.
- Several modest warnings can matter more than one dramatic but isolated ratio.
