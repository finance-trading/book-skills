# Glossary

**Accounting change** — A change in principle, estimate, or reporting treatment; isolate its effect before comparing periods (Ch 3, 7).
**Adjusted earnings** — Reported earnings after selected items are removed; meaningful only with a stated definition (Ch 3, 5).
**Audit opinion** — Auditor’s conclusion within the scope of an audit, not assurance of investment quality or fraud absence (Ch 9).
**Backlog** — Committed future sales or orders; not equivalent to recognized or collectible revenue (Ch 6).
**Balance sheet** — Dated statement of recognized assets, liabilities, and equity (Ch 2).
**Big bath** — Concentrating losses or write-offs in one period, potentially making later results look stronger (Ch 1).
**Book value** — Accounting equity; not necessarily market or liquidation value (Ch 2).
**Capital expenditure** — Cash investment in long-lived assets, generally classified as investing cash flow (Ch 4, 8).
**Capitalization** — Recording a cost as an asset and recognizing expense over time (Ch 7).
**Cash-flow statement** — Reconciliation of cash movement among operating, investing, and financing activities (Ch 4).
**Collectibility** — Likelihood that consideration will be received; central to revenue quality (Ch 6).
**Common-size statement** — Statement with lines expressed as percentages of a base such as sales or assets (Ch 3).
**Contingent consideration** — Deal payment dependent on future conditions and requiring measurement judgment (Ch 10).
**Coverage ratio** — Earnings or cash relative to interest, fixed charges, or debt service; definitions vary (Ch 13).
**Current ratio** — Current assets divided by current liabilities; interpret with asset quality and seasonality (Ch 13).
**Depreciation** — Allocation of a long-lived asset’s cost over an assumed useful life (Ch 5, 7).
**Disclosure** — Information in statements, notes, or filings needed to understand policies, estimates, and risks (Ch 9).
**Diversification discount** — Market valuation effect that may penalize a diversified firm relative to focused alternatives (Ch 1).
**EBIT** — Earnings before interest and taxes (Ch 5, 8).
**EBITDA** — Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization; not cash available to owners (Ch 8).
**Earnings quality** — Degree to which reported earnings are recurring, measurable, and supported by cash (Ch 5–7).
**Economic profit** — Performance after charging for capital employed; distinct from accounting profit (Ch 5).
**EPS** — Earnings per share; depends on both earnings and the share count (Ch 3, 14).
**Fixed-charge coverage** — Measure of capacity to meet contractual fixed obligations; state inclusions explicitly (Ch 13).
**Forecast** — Conditional projection based on explicit economic, competitive, and management assumptions (Ch 12).
**Free cash flow** — Derived cash measure, commonly operating cash flow less capital expenditure; define it each time (Ch 4, 8).
**Goodwill** — Acquisition residual recognized as an asset and tested for impairment (Ch 2, 10).
**Gross margin** — Revenue less direct cost of sales, divided by revenue (Ch 3, 12).
**Internal control** — Processes supporting reliable reporting; deficiencies reduce confidence (Ch 9).
**Leverage** — Use of debt or other fixed claims relative to earnings, assets, or equity (Ch 13).
**Materiality** — Significance of information to a reasonable user’s decision (Ch 9).
**Off-balance-sheet exposure** — Economically relevant obligation or risk not shown as conventional debt (Ch 2, 13).
**Operating cash flow** — Cash classified as generated by operations; affected by working capital and classification (Ch 4).
**Operating margin** — Operating income divided by revenue (Ch 3).
**P/E multiple** — Price divided by earnings; comparable only after adjusting for quality, growth, and risk (Ch 14).
**Principal versus agent** — Distinction determining whether revenue is reported gross or net (Ch 6).
**Provision** — Estimated liability for an uncertain obligation (Ch 7).
**Purchase accounting** — Allocation of acquisition consideration to identifiable items, with residual goodwill (Ch 10).
**Red flag** — Anomaly warranting investigation, not proof of fraud (Ch 11).
**Related-party transaction** — Transaction with a connected party that may not occur on ordinary terms (Ch 11).
**Required return** — Return demanded for time and risk in valuation (Ch 14).
**Restatement** — Revision of previously reported financial information (Ch 11).
**Restructuring charge** — Cost associated with changing operations; test whether it is truly nonrecurring (Ch 7).
**Revenue recognition** — Determination of when and how much revenue is reported (Ch 6).
**Sensitivity analysis** — Assessment of how outcomes change when assumptions vary (Ch 12, 14).
**Synergy** — Incremental benefit expected from combining businesses; requires realized evidence (Ch 10).
**Terminal value** — Value assigned beyond an explicit forecast horizon (Ch 14).
**Working capital** — Short-term operating assets and liabilities; changes can absorb or release cash (Ch 4, 12).
