# Decision Cheatsheet

| If you observe… | Do this | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth growth or a persuasive story | Test incentives, market limits, cash conversion, and subsequent results | Reporting can influence capital cost and valuation (Ch 1). |
| Rising book value or goodwill | Inspect measurement, claims, and recoverability | Accounting equity is not realizable value (Ch 2, 10). |
| Margin or EPS change | Common-size results and reconcile shares and unusual items | Composition and dilution can mimic improvement (Ch 3). |
| Earnings diverge from cash | Bridge working capital and classifications | Profit and liquidity answer different questions (Ch 4). |
| A new “adjusted” profit | Rebuild it and test whether exclusions recur | Labels do not establish economic earnings (Ch 5, 7). |
| Revenue outpaces collections | Check terms, returns, concentration, and receivables | Timing and collectibility drive revenue quality (Ch 6). |
| Repeated special charges | Reclassify them into recurring costs | Frequency defeats the nonrecurring label (Ch 7). |
| EBITDA used for leverage or value | Deduct capex, working capital, taxes, and debt service | EBITDA omits real cash obligations (Ch 8, 13). |
| Complex estimates or auditor change | Read primary disclosures and lower confidence where gaps remain | Audit scope is limited (Ch 9). |
| Post-acquisition EPS growth | Separate financing, accounting, and realized synergies | Accretion need not create value (Ch 10). |
| Several reporting anomalies | Test benign alternatives before escalating concern | A flag prompts investigation, not a verdict (Ch 11). |
| A forecast or valuation | Link statements, stress key drivers, and show sensitivity | Future assumptions dominate credit and equity outcomes (Ch 12–14). |

## Compact calculations

- Common-size line = line item / sales (or total assets for balance-sheet composition).
- Gross margin = (sales − cost of sales) / sales.
- Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities.
- Receivables intensity: compare receivables growth with sales growth; investigate a sustained gap.
- Basic cash bridge: operating cash flow − capex − other required cash commitments. State every omission.
- Fixed-charge coverage: available earnings or cash / stated fixed charges. Include interest and relevant lease or principal obligations explicitly.
- Growth: future amount = current amount × (1 + growth rate) ^ periods. Do not extend a high rate without a capacity constraint.
- Terminal value requires a consistent earnings or cash measure, growth rate, and discount rate; test small changes because they can dominate equity value.

## Stop rules

1. Define every profit, cash-flow, debt, and coverage measure before comparing it.
2. Reconcile income, balance sheet, and cash flow before relying on one ratio.
3. Include current maturities and economically relevant off-balance-sheet claims in leverage work.
4. Stop adding ratios when they no longer change the repayment or valuation judgment.
5. Treat fraud as unproven until evidence discriminates it from error or acceptable judgment; reflect unresolved risk through confidence, forecasts, and scenarios.
