# Patterns

## Incentive–Claim–Evidence Review
**When to use**: Management promotes growth, margin improvement, or a strategic transformation.
**How**: Identify the incentive, translate the claim into expected statement effects, and compare those effects with filings, notes, cash flow, and subsequent periods.
**Trade-offs**: Finds inconsistencies but cannot establish intent from incentives alone.

## Three-Statement Reconciliation
**When to use**: Any material performance or liquidity conclusion.
**How**: Reconcile income with balance-sheet movements and cash-flow classifications. Explain working capital, noncash items, investment, and financing.
**Trade-offs**: Requires complete statements and judgment about classification; it prevents isolated-metric errors.

## Common-Size and Peer Comparison
**When to use**: Comparing periods, business models, or companies of different scale.
**How**: Express lines as percentages of sales or assets, then test differences for mix, accounting policy, and period alignment.
**Trade-offs**: Improves comparability but can conceal absolute scale, cyclicality, or one-time items if used alone.

## Revenue Quality Probe
**When to use**: Revenue growth is central to valuation or credit capacity.
**How**: Examine contract terms, timing, collectibility, returns, customer concentration, receivables, and cash conversion.
**Trade-offs**: Public evidence may be incomplete; unresolved gaps should widen uncertainty rather than force a fraud conclusion.

## Expense and Capitalization Audit
**When to use**: Margins improve or unusual charges recur.
**How**: Reconstruct costs shifted to assets or labeled nonrecurring, review lives and assumptions, and compare cash investment and impairments.
**Trade-offs**: Accounting deferral can be appropriate, so test economic consumption rather than rejecting capitalization automatically.

## EBITDA-to-Cash Bridge
**When to use**: EBITDA is used for leverage, valuation, or peer comparison.
**How**: Start with defined EBITDA and deduct replacement capex, working-capital needs, taxes, interest, principal, and other material commitments.
**Trade-offs**: Estimates of maintenance versus growth capex are judgmental, but the bridge exposes cash demands EBITDA omits.

## Disclosure and Audit Reliability Check
**When to use**: A conclusion depends on estimates, controls, or complex obligations.
**How**: Read the relevant notes, assess estimate sensitivity, review auditor changes and control findings, and compare narratives with primary filings.
**Trade-offs**: An audit adds assurance within scope but does not eliminate judgment or guarantee fraud detection.

## Acquisition Accounting Bridge
**When to use**: Results follow a merger or acquisition.
**How**: Separate consideration, financing, acquired earnings, amortization, goodwill, integration costs, and realized synergies from underlying operations.
**Trade-offs**: Limited segment disclosure may require explicit assumptions; EPS accretion can mislead without return-on-capital analysis.

## Fraud-Flag Triage
**When to use**: Several accounting anomalies appear together.
**How**: List independent flags, formulate benign alternatives, identify discriminating evidence, and update the risk assessment as filings arrive.
**Trade-offs**: Screening can prioritize work but produces false positives; it does not prove misconduct.

## Driver-Based Forecast
**When to use**: Building projections for credit or equity analysis.
**How**: Forecast sales from suitable industry or company drivers, then link margins, costs, working capital, investment, financing, and cash. Record and stress key assumptions.
**Trade-offs**: More transparent than trend extrapolation, but forecast quality depends on evidence and scenario design.

## Credit Capacity Assessment
**When to use**: Evaluating repayment risk.
**How**: Combine business stability, liquidity, leverage, coverage, maturities, contractual claims, and downside cash flow. Estimate recovery only after claim priority.
**Trade-offs**: Ratios aid comparison, but inconsistent definitions and omitted obligations can distort risk.

## Equity Valuation Consistency Check
**When to use**: Comparing an equity value or market multiple with fundamentals.
**How**: Use a defined earnings or cash-flow measure, connect growth to reinvestment and returns, set a risk-consistent discount rate, and test terminal-value sensitivity.
**Trade-offs**: Precision declines as terminal assumptions dominate; presenting ranges is more informative than a false point estimate.
