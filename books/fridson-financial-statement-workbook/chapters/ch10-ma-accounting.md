# Chapter 10: Mergers-and-Acquisitions Accounting

## Core Idea
Acquisitions create accounting estimates, integration risks, and incentives that can make post-deal results hard to compare. Separate genuine operating improvement from purchase accounting, transaction effects, and reclassification.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Deal-to-results bridge**: Identify purchase consideration, assets and liabilities recognized, goodwill, financing, transaction costs, and subsequent amortization or impairment. Reconcile reported post-deal profit to the combined operating economics.
- **Acquisition-quality review**: Compare the stated strategic rationale and expected synergies with segment results, cash flows, integration spending, and the target’s results after closing.
- **Post-deal red-flag check**: Review acquired intangibles, restructuring reserves, contingent consideration, related-party transactions, and unusual acquired-entity performance.

## Key Concepts
- **Purchase accounting**: Allocation of consideration to recognized assets and liabilities, with any residual recorded as goodwill.
- **Goodwill impairment**: Recognition that an acquired reporting unit’s carrying amount may not be recoverable under applicable standards.
- **Synergy**: Incremental benefit expected from combining businesses; it must be evidenced in realized cost, revenue, or cash-flow outcomes.
- **Contingent consideration**: Payment dependent on future conditions, requiring careful measurement and disclosure.

## Mental Models
- Deal accretion is not synonymous with value creation; financing and share issuance can change EPS mechanically.
- Follow transaction claims into cash and later segment performance.
- Acquisition accounting can change the comparison baseline without changing economics.

## Anti-patterns
- Assuming a higher post-deal EPS proves that the acquisition benefited existing owners.
- Accepting vague synergy targets without a measurable baseline and timeline.

## Worked Example
A buyer claims an acquisition will be EPS-accretive. Reconstruct pre-deal EPS, financing cost, new shares, acquired earnings, amortization, and one-time transaction charges. Then compare realized cash generation and integration costs with the synergy plan. EPS may rise even if return on invested capital falls; judge the deal on durable economics, not accretion alone.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reconcile purchase accounting and financing effects.
2. Test synergies against a baseline and realized cash flows.
3. Monitor acquired-segment performance and impairment assumptions.
4. Compare per-share outcomes with total capital invested.
