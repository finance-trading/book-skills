# Chapter 5: What Is Profit?

## Core Idea
“Profit” is not one unambiguous quantity. Accounting rules, classification choices, and economic substance produce different numbers. The analyst must say which profit is being used, what it includes, and why that version matters.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Profit taxonomy**: Separate net income, operating income, EBIT, EBITDA, adjusted earnings, and cash-based measures. Define exclusions such as depreciation, amortization, interest, taxes, unusual items, and accounting changes.
- **Measure-purpose test**: Choose the profit metric that matches the decision. Use operating and recurring measures for underlying business performance; use cash-adjusted measures for capacity to fund obligations; use per-share or return measures only with their denominators and dilution effects.

## Key Concepts
- **EBIT**: Earnings before interest and taxes; removes financing and tax, not depreciation.
- **Depreciation and amortization**: Allocation of past costs, not current cash expenditure; economic replacement can differ.
- **Adjusted profit**: Reported profit with selected items removed; the definition and repeatability of adjustments must be disclosed or reconstructed.
- **Economic profit**: Performance after a charge for capital; not the same as accounting profit.

## Mental Models
- Each profit label changes which costs are counted and what claim the number supports.
- A higher “profit” figure is not automatically a better signal.
- Ask what the excluded items will cost again in the future.

## Anti-patterns
- Comparing EBITDA from one company with net income from another.
- Treating adjusted figures as recurring when the adjustment repeats.

## Worked Example
A company presents EBITDA as its headline result while net income is weak. Rebuild EBITDA, then subtract replacement capex, working-capital cash needs, and fixed charges. Compare that result with reported operating income and peers using the same measure. If the gap depends on repeated charges or underfunded asset replacement, discount the headline EBITDA.

## Key Takeaways
1. State the exact profit definition before using it.
2. Match the profit measure to the analytical question.
3. Reconstruct “adjusted” figures from the statements.
4. Test whether excluded costs recur.
