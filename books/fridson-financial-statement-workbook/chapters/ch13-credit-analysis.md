# Chapter 13: Credit Analysis

## Core Idea
Credit analysis asks whether a borrower can meet obligations in amount and timing under realistic downside conditions. Profits, liquidity, leverage, coverage, and cash sources matter, but only as inputs to a repayment judgment.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Capacity sequence**: Assess business stability and cash generation, liquidity, leverage, fixed-charge coverage, debt maturity, and recovery prospects. Include current maturities, leases, guarantees, and other contractual claims where economically relevant.
- **Ratio-purpose rule**: Define each ratio consistently and use it only where it addresses repayment risk. Compare peers and trends, but recognize that higher returns may accompany higher risk and that exact formulas matter less than consistent interpretation.
- **Stress and recovery check**: Test lower operating results, higher interest rates, working-capital needs, and refinancing constraints. Estimate how senior claims and collateral affect recovery if repayment fails.

## Key Concepts
- **Current ratio**: Current assets divided by current liabilities; a liquidity indicator requiring asset-quality review.
- **Fixed-charge coverage**: A measure of earnings or cash available relative to fixed obligations; definitions vary and must be stated.
- **Leverage**: Debt or adjusted claims relative to earnings, assets, or equity; include economically relevant off-balance-sheet claims.
- **Cash-flow coverage**: Cash generation relative to debt service and required investment.

## Mental Models
- Repayment capacity, not reported profit, is the credit question.
- Ratios diagnose; they do not replace the business and liquidity story.
- A company may avoid showing some leverage as conventional debt while retaining the economic obligation.

## Anti-patterns
- Omitting current maturities, leases, guarantees, or pensions from the debt picture.
- Adding ratios after they cease to change the repayment conclusion.
- Assuming EBITDA coverage equals cash available for debt service.

## Worked Example
A borrower reports adequate EBITDA coverage but has large current debt maturities and a seasonal working-capital peak. Rebuild fixed charges with interest, principal, and lease obligations; test cash flow after capex and the working-capital swing. If coverage fails in the downside, the credit conclusion is weaker even if headline leverage looks acceptable.

## Key Takeaways
1. Focus on timely repayment and recovery.
2. Use consistent, economically complete ratios.
3. Include contractual claims beyond headline debt.
4. Stress cash flow, refinancing, and collateral assumptions.
