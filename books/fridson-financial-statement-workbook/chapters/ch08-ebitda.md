# Chapter 8: The Applications and Limitations of EBITDA

## Core Idea
EBITDA can help compare businesses with different financing, tax, and asset structures, but it omits capital consumption and cash needs. Its value depends on the use case and on reconstructing what was excluded.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Use-case test**: Ask whether the decision is operating comparison, credit capacity, leverage, or valuation. EBITDA is most useful for controlling for financing and tax differences; it is insufficient alone to establish cash available to shareholders or creditors.
- **EBITDA-to-cash bridge**: Begin with reported or reconstructed EBITDA, then deduct replacement capex, working-capital needs, interest, taxes, principal payments, dividends, and significant contractual commitments to approximate distributable cash under stated assumptions.

## Key Concepts
- **Capital expenditure**: Cash required for long-lived assets; omitted from EBITDA but essential to maintain or grow capacity.
- **Working-capital change**: Growth can consume cash independent of depreciation.
- **EBITDA-based leverage**: A ratio requiring a defined EBITDA numerator and debt denominator; it can mask asset replacement and cash-conversion differences.

## Mental Models
- “Before” interest and taxes does not mean “free of” capital structure or operating cash requirements.
- A high EBITDA margin may coexist with low free cash flow in capital-intensive businesses.

## Anti-patterns
- Presenting EBITDA as cash available to equity holders.
- Comparing a maintenance-capex business with a growth business without normalizing capex intensity.

## Worked Example
Two companies have similar EBITDA margins, but one’s capex-to-revenue is two and a half times the other’s. Build an EBITDA-to-cash bridge for each. If the high-capex firm must also fund growth and debt service, EBITDA overstates its cash capacity and understates leverage risk. The comparison should use the bridge, not EBITDA alone.

## Key Takeaways
1. State why EBITDA fits the decision.
2. Reconstruct excluded cash outflows.
3. Differentiate maintenance and growth capex.
4. Pair EBITDA ratios with liquidity and debt maturity checks.
