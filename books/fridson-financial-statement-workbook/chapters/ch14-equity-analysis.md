# Chapter 14: Equity Analysis

## Core Idea
Equity value depends on future residual cash flows or earnings, growth, risk, and dilution. Current statements and forecasts inform value, but market price is not a substitute for an independent analysis.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Equity value chain**: Forecast residual earnings or dividends, estimate growth and its duration, assess required return and risk, then compare the resulting value with the market price and its assumptions. State what part of the conclusion depends on uncertain growth.
- **Growth-quality test**: Separate growth from accounting changes, leverage, share count, and one-time items. Ask whether growth earns returns above its cost of capital and can be funded without excessive risk.
- **Valuation-consistency check**: Ensure the earnings measure, discount rate, growth rate, and terminal assumption are mutually consistent and grounded in the business.

## Key Concepts
- **Dividend discount model**: Value based on expected future dividends discounted at a required return.
- **P/E multiple**: Price relative to earnings; meaningful only when earnings quality, growth, and risk are comparable.
- **Required return**: Compensation demanded for time and risk; higher uncertainty raises it.
- **Terminal value**: Value beyond the explicit forecast period, often the largest and most assumption-sensitive component.

## Mental Models
- Growth can increase value only if the business earns an adequate return on the capital it consumes.
- A low multiple may reflect poor quality or high risk, not an opportunity.
- Dilution and debt service can reduce equity value even when enterprise results improve.

## Anti-patterns
- Extrapolating high growth indefinitely without a market or return constraint.
- Using a multiple from a dissimilar peer group without adjusting for quality, growth, and risk.
- Ignoring the sensitivity of terminal value to small changes in growth or discount rate.

## Worked Example
A company grows earnings at 15% and is expected to slow toward a mature multiple. Forecast earnings over the explicit period, discount terminal value at the required return, and test whether growth requires reinvestment that reduces free cash flow. Compare the resulting equity value with price. If value depends mainly on the terminal assumption, present the sensitivity rather than a single precise result.

## Key Takeaways
1. Value equity from future residual outcomes and risk.
2. Test growth for return, funding, and durability.
3. Keep valuation assumptions internally consistent.
4. Show sensitivity where terminal value dominates.
