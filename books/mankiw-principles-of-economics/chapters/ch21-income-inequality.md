# Chapter 21: Income Inequality and Poverty

## Core Idea
Income distribution is a policy concern, but a single year's cash income is an incomplete measure of well-being. Redistribution choices depend on both evidence and ethical criteria.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Measurement audit**: Compare annual income with lifetime income and consumption; account for life-cycle position, temporary shocks, in-kind transfers, tax credits, household size, and mobility before interpreting inequality.
- **Three ethical lenses**: Utilitarianism seeks greatest total utility; Rawls's liberal contractarian view asks what rules one would choose behind a veil of ignorance; libertarianism emphasizes just acquisition and exchange. Make the ethical premise visible rather than presenting it as a statistic.
- **Poverty-policy comparison**: Compare minimum wages, welfare transfers, negative income tax or earned-income credits, and in-kind benefits by targeting, work incentives, administration, and coverage.

## Key Concepts
- **Poverty rate**: Share of people with income below an official threshold.
- **In-kind transfer**: Benefit supplied as goods or services rather than cash.
- **Life-cycle variation**: Income changes as people age and work through careers.
- **Economic mobility**: Movement across income ranks over time.
- **Veil of ignorance**: Choice of social rules without knowing one's future position.

## Mental Models
- An inequality statistic is a measurement choice; ask what it excludes before using it for policy.
- Separate the moral case for redistribution from the empirical effect of a chosen program.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating one-year earnings as permanent welfare**: Students, retirees, and temporarily unemployed people may have different lifetime resources.
- **Calling every transfer costless**: Taxes and program rules change work and reporting incentives.

## Worked Example
Two households can report the same low cash income while receiving very different in-kind benefits, owning different assets, or occupying different life-cycle stages. Mankiw's measurement discussion asks the analyst to add those missing dimensions before ranking living standards. A Rawlsian may still prefer strong protection for the least advantaged, while a utilitarian or libertarian could evaluate the same transfer differently; the numerical adjustment and ethical judgment are distinct steps.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define the welfare measure and population before comparing inequality.
2. State the ethical criterion behind a redistribution recommendation.
3. Test program targeting and work incentives alongside benefits.
