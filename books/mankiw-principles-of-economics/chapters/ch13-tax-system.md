# Chapter 13: The Design of the Tax System

## Core Idea
Tax design balances revenue collection, efficiency, administrative burden, and equity. Average and marginal rates answer different questions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Efficiency ledger**: Count deadweight loss from changed behavior plus the time and resources spent complying and administering. These are costs beyond the transfer of tax receipts.
- **Tax-rate distinction**: Average tax rate is tax divided by income and measures the overall share paid. Marginal tax rate is the extra tax on another dollar of income and shapes work, saving, and reporting incentives.
- **Equity tests**: Horizontal equity asks whether similar taxpayers pay similarly; vertical equity asks how burdens differ across ability to pay. Compare benefits-received and ability-to-pay rationales explicitly.

## Key Concepts
- **Average tax rate**: Total tax divided by total income.
- **Marginal tax rate**: Additional tax divided by additional taxable income.
- **Lump-sum tax**: Fixed amount independent of a person's actions or income.
- **Progressive tax**: Average tax rate rises as income rises.
- **Administrative burden**: Real resources used to comply with and enforce taxes.

## Mental Models
- A tax can be simple and efficient yet unpopular on equity grounds; report both dimensions.
- Use marginal rates to predict behavior and average rates to describe overall burden.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using average rates to infer the incentive for an extra hour of work**: The marginal rate governs that decision.
- **Treating statutory rates as the whole cost**: Compliance and avoidance absorb resources.

## Worked Example
Compare a fixed lump-sum tax with an income tax that raises the same revenue. The fixed tax does not change the return to one more hour of work, so it causes less substitution away from work. Yet requiring the same dollar amount from a low-income and a high-income household can conflict sharply with ability-to-pay fairness. The example makes the efficiency–equity trade-off concrete rather than assuming one tax dominates on every criterion.

## Key Takeaways
1. State the revenue and equity goals before ranking taxes.
2. Use marginal rates for incentives and average rates for burden.
3. Include compliance costs in the efficiency calculation.
