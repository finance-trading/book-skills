# Chapter 20: Earnings and Discrimination

## Core Idea
Wage differences can reflect job conditions, human capital, ability, institutions, chance, and discrimination. A measured gap is evidence to investigate, not a complete causal explanation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Wage-gap decomposition**: Compare workers with similar education, experience, job characteristics, and hours; then inspect remaining differences with care about omitted variables and selection.
- **Human-capital versus signaling test**: Education may raise productivity, reveal preexisting ability, or do both. The social benefit of expanding education differs across these mechanisms.
- **Discrimination channels**: Distinguish employer taste, customer preference, and statistical inference under incomplete information. Competition can constrain some employer discrimination but does not eliminate all channels.

## Key Concepts
- **Compensating differential**: Wage difference offsetting nonwage job attributes.
- **Human capital**: Skills and knowledge that increase productivity.
- **Signaling**: Observable credential conveying information about unobserved ability.
- **Discrimination**: Different treatment based on group attributes rather than relevant productivity.
- **Efficiency wage**: Above-market wage paid to raise effort or reduce turnover.

## Mental Models
- Separate what determines an individual's wage from whether a gap across groups reveals unfair treatment.
- Ask whether a credential changes ability or simply changes employers' information.

## Anti-patterns
- **Attributing an entire raw wage gap to discrimination**: Job and experience differences may contribute.
- **Explaining away all gaps with measured controls**: Relevant unmeasured discrimination can remain.

## Worked Example
The chapter contrasts fast-food cooks, teachers, physicians, CEOs, and superstar musicians, showing wages far apart in one year. Begin with training, scarcity of skills, and job characteristics; then ask whether pay reflects a winner-take-most audience or barriers. The figures alone cannot identify a single mechanism. For a group comparison, hold observable job attributes constant, inspect residual gaps, and explicitly state what the data cannot resolve.

## Key Takeaways
1. Treat wages as outcomes of multiple interacting mechanisms.
2. Distinguish educational productivity from credential signaling.
3. Use adjusted comparisons without claiming they prove or disprove discrimination completely.
