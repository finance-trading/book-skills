# Chapter 19: The Markets for the Factors of Production

## Core Idea
Demand for labor, land, and capital is derived from the goods those inputs help produce. In competitive markets, firms hire an input until its value of marginal product matches its price.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Derived-demand chain**: Start with demand for a product, derive the firm's desired output, then its demand for inputs needed to produce that output. A product-price change alters the value of each factor's contribution.
- **Labor hiring rule**: Estimate the extra output from another worker (`MPL`), multiply by the product price to obtain `VMPL`, and hire while `VMPL ≥ wage`. Diminishing marginal product makes this schedule slope downward.
- **Joint-input interaction**: Additional capital can raise labor productivity and labor demand; a change in one factor's supply or price can therefore alter returns to others.

## Key Concepts
- **Factor of production**: Input such as labor, land, or capital.
- **Derived demand**: Demand for an input arising from demand for output.
- **Marginal product of labor**: Extra output from an additional worker.
- **Value of marginal product**: Product price times marginal product.
- **Labor–leisure trade-off**: Choice between earning wages and using time outside paid work.

## Mental Models
- Trace a wage shock through both labor supply and the value of what workers produce.
- A high factor price can reflect scarcity, productivity, or complementary inputs, not moral worth.

## Anti-patterns
- **Hiring on average revenue per worker**: Incremental hiring turns on marginal value.
- **Treating labor demand as independent of product demand**: A fall in software demand changes demand for programmers.

## Worked Example
Mankiw links programmer demand to software output and gas-station attendant demand to gasoline retailing. If software demand raises the product price, an additional programmer's same physical output becomes more valuable to the firm, shifting labor demand right. If equipment also helps programmers produce more, the marginal product rises further. Compare the resulting value of marginal product with wage to determine how many workers to hire.

## Key Takeaways
1. Derive factor demand from product value and marginal productivity.
2. Compare `P × MPL` with wage at the hiring margin.
3. Check how complementary factors alter productivity.
