# Chapter 24: Measuring a Nation's Income

## Core Idea
GDP measures the market value of final goods and services produced within a country during a period. Every final sale is both someone's spending and someone's income, so the two economy-wide totals match.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Expenditure identity**: Record GDP as `Y = C + I + G + NX`, classifying household consumption, investment (including inventories and new housing), government purchases, and exports minus imports.
- **Final-output boundary**: Count domestic production in the period, not resale of used goods or purely financial trades. Avoid double counting intermediate inputs already embodied in final products.
- **Real-versus-nominal separation**: Nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP uses base-year prices to isolate quantity. The GDP deflator is `100 × nominal GDP / real GDP`.

## Key Concepts
- **GDP**: Market value of final production within national borders over a period.
- **Consumption**: Household spending on goods and services other than new housing.
- **Investment**: Spending on capital, inventories, and new housing.
- **Net exports**: Exports less imports.
- **GDP deflator**: Price index implied by nominal divided by real GDP.

## Mental Models
- If an item is counted as an intermediate input, its value is already in the final good; classify once.
- GDP is a production measure, not a full scorecard of leisure, environment, distribution, or nonmarket work.

## Anti-patterns
- **Counting a used home sale as current construction**: Only current services such as brokerage fees enter current GDP.
- **Calling nominal growth real growth**: Price increases can raise nominal GDP without more output.

## Worked Example
Suppose a mill sells steel to a carmaker and the carmaker sells the finished car. Counting both the full steel sale and the full car price would count the steel twice. Count the final car price, or sum each firm's value added. If the car is made domestically and exported, it enters exports; an imported car purchased domestically raises consumption and imports by equal amounts in `C + I + G + NX`, leaving domestic GDP unaffected.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define period, border, and final production before totaling output.
2. Classify expenditure components and offset imports.
3. Use real GDP for quantity comparisons and note its welfare limits.
