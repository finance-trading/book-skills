# Chapter 23: Frontiers of Microeconomics

## Core Idea
Information gaps, political incentives, and cognitive limits complicate the simple competitive model. Diagnose these mechanisms before assuming either markets or government will automatically correct them.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Asymmetric-information split**: Hidden actions after a contract create moral hazard; hidden characteristics before a contract create adverse selection. Signaling and screening can reduce information gaps, but require credible, incentive-compatible evidence.
- **Political-economy check**: Majority preferences can cycle, and the median voter can dominate a simple majority contest. Examine officials' incentives and voting rules when a market remedy depends on government action.
- **Behavioral-economics audit**: Test for present bias, overconfidence, or other systematic departures from the fully rational model; compare the benefits and limits of corrective defaults or disclosures.

## Key Concepts
- **Principal**: Party delegating a task or bearing an agent's outcome.
- **Agent**: Party acting on behalf of a principal.
- **Moral hazard**: Hidden action after agreement changes risk or effort.
- **Adverse selection**: Hidden type changes who enters a transaction.
- **Median-voter theorem**: Under restrictive conditions, a majority contest tends toward the median voter's preferred policy.

## Mental Models
- Ask whether the unknown is a person's type or future behavior; the remedy differs.
- A policymaker can have limited information and self-interested incentives too.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating any insurance problem as one generic information failure**: Selection before coverage and behavior after coverage require different responses.
- **Assuming majority voting reveals one consistent social preference**: Preferences can cycle across choices.

## Worked Example
In a used-car sale, a seller may know vehicle quality better than a buyer. If buyers discount all cars because they cannot identify lemons, owners of better cars may withdraw, worsening the pool: adverse selection. In insurance, a covered driver's reduced care after signing is moral hazard. The first problem calls for credible quality signals or screening; the second for contract incentives such as deductibles and monitoring.

## Key Takeaways
1. Locate the information asymmetry in time and actor.
2. Evaluate policy implementation through political and administrative incentives.
3. Use behavioral findings as testable mechanisms, not a blanket claim that people are irrational.
