# Chapter 29: Unemployment

## Core Idea
Unemployment combines job-search friction and wage-setting forces with fluctuations around a long-run natural rate. The headline rate is a useful but incomplete measure of joblessness.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Labor-force measurement**: Classify employed, unemployed actively seeking work, and people outside the labor force; calculate unemployment as unemployed divided by the labor force. Check participation and discouraged workers alongside the headline rate.
- **Frictional versus structural diagnosis**: Search and matching take time even in a healthy market. Wages held above clearing levels by minimum wages, unions, or efficiency-wage strategies can produce a different persistent gap between labor supplied and demanded.
- **Policy trade-off**: Unemployment insurance protects income during search but can lengthen search; job information and training may improve matching. Evaluate both insurance value and behavioral response.

## Key Concepts
- **Labor force**: Employed people plus unemployed people seeking work.
- **Natural rate of unemployment**: Normal long-run level around which actual unemployment fluctuates.
- **Cyclical unemployment**: Short-run deviation associated with economic fluctuations.
- **Frictional unemployment**: Joblessness arising from time needed to find a suitable match.
- **Efficiency wage**: Above-clearing pay used to improve worker effort or retention.

## Mental Models
- A fall in unemployment caused by people leaving the labor force is different from one caused by hiring.
- Some search time can improve matching, so eliminating every unemployment spell is not the objective.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reading a single unemployment rate as all unused labor**: Discouraged workers and involuntary part-time workers can be missed.
- **Assuming every persistent spell is search friction**: Wage-setting institutions can also matter.

## Worked Example
An unemployed worker may receive insurance while searching for a better match. Benefits soften a sudden income loss, but also reduce the immediate cost of rejecting a weak offer, potentially lengthening unemployment. Compare the quality of the eventual match and the length and fiscal cost of search; a longer spell is not by itself proof that the program fails.

## Key Takeaways
1. Read participation and duration with the unemployment rate.
2. Distinguish matching friction from wages above market-clearing levels.
3. Assess income protection and incentive effects together.
