# Chapter 33: A Macroeconomic Theory of the Open Economy

## Core Idea
The real interest rate coordinates national saving with domestic investment and net capital outflow; the real exchange rate coordinates currency supply from capital outflow with demand from net exports.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two-market sequence**: Begin with loanable funds `S = I + NCO`; find the real-rate effect of a policy. Translate the new `NCO` into the supply of domestic currency in the foreign-exchange market, then infer the real exchange-rate and net-export response.
- **Budget-deficit chain**: Lower public saving shifts funds supply left, raises the real interest rate, reduces net capital outflow, reduces domestic-currency supply abroad, appreciates the currency, and lowers net exports, all else equal.
- **Trade-policy test**: An import restriction can alter the composition of trade, but in the baseline model the exchange-rate response offsets its direct effect on aggregate net exports; do not equate import reduction with a smaller trade deficit automatically.

## Key Concepts
- **Loanable funds**: Saving available to finance domestic investment or foreign asset acquisition.
- **Net capital outflow**: Link between the funds market and currency market.
- **Real appreciation**: Rise in domestic goods' price relative to foreign goods.
- **Net exports**: Foreign demand for domestic output minus domestic demand for foreign output.
- **Capital flight**: Sudden rise in demand to move assets abroad.

## Mental Models
- Trace shocks through both connected markets; a direct trade effect may be offset by exchange-rate adjustment.
- Separate the accounting identity from the behavioral model used to predict the direction of change.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming tariffs mechanically close a trade deficit**: The currency can appreciate and reduce exports.
- **Skipping the interest-rate step**: Capital flows respond to returns as well as trade restrictions.

## Worked Example
For a larger budget deficit, public saving falls. Loanable funds become scarcer, the real interest rate rises, and fewer domestic savers acquire foreign assets. With less net capital outflow, fewer dollars are supplied to the foreign-exchange market. The dollar appreciates in real terms; foreign buyers find U.S. goods relatively expensive and U.S. net exports fall. The chain is conditional on other curves remaining stable.

## Key Takeaways
1. Analyze the loanable-funds market before the currency market.
2. Link them through net capital outflow.
3. Recheck exchange-rate feedback before asserting a trade policy's net-export effect.
