# Chapter 9: Application — International Trade

## Core Idea
Compare the domestic no-trade price with the world price to predict whether a country imports or exports. Opening trade raises total surplus, while the gains and losses fall on different domestic groups.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **World-price test**: If the world price is below the domestic equilibrium, imports enter and domestic buyers gain while producers lose; if above, exports leave and domestic producers gain while buyers lose. In both cases, compare gain and loss areas rather than only counting affected groups.
- **Tariff and quota analysis**: A tariff raises the domestic price above the world price and reduces imports; a quota limits imports directly. Both create transfers and deadweight losses. Identify who receives tariff revenue or quota rents.
- **Trade-restriction claims audit**: Separate evidence on jobs, national security, infant industry, unfair competition, and bargaining power. Even where a goal is valid, compare targeted alternatives and costs.

## Key Concepts
- **World price**: Price prevailing in world markets.
- **Tariff**: Tax on imports.
- **Import quota**: Legal limit on quantity imported.
- **Comparative advantage**: Lower opportunity cost of production.
- **Quota rent**: Gain from the right to import at a lower world price and sell at a higher domestic price.

## Mental Models
- Trade changes the division of gains within a country and can still increase their total.
- When evaluating protection, count higher prices to consumers as well as benefits to protected producers.

## Anti-patterns
- **Only counting domestic producer jobs**: It omits consumers and sectors that use the protected good as an input.
- **Treating all imports as national loss**: Imports are goods received in exchange for exports or assets.

## Worked Example
In Mankiw's world-price diagram, a country whose domestic price exceeds the world price imports the good. Domestic supply contracts at the lower price, demand expands, and the difference arrives from abroad. A tariff raises the domestic price, recovers part of producer surplus and government revenue, but blocks some units that buyers value above the world cost. The welfare ledger exposes the gain, transfer, and lost-trade regions separately.

## Key Takeaways
1. Predict trade direction from prices before judging policy.
2. Compare total surplus and its distribution separately.
3. Identify who receives quota rents or tariff revenue.
