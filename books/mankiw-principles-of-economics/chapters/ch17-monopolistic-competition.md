# Chapter 17: Monopolistic Competition

## Core Idea
Many firms can enter, yet differentiated products give each a downward-sloping demand curve. Long-run entry erodes economic profit while leaving markups and excess capacity.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three attributes**: Check for many sellers, product differentiation, and free entry or exit before applying the monopolistic-competition model.
- **Short-run to long-run adjustment**: A firm chooses `MR = MC` and a price on its demand curve. Profit invites similar products; entry shifts each incumbent's demand until economic profit is zero, with price equal to average total cost but still above marginal cost.
- **Advertising evaluation**: Distinguish information about products and quality from persuasive claims or barriers. Advertising and branding can signal quality but may also influence tastes without improving the product.

## Key Concepts
- **Differentiation**: Products are imperfect substitutes in buyers' eyes.
- **Excess capacity**: Output below the scale minimizing average total cost.
- **Markup**: Price above marginal cost.
- **Free entry**: New firms can enter when profits attract them.
- **Brand signal**: Reputation or advertising that may convey quality information.

## Mental Models
- Entry can remove excess profit without removing each firm's limited pricing power.
- More varieties may benefit consumers, but a markup can exclude some beneficial marginal sales.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming many sellers means perfect competition**: Differentiation makes each firm's demand downward sloping.
- **Assuming all advertising is waste**: Some advertising reduces search cost or signals reliability.

## Worked Example
Book publishing has many authors and open entry, but each title is distinct. An author cannot sell an unlimited number at any chosen price; her title has a downward-sloping demand curve. A profitable genre draws competing titles, reducing each title's demand. Long-run economic profit can approach zero even while the publisher charges more than marginal printing and distribution cost.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose structure by differentiation and entry, not seller count alone.
2. Keep zero long-run profit separate from zero markup.
3. Evaluate advertising by its information and incentive effects.
