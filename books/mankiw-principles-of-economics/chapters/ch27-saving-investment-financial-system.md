# Chapter 27: Saving, Investment, and the Financial System

## Core Idea
Financial institutions connect savers with investors. In a closed economy, accounting requires national saving to equal domestic investment; the real interest rate helps coordinate the two.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **National-saving identity**: From `Y = C + I + G`, derive `S = Y − C − G = I` for a closed economy. Split private saving as `Y − T − C` and public saving as `T − G`; a budget deficit makes public saving negative.
- **Loanable-funds model**: Saving supplies funds, investment demands them, and the real interest rate equilibrates the market. For a policy shock, identify which curve moves and how equilibrium saving, investment, and rate change.
- **Crowding-out channel**: A larger government deficit reduces public saving and shifts funds supply left, tending to raise the real interest rate and reduce private investment, holding other forces constant.

## Key Concepts
- **Financial system**: Institutions matching saving with borrowing and investment.
- **Financial intermediary**: Institution such as a bank that channels funds between parties.
- **National saving**: Income left after consumption and government purchases.
- **Budget deficit**: Government spending exceeding tax revenue.
- **Crowding out**: Reduced private investment associated with government borrowing.

## Mental Models
- The accounting identity `S = I` is always true for the defined closed economy; the interest rate and other adjustments make planned decisions consistent.
- Government borrowing competes for funds, but the size of crowding out depends on saving and investment responses.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling a stock trade new investment automatically**: Buying an existing share transfers ownership; real investment is new capital formation.
- **Applying closed-economy `S = I` unmodified to an open economy**: Foreign capital flows create a difference.

## Worked Example
Suppose a closed-economy government increases spending without raising taxes. Public saving `T − G` falls. In the loanable-funds diagram, the supply of funds shifts left, so the real interest rate rises and fewer private projects meet the financing cost. The national accounting identity still holds: after adjustment, the lower saving equals lower domestic investment.

## Key Takeaways
1. Derive identities before drawing policy conclusions.
2. Treat the real interest rate as the coordinating price for funds.
3. Separate financial asset transfers from construction of new capital.
