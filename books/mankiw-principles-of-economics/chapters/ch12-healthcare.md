# Chapter 12: The Economics of Healthcare

## Core Idea
Healthcare combines insurance against unpredictable expense with externalities, quality information problems, and equity concerns. Each problem requires its own diagnosis before choosing policy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Insurance trade-off**: Pool costly uncertain events to reduce individual risk, then account for moral hazard after coverage and adverse selection before coverage. Deductibles, coverage rules, and mandates alter these incentives.
- **Healthcare-market diagnosis**: Identify vaccination or research spillovers, physician–patient information asymmetry, and access goals separately. A single intervention may address one while worsening another.
- **System comparison**: Compare spending, outcomes, coverage, and access under consistent definitions and periods; do not infer a causal policy ranking from one aggregate statistic.

## Key Concepts
- **Moral hazard**: Insured users have less incentive to restrain use or risk after coverage.
- **Adverse selection**: People with higher expected costs are more likely to seek coverage at a pooled price.
- **Premium**: Price paid for insurance coverage.
- **Deductible**: Amount paid by the insured before coverage begins.
- **Externality**: Benefit or cost to others beyond patient and provider.

## Mental Models
- Separate financial risk protection from the cost and quantity of care delivered.
- Ask which friction a rule addresses, then check what new incentives it creates.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating every medical visit as an ordinary retail purchase**: Buyers often cannot assess quality or need, and costs can be catastrophic.
- **Treating insurance expansion as costless**: More coverage changes use, premiums, taxes, and selection.

## Worked Example
Vaccination protects the patient and lowers infection risk for others. An unassisted private choice can therefore produce fewer vaccinations than the social optimum. Insurance can help the patient afford the dose, but coverage design also shapes how much care is demanded. Analyze the spillover, access, and insurance effects as separate channels before choosing a subsidy, mandate, or coverage rule.

## Key Takeaways
1. Name the specific failure or equity goal for each healthcare policy.
2. Evaluate insurance with both risk pooling and incentive effects.
3. Compare systems using multiple outcomes and consistent populations.
