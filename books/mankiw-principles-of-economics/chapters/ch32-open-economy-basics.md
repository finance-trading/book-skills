# Chapter 32: Open-Economy Macroeconomics — Basic Concepts

## Core Idea
International transactions connect trade in goods with trade in assets. Accounting requires net exports to equal net capital outflow, and exchange rates compare currencies and goods across countries.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Open-economy identities**: Define `NX = exports − imports` and `NCO = domestic purchases of foreign assets − foreign purchases of domestic assets`. By transaction accounting, `NX = NCO`; national saving satisfies `S = I + NCO`.
- **Real exchange rate**: Convert the nominal exchange rate into the relative price of domestic and foreign goods, using a consistent currency convention. A higher real value of domestic goods tends to reduce net exports.
- **Purchasing-power parity (PPP)**: In a frictionless benchmark, a unit of currency should buy similar baskets across countries after conversion. Transport costs, nontraded goods, and product differences limit its short-run accuracy.

## Key Concepts
- **Open economy**: Economy trading goods and assets internationally.
- **Net exports**: Exports minus imports.
- **Net capital outflow**: Net domestic acquisition of foreign assets.
- **Nominal exchange rate**: Relative price of two currencies.
- **Real exchange rate**: Relative price of goods across countries after currency conversion.

## Mental Models
- A trade surplus is paired with net lending or asset acquisition abroad; a deficit is paired with net foreign financing.
- Always state whether an exchange-rate quote is foreign currency per domestic unit or the reverse.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using closed-economy `S = I` unchanged**: Saving can finance investment abroad.
- **Treating PPP as an exact daily trading rule**: Nontraded services and frictions matter.

## Worked Example
When a U.S. resident buys a foreign bond, dollars flow abroad to purchase an asset: this is capital outflow. A foreign seller can eventually use those dollars to buy U.S. goods, hold a U.S. asset, or transfer them to someone who does. The flows demonstrate why a nation's net exports and net capital outflow are linked even when the two transactions occur through different counterparties.

## Key Takeaways
1. Write the trade and capital-flow identities before interpreting a deficit.
2. Convert currencies consistently when evaluating goods prices.
3. Use PPP as a long-run benchmark with explicit limitations.
