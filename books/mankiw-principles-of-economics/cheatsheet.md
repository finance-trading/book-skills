# Decision Cheatsheet

| If you observe... | Do this | Because / limit |
|---|---|---|
| A proposed choice appears free | Name the best forgone alternative and compare the next unit's benefit and cost | Opportunity cost includes implicit uses of time and resources (Ch 1, 14) |
| One producer is faster at everything | Compute each side's opportunity cost in the other good | Comparative, not absolute, advantage governs gains from specialization (Ch 3) |
| A market price changes | Classify demand/supply shift before drawing the new equilibrium | Own-price movement alone does not shift a curve (Ch 4) |
| Price rises and seller revenue changes | Estimate demand elasticity over the relevant horizon | Below 1, revenue tends to rise; above 1, it tends to fall locally (Ch 5) |
| A price ceiling or floor is proposed | Compare it with equilibrium price | Only a binding ceiling creates shortage; a binding floor creates surplus (Ch 6) |
| A tax is assigned legally to one party | Compare supply and demand elasticities | The less elastic side usually bears more economic burden (Ch 6) |
| A market is called inefficient | Check externalities, market power, information, and rights | Competitive surplus efficiency needs its assumptions (Ch 7, 10, 23) |
| A firm reports accounting profit | Subtract implicit opportunity costs | Economic profit determines whether resources are best used there (Ch 14) |
| A competitive firm has a loss | Compare price with AVC now and ATC in the long run | Avoidable costs differ by horizon (Ch 15) |
| A monopolist selects output | Set MR = MC, then read P from demand | MR lies below price when increasing sales lowers price on existing units (Ch 16) |
| GDP or salary grew in dollars | Convert to real terms with the appropriate index | Price changes can masquerade as output or purchasing-power growth (Ch 24–25) |
| A future payment looks larger | Compute PV at a stated risk-appropriate rate | Date and uncertainty matter (Ch 28) |
| A budget deficit is said to affect trade | Trace saving → real rate → NCO → exchange rate → NX | The open-economy links operate through two markets (Ch 33) |
| Inflation and unemployment rise together | Test for an adverse supply shock | The old short-run Phillips curve may have shifted (Ch 34, 36) |
| A regression shows a strong correlation | Test confounding and reverse causation | Precision is not causal proof (Ch 38) |

## Formula anchors

| Relationship | Use and assumption |
|---|---|
| `P × Q = total revenue` | Check local price–volume response with elasticity; demand elasticity varies along a straight-line curve. |
| `CS + PS = total private surplus` | Add tax revenue when comparing taxed outcomes; include external effects for social surplus. |
| `profit = total revenue − explicit and implicit costs` | Economic, not accounting, profit. |
| `Y = C + I + G + NX` | GDP expenditure identity; imports are subtracted from spending components. |
| `S = I` closed; `S = I + NCO` open | Define economy and period before applying. |
| `NX = NCO` | Open-economy accounting identity. |
| `PV = FV/(1+r)^N` | Match discount rate to horizon and risk. |
| `M × V = P × Y` | Identity; an inflation inference requires assumptions about velocity and real output. |
