# Glossary

Book terms, synthesized from the named chapters; consult each chapter for assumptions and examples.

**Absolute advantage** — Producing a unit with fewer inputs than another producer. (Ch 3)
**Adverse selection** — People with higher expected costs are more likely to seek coverage at a pooled price. (Ch 12)
**Aggregate demand** — Quantity of domestic goods and services demanded at each price level. (Ch 34)
**Average tax rate** — Total tax divided by total income. (Ch 13)
**Average total cost** — Total cost divided by quantity produced. (Ch 14)
**Budget constraint** — Bundles affordable at given income and prices. (Ch 22)
**Coase theorem** — Under suitable conditions, bargaining can reach an efficient allocation regardless of initial rights. (Ch 10)
**Common resource** — Rival good for which exclusion is difficult. (Ch 11)
**Comparative advantage** — Producing a unit at a lower opportunity cost. (Ch 3)
**Confounder** — Factor related to both explanatory variable and outcome. (Ch 38)
**Consumer price index** — Cost of fixed consumer basket relative to base-period cost. (Ch 25)
**Consumer surplus** — Buyer value less payment. (Ch 7)
**Corrective tax** — Tax intended to align private incentives with social cost. (Ch 10)
**Crowding out** — Reduced private investment associated with government borrowing. (Ch 27)
**Deadweight loss** — Reduction in total surplus beyond government revenue due to a policy distortion. (Ch 8)
**Demand shift** — Change in willingness to buy at every price. (Ch 4)
**Derived demand** — Demand for an input arising from demand for output. (Ch 19)
**Diminishing returns** — Smaller incremental output from successive additions of one input when others are held fixed. (Ch 26)
**Discrimination** — Different treatment based on group attributes rather than relevant productivity. (Ch 20)
**Diversification** — Combining exposures to reduce asset-specific risk. (Ch 28)
**Econometrics** — Statistical methods for studying economic data. (Ch 38)
**Economic profit** — Revenue minus explicit and implicit costs. (Ch 14)
**Efficiency** — Getting the most from scarce resources; distinct from equality of distribution. (Ch 1)
**Equilibrium** — Price and quantity where planned purchases equal planned sales. (Ch 4)
**Excess capacity** — Output below the scale minimizing average total cost. (Ch 17)
**Excludability** — Ability to prevent nonpayers from using a good. (Ch 11)
**Externality** — An uncompensated effect of one person's action on a third party. (Ch 1)
**Fiat money** — Money valued because it is accepted, not because of intrinsic material value. (Ch 30)
**Fisher effect** — Nominal interest rates tend to rise with expected inflation in the long run. (Ch 31)
**Fractional-reserve banking** — Banking with reserves smaller than deposits. (Ch 30)
**Free rider** — Beneficiary who avoids paying for a nonexcludable good. (Ch 11)
**Frictional unemployment** — Joblessness arising from time needed to find a suitable match. (Ch 29)
**GDP** — Market value of final production within national borders over a period. (Ch 24)
**GDP deflator** — Price index implied by nominal divided by real GDP. (Ch 24)
**Human capital** — Skills and knowledge that increase productivity. (Ch 20)
**Import quota** — Legal limit on quantity imported. (Ch 9)
**Income effect** — Response to the change in purchasing power from a price or wage change. (Ch 22)
**Lump-sum tax** — Fixed amount independent of a person's actions or income. (Ch 13)
**Marginal cost** — Extra total cost of one more output unit. (Ch 14)
**Marginal rate of substitution** — Rate at which a consumer is willing to exchange one good for another. (Ch 22)
**Marginal revenue** — Extra revenue from one more unit sold. (Ch 15)
**Marginal tax rate** — Additional tax divided by additional taxable income. (Ch 13)
**Monetary neutrality** — Long-run proposition that money changes nominal rather than real variables. (Ch 31)
**Monopoly** — Sole seller of a good without close substitutes. (Ch 16)
**Moral hazard** — Insured users have less incentive to restrain use or risk after coverage. (Ch 12)
**Nash equilibrium** — Strategies stable against unilateral deviation. (Ch 18)
**National saving** — Income left after consumption and government purchases. (Ch 27)
**Natural monopoly** — Single provider can serve the market at lower cost than multiple firms. (Ch 16)
**Natural rate of unemployment** — Normal long-run level around which actual unemployment fluctuates. (Ch 29)
**Net capital outflow** — Net domestic acquisition of foreign assets. (Ch 32)
**Net exports** — Exports less imports. (Ch 24)
**Normative statement** — A claim about what the world ought to be, requiring a value judgment. (Ch 2)
**Oligopoly** — Market dominated by a small number of sellers. (Ch 18)
**Opportunity cost** — The value of the best forgone alternative of an action. (Ch 1)
**Phillips curve** — Short-run relation between inflation and unemployment. (Ch 36)
**Positive statement** — A claim about how the world is that can, in principle, be tested. (Ch 2)
**Poverty rate** — Share of people with income below an official threshold. (Ch 21)
**PPF** — Boundary between attainable and currently unattainable production combinations. (Ch 2)
**Present value** — Today's amount equivalent to future cash given a discount rate. (Ch 28)
**Price ceiling** — Legal maximum price. (Ch 6)
**Price discrimination** — Different prices to different buyers for the same product based on willingness to pay. (Ch 16)
**Price elasticity of demand** — Absolute percentage change in quantity demanded divided by percentage change in price. (Ch 5)
**Price elasticity of supply** — Percentage change in quantity supplied divided by percentage change in price. (Ch 5)
**Price floor** — Legal minimum price. (Ch 6)
**Producer surplus** — Seller receipt less cost. (Ch 7)
**Productivity** — Goods and services produced per unit of labor input; a central driver of living standards. (Ch 1)
**Real exchange rate** — Relative price of goods across countries after currency conversion. (Ch 32)
**Real interest rate** — Interest rate adjusted for inflation. (Ch 25)
**Rivalry** — One person's use reduces what others can use. (Ch 11)
**Scarcity** — Society has limited resources relative to desired uses. (Ch 1)
**Short-run aggregate supply** — Quantity firms produce at each price level while some expectations or prices adjust slowly. (Ch 34)
**Social cost** — Private cost plus external cost. (Ch 10)
**Standard error** — Estimate of sampling uncertainty in a statistic. (Ch 38)
**Substitution effect** — Response to a change in relative prices holding purchasing power conceptually fixed. (Ch 22)
**Sunk cost** — Past or committed cost unaffected by the present decision. (Ch 15)
**Tariff** — Tax on imports. (Ch 9)
**Tax incidence** — How the economic burden of a tax is divided. (Ch 6)
**Total revenue** — Price multiplied by quantity sold. (Ch 5)
**Total surplus** — Sum of consumer and producer surplus. (Ch 7)
**Tradable permit** — Transferable right to undertake a limited amount of an activity. (Ch 10)
**Value of marginal product** — Product price times marginal product. (Ch 19)
**Velocity** — Rate at which money circulates in transactions. (Ch 31)
**World price** — Price prevailing in world markets. (Ch 9)
