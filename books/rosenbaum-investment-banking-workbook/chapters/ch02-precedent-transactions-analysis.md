# Chapter 2: Precedent Transactions Analysis

## Core Idea
Precedent transactions value a business by examining prices paid for comparable companies in completed or announced acquisitions. Transaction multiples can include control value, expected synergies, auction dynamics, and market conditions; therefore, deal selection and context matter as much as arithmetic.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Transaction-comps workflow**: Identify relevant deals; collect announcement and target financial data; calculate transaction value; normalize multiples; compare deal circumstances; apply a justified range.
  - When to use: M&A valuation, fairness context, or a market-based estimate of control value.
  - How: Define a date window and business criteria; identify consideration and assumed/refinanced claims; use the correct unaffected or agreed share count and financial period; calculate EV and equity multiples; review premiums and circumstances; apply only the most relevant range.
- **Transaction value bridge**: Start with consideration to equity holders and add debt-like claims assumed or repaid, then subtract acquired cash, following a consistent EV definition.
- **Context-adjusted peer judgment**: Compare growth, margins, size, strategic fit, competitive process, financing environment, and date; do not treat the raw median as a control premium that transfers mechanically.

## Key Concepts
- **Precedent transaction**: Completed or announced acquisition used as a valuation benchmark.
- **Control premium**: Difference between acquisition consideration and an appropriate unaffected market reference; not automatically equal to synergy value.
- **Transaction value**: Deal price measure used in the multiple numerator; specify whether equity value or enterprise value.
- **Unaffected price**: Target share price before deal-related information affected trading.
- **Strategic buyer**: Acquirer that may derive operating or strategic benefits unavailable to a standalone buyer.
- **Financial buyer**: Investor whose returns depend substantially on financial performance and exit economics.
- **Purchase multiple**: Deal value divided by a historical or forecast target metric.

## Mental Models
- A deal price is an observed negotiated outcome under specific facts, not a universal market-clearing value.
- Distinguish target standalone value, control value, and buyer-specific synergies.
- A dated sample requires an explicit market-cycle and financing-regime check.

## Anti-patterns
- **Equating premium with synergy**: The premium is a price comparison; it may reflect competition, expectations, or overpayment.
- **Mixing announcement and financial dates**: Pairing stale share counts or inconsistent periods distorts consideration and multiples.
- **Treating all deals as comparable**: Deal rationale, buyer type, market conditions, and process change economics.
- **Applying a transaction multiple without a control/standalone distinction**.

## Reference Tables
| Check | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Deal status/date | Announced, completed, withdrawn? What was the market regime? | Price and information set differ |
| Consideration | Cash, stock, mixed, contingent? | Determines equity consideration and dilution |
| Claims bridge | Debt, preferred, minority interest, cash? | Defines EV consistently |
| Target metric | LTM, current year, forward? Same accounting basis? | Controls denominator comparability |
| Deal context | Strategic fit, auction, distressed sale, synergies? | Explains observed premium/multiple |

## Worked Example
Suppose an acquired target has $1,000 million equity consideration, $300 million debt assumed or refinanced, and $50 million cash acquired. Under a simple EV convention, transaction EV is $1,250 million. If normalized LTM EBITDA is $125 million, EV/EBITDA is 10.0x. That arithmetic does not prove a 10.0x multiple is appropriate for another target: first compare margins, growth, leverage needs, buyer synergies, deal date, and competitive process; then select the subset and range that matches the valuation question.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define transaction value and EV bridge before computing multiples.
2. Normalize target financials consistently and disclose the period used.
3. Interpret observed premiums through deal conditions rather than calling them pure synergy.
4. Weight comparability and recency over sample size.
5. Apply a range only after explaining which deals drive it.
