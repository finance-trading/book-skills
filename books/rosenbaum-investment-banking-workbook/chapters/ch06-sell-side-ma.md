# Chapter 6: Sell-Side M&A

## Core Idea
A sell-side M&A process prepares and markets a company to qualified buyers while protecting confidentiality, maintaining competitive tension, and enabling informed bids. Process design, information quality, and disciplined comparisons affect both price and execution certainty.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Sell-side process sequence**: Prepare analysis and materials; identify and screen buyers; execute confidentiality arrangements; distribute teaser/CIM; manage diligence and Q&A; collect initial and final bids; negotiate documentation; sign and close.
  - When to use: Planning or evaluating a competitive sale of a company or asset.
  - How: Define seller objectives and constraints; establish an approved buyer universe and information protocol; set milestones; compare bids on value, terms, financing, diligence, and certainty; maintain a controlled process through signing and closing.
- **Information ladder**: Use a brief anonymous teaser to create interest, then release a more detailed CIM and data-room access to screened parties under appropriate confidentiality protections.
- **Bid comparison**: Compare headline consideration with financing, conditions, structure, rollover, timing, regulatory exposure, diligence requests, and closing probability.

## Key Concepts
- **Sell-side advisor**: Advisor helping seller prepare, market, evaluate, and negotiate a transaction.
- **Teaser**: Short, often anonymized company description used to assess buyer interest.
- **CIM**: Confidential information memorandum with business, market, and financial detail.
- **Data room**: Controlled repository of diligence materials for authorized parties.
- **Indication of interest (IOI)**: Preliminary, generally nonbinding expression of price and interest.
- **Letter of intent (LOI)**: Written proposed transaction terms, typically subject to definitive documentation and conditions.
- **Management presentation**: Structured opportunity for management to present the business and address buyer questions.
- **Competitive tension**: Credible competition among buyers that may improve terms, subject to execution and confidentiality trade-offs.

## Mental Models
- Optimize for risk-adjusted transaction outcome, not headline price alone.
- Release information in stages, tied to buyer qualification and process progression.
- A broad process can improve competition but raises confidentiality, distraction, and leakage costs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Choosing by headline bid alone**: Financing condition, diligence outs, regulatory risks, and timing can reduce certainty-adjusted value.
- **Uncontrolled disclosure**: Premature or excessive data distribution creates leakage and competitive risk.
- **Inconsistent information to bidders**: Unequal or poorly tracked disclosure can distort bids and create process risk.
- **Ignoring management capacity**: Diligence and presentations can impair business operations if not planned.

## Reference Tables
| Material/stage | Purpose | Control point |
|---|---|---|
| Teaser | Generate qualified interest | Limit identifying/confidential detail where appropriate |
| NDA | Protect information and govern use | Confirm permitted recipients and restrictions |
| CIM | Explain business and investment case | Reconcile claims to reliable data |
| Data room/Q&A | Enable diligence | Track access, answers, and version control |
| IOI / LOI / final bid | Compare buyer proposals | Capture price, structure, conditions, and certainty |

## Worked Example
Two bidders offer the same headline value. Bid A relies on committed financing, has limited conditions, and can close quickly. Bid B offers more but has uncommitted financing, broad diligence conditions, and a longer regulatory path. Compare not only nominal value but also probability and timing of close, conditionality, financing risk, and seller obligations. Keep both engaged if process rules permit while assessing the expected outcome and reputational implications of each path.

## Key Takeaways
1. Prepare coherent, supportable materials before launching the process.
2. Stage access and maintain disciplined confidentiality controls.
3. Compare complete bid terms and certainty, not just price.
4. Maintain consistent, tracked communications and diligence responses.
5. Tailor buyer breadth and timing to seller priorities and execution risk.
