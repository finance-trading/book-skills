# Chapter 7: Buy-Side M&A

## Core Idea
A buyer should establish standalone target value, quantify buyer-specific benefits and costs, and set a price ceiling before negotiating. Diligence tests whether the thesis is real and achievable; disciplined process management prevents strategic enthusiasm from turning into overpayment.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Buy-side acquisition sequence**: Define strategic rationale; screen targets; value standalone economics; estimate synergies and integration costs; conduct diligence; structure and finance a bid; negotiate; plan integration and measure outcomes.
  - When to use: Evaluating an acquisition, merger, or strategic investment.
  - How: State why ownership beats alternatives; build standalone value; separate synergies by type, timing, probability, and owner; account for implementation cost and risk; establish a maximum price; update only when verified facts justify it.
- **Value allocation**: Separate target standalone value from buyer-specific incremental value; the buyer should not pay away all synergies unless competition and strategy justify the trade.
- **Diligence-to-model loop**: Translate diligence findings into explicit changes to revenue, margins, capex, working capital, taxes, liabilities, and closing conditions; revise valuation and bid terms accordingly.
- **Bid discipline**: Set a walk-away price and conditions before the auction intensifies; re-evaluate only with decision-useful new information.

## Key Concepts
- **Standalone value**: Value of the target under its own ownership and operating plan.
- **Synergy**: Incremental value from combining businesses, net of costs and execution risk.
- **Revenue synergy**: Incremental sales or pricing benefit expected from combination.
- **Cost synergy**: Expense or operating-cost reduction from combination.
- **Integration cost**: One-time or ongoing cost required to realize transaction benefits.
- **Diligence**: Verification and risk assessment of financial, commercial, legal, operational, tax, and other matters.
- **Walk-away price**: Maximum price consistent with buyer's valuation, return requirements, and risk tolerance.
- **Accretion/dilution**: Effect of a transaction on buyer earnings per share; not equivalent to value creation.

## Mental Models
- A strategic rationale is a hypothesis; every synergy needs an owner, mechanism, timeline, cost, and probability.
- Value creation and value capture differ: a deal can create surplus while the buyer overpays for it.
- EPS accretion is an accounting outcome, not proof of positive NPV.

## Anti-patterns
- **Strategic premium without quantified benefits**: Narrative alone does not support a higher bid.
- **Counting gross synergies**: Ignore probability, timing, tax, dis-synergies, and integration expenditure.
- **Treating accretion as value creation**: Financing and accounting can make EPS rise even when returns fail to cover cost of capital.
- **Moving the walk-away price due to auction pressure** rather than verified new economics.
- **Leaving diligence disconnected from valuation and transaction protections**.

## Reference Tables
| Diligence finding | Model/term to revisit |
|---|---|
| Customer loss or concentration | Revenue, growth, retention, downside case |
| Cost base / overlap | Synergy amount, timing, one-time integration cost |
| Required investment | Capex, working capital, cash flow, purchase price |
| Debt, pension, litigation, tax exposure | EV bridge, liabilities, indemnities, conditions |
| Regulatory or closing uncertainty | Probability, timing, financing, conditions precedent |

## Worked Example
A buyer estimates a target's standalone value at $500 million and identifies $100 million of gross synergy value. If the buyer expects $30 million of integration costs and believes only 70% of the remaining benefit is achievable, the risk-adjusted incremental value is not $100 million: a simple illustrative estimate is `70% × ($100m − $30m) = $49m`, before timing, tax, discounting, and any dis-synergies. The buyer's value ceiling is therefore informed by standalone value plus risk-adjusted synergy value, while the negotiated price depends on competition and the share of surplus conceded to the seller.

## Key Takeaways
1. Set a standalone valuation and maximum price before negotiating.
2. Underwrite each synergy from mechanism through cost, timing, and probability.
3. Convert diligence findings into forecast changes and transaction protections.
4. Distinguish EPS effects from economic value creation.
5. Preserve a credible walk-away discipline.
