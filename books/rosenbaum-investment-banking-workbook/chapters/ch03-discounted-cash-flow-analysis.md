# Chapter 3: Discounted Cash Flow Analysis

## Core Idea
A DCF values operating assets as the present value of forecast unlevered free cash flow plus terminal value. Reliability depends on internally consistent operating forecasts, reinvestment, discount rate, and terminal assumptions—not spreadsheet precision.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **FCFF DCF sequence**: Forecast operating drivers; calculate EBIT and taxes; add back noncash D&A; subtract capital expenditure and working-capital investment; discount FCFF at WACC; add discounted terminal value; bridge enterprise value to equity.
  - When to use: Standalone operating valuation where financing structure is separated from operations.
  - How: Use a forecast horizon long enough to reach sustainable economics; match nominal/real and currency assumptions; reconcile terminal growth with reinvestment and returns.
- **Unlevered free cash flow**: `FCFF = EBIT × (1 − tax rate) + D&A − capex − change in NWC` (with other operating adjustments where relevant).
- **Perpetuity-growth terminal value**: `TV_n = FCFF_(n+1) / (WACC − g)`; require `WACC > g` and a stable-state forecast consistent with perpetual growth.
- **Exit-multiple terminal value**: Apply a defensible terminal multiple to a terminal-period metric, then discount to present; cross-check it against implied growth and return assumptions.
- **Enterprise-to-equity bridge**: `Equity value = EV − debt − preferred − noncontrolling interests + cash` (adapt for the specific claims and definitions).

## Key Concepts
- **FCFF**: Cash generated by operations for all providers of capital after operating taxes and reinvestment.
- **EBIAT/NOPAT**: EBIT after operating taxes, before financing costs.
- **WACC**: Market-value-weighted required return for debt and equity used to discount FCFF.
- **NWC investment**: Incremental operating working capital tied up to support growth.
- **Terminal value**: Value at the end of explicit forecasts representing cash flows thereafter.
- **Sensitivity analysis**: Recompute value over plausible key assumptions to reveal valuation dependence.
- **Stub period**: Partial period between valuation date and the next forecast cash flow date.

## Mental Models
- Growth consumes capital: forecast sales, margins, and reinvestment together.
- Terminal value is a compressed stable-state business model, not a plug.
- An enterprise value and equity value differ because claims senior to common equity must be recognized.

## Anti-patterns
- **Discount-rate mismatch**: Discounting FCFF at cost of equity or FCFE at WACC.
- **Unfunded growth**: Forecasting revenue expansion without the corresponding capex and working capital.
- **Terminal-value dominance without scrutiny**: A result driven by terminal assumptions needs a robust sensitivity and stable-state check.
- **Inconsistent nominal/currency assumptions** or a perpetual growth rate at/above the discount rate.
- **Double counting cash or debt** between forecast flows and the EV-to-equity bridge.

## Reference Tables
| Forecast line | Typical modeling relationship | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Prior period × (1 + growth) | Growth supported by operating narrative |
| Gross profit | Sales × gross margin | Margin trend has an operational driver |
| EBITDA | Gross profit − operating expenses excluding D&A | Consistent add-backs and classification |
| EBIT | EBITDA − D&A | D&A tied to asset base or revenue |
| EBIAT | EBIT × (1 − operating tax rate) | Taxes applied to operating earnings |
| FCFF | EBIAT + D&A − capex − ΔNWC | Reinvestment and working capital funded |

## Worked Example
Assume projected EBIT of $100, a 25% operating tax rate, D&A of $10, capex of $18, and an increase in NWC of $4. FCFF is `$100×(1−25%) + $10 − $18 − $4 = $63`. At a 9% WACC and 3% perpetual growth, if this $63 is the next-period FCFF, terminal value is `$63/(9%−3%) = $1,050` at the forecast endpoint. Discount it back using the correct period convention, add the PV of explicit FCFF, then subtract debt-like claims and add excess cash to derive equity value. The example is illustrative; actual model inputs must be forecast and period-aligned.

## Key Takeaways
1. Forecast operating economics and reinvestment before calculating value.
2. Match cash-flow definition to discount rate.
3. Make terminal assumptions consistent with sustainable growth and returns.
4. Bridge EV to equity once, with explicit treatment of all claims and cash.
5. Use sensitivities to show which assumptions drive the result.
