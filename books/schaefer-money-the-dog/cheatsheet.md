# Cheatsheet

## Decision rules

| If… | Then… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| A goal is vague | Pick a few priorities; cost and date each | “More” gives no funding decision |
| You doubt a capability | Review factual wins and what caused them; take one small step | Confidence follows evidence and action |
| You need income | Find a recurring problem and test a useful offer | Customers reveal value more clearly than guessing |
| Money arrives | Cover essentials and obligations, then allocate deliberately | Leftovers rarely become a plan |
| Considering borrowing | Compare total repayment cost, necessity, and alternatives first | Monthly affordability hides interest and risk |
| Debt feels urgent | Inventory terms and due dates; preserve essentials; contact creditors | Panic and rolling balances obscure options |
| Considering investment | Check what it owns, fees, risk, custody, and horizon | A familiar label is not understanding |
| Money is needed soon | Keep it accessible rather than relying on volatile assets | Forced selling can lock in loss |
| Prices fall | Recheck needs and plan; don’t panic-sell or blindly hold | A decline alone is not a complete diagnosis |
| A child faces danger | Leave, alert an adult/authorities, don’t investigate | Safety outranks curiosity and reward |

## Story rules vs. adaptable rules

| Book heuristic | Safe use |
|---|---|
| 50/40/10 split | Illustrative allocation only; adapt after essentials, debt, and reserves |
| Save half of post-expense surplus for debt | One example, not universal; compare rates, penalties, and liquidity |
| 10-minute daily practice | Useful habit floor; choose a sustainable cadence |
| 72-hour rule | Make a small first move, not a rushed commitment |
| Rule of 72 | Rough compounding arithmetic, not a return prediction |
| Fund held 5–10 years | Historical story heuristic, not a guarantee or universal horizon |

## Fast tells

- “I need more money” without a named goal → define the goal first.
- One customer or income source → test a second source.
- New borrowing to repay old borrowing → pause and seek a full debt review.
- “Safe because diversified” → check broad market, inflation, currency, and liquidity risks.
- Cash at home as a reserve → assess theft and safety; use suitable regulated options.
- A child expected to solve adult debt → transfer responsibility to adults and qualified help.
