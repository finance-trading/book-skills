# Glossary

**72-hour rule** — Take a first concrete step within 72 hours of deciding; a prompt against delay, not a reason to rush financial commitments (Ch 5).

**Compounding** — Reinvested returns can generate additional returns over time; actual results vary (Ch 7, Ch 16).

**Consumer debt** — Borrowing for purchases that do not create lasting value or repayment capacity; costs include interest and fees (Ch 6).

**Diversification** — Distributing investments across holdings to reduce dependence on a single asset; it does not eliminate market risk (Ch 12, Ch 16–17).

**Dream album** — A visual reminder of prioritized goals, intended to sustain attention and motivation (Ch 2).

**Emergency reserve** — Accessible funds for unexpected or near-term needs, sized to personal circumstances (Ch 8, Ch 12, Ch 17).

**Golden goose** — Metaphor for capital that can produce future income; consuming principal may reduce future options (Ch 7).

**Liquidity** — Ability to access funds when needed without forced sale or excessive loss (Ch 12, Ch 17).

**Opportunity attention** — Noticing options relevant to a clearly defined goal (Ch 2–4).

**Outcome pricing** — Setting compensation around useful value delivered rather than time alone (Ch 3–4).

**Principal** — Original amount of money invested or saved, distinct from returns (Ch 7).

**Rule of 72** — Approximation for doubling time: 72 divided by annual percentage rate; assumes steady compounding and is not a forecast (Ch 16–17).

**Share** — A unit representing partial ownership in a company (Ch 14, Ch 16).

**Success journal** — Factual record of completed actions and their causes, used to identify strengths and build evidence-based confidence (Ch 3, Ch 5, Ch 11, Ch 15).

**Top-three goals** — Choose the few most important wishes to focus planning and scarce resources (Ch 2).

**Volatility** — Variation in asset prices over time; can cause large declines and does not imply recovery is assured (Ch 16–17).
