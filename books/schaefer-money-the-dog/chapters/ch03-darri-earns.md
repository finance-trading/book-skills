# Chapter 3: 达瑞——一个很能挣钱的男孩

## Core Idea
Confidence determines whether useful ideas become attempts. A success journal records evidence of capability, while earning begins by solving a real problem with what one already knows, can do, and has.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Success journal**: Record at least five completed actions daily, including small actions, and note what made them possible.
  - When to use: When self-doubt makes someone discount their ability before trying.
  - How: Keep entries factual; review them before difficult actions and identify repeatable strengths.
- **Solve a problem**: Look for a recurring inconvenience and offer a specific service that removes it.
- **Use known assets**: Start with skills, tools, relationships, and circumstances already available rather than waiting for ideal resources.

## Key Concepts
- **Confidence**: A belief supported by observed actions, sufficient to begin despite uncertainty.
- **Problem-based income**: Payment follows from solving something another person values.
- **Outcome pricing**: The story contrasts payment for completed value with payment only for time.

## Mental Models
- Use evidence of past effort to challenge the assumption “I cannot.”
- Think of early work as a small experiment: find a customer, test the service, improve it, then expand.
- Treat setbacks as information about demand, timing, or delivery.

## Anti-patterns
- **Focusing on absent resources**: Fixating on what one lacks prevents noticing available opportunities.
- **Overconfidence without evidence**: Recording aspirations as achievements does not create capability.

## Worked Example
The story’s boy fails to sell homemade soda in winter, then notices that retrieving a newspaper in cold weather is inconvenient. He offers daily delivery for a small monthly fee, adds related household services, delegates repeatable tasks, and records customer payments. The transferable method is to test demand, price the result, and scale only after delivery works.

## Key Takeaways
1. Confidence is built by documented action, not slogans.
2. Start with an observable problem and an available capability.
3. Improve, delegate, and keep records as demand grows.
