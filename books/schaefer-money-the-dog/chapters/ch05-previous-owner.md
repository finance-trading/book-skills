# Chapter 5: 钱钱以前的主人

## Core Idea
Long-term goals survive interruptions when they have a fixed daily practice and a prompt to begin quickly. The chapter also models responsible care, honest communication, and paid work based on trust.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Ten-minute daily practice**: Protect a brief, regular block for the most important goal even when urgent events compete for attention.
- **72-hour rule**: Take one concrete first step within 72 hours of making a decision.
  - When to use: To reduce procrastination after choosing a goal or commitment.
  - How: Define a small action that can actually be completed; do not treat the deadline as permission to make a consequential financial commitment hastily.

## Key Concepts
- **Consistency**: Repeated small action matters more than occasional intensity.
- **Trust-based work**: Reliability and care can justify payment without changing the fact that the work is enjoyable.
- **Respectful boundaries**: Keep appropriate confidences while communicating honestly.

## Mental Models
- Schedule the goal before the day fills with distractions.
- Convert every decision into a visible next action.
- Separate emotional attachment from practical arrangements and agreements.

## Anti-patterns
- **Waiting for a perfect stretch of time**: Important work disappears behind urgent but less meaningful tasks.
- **Decision without movement**: A goal that produces no action within a short period often stalls.

## Worked Example
The protagonist wants to write a book but repeatedly postpones it. The practice is to write for ten minutes daily, regardless of schoolwork or dog care, and to begin the project within 72 hours of deciding. The same logic applies to opening an account or contacting a potential customer.

## Key Takeaways
1. Give priority goals a protected daily minimum.
2. Begin with a small action promptly after deciding.
3. Reliability builds both trust and earning opportunities.
