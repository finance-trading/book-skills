# Chapter 9: 冒险经历

## Core Idea
Practical learning comes from acting, interacting with institutions, and taking proportionate risks. The chapter’s adventure should not be read as a model for children to enter unsafe places; its financial lesson is subordinate to safety and lawful conduct.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Learn by doing**: Open an account, ask questions, and handle small decisions with appropriate adult support.
- **Prepared risk**: Understand the task, get permission, and identify a safe fallback before acting.
- **Opportunity from connection**: Good service and reliability can lead to referrals and further paid work.

## Key Concepts
- **Bank account**: A place to hold money under specific terms, protections, fees, and access rules.
- **Referral**: Trust earned in one task can lead others to request related services.
- **Risk boundary**: Curiosity and courage do not override consent, legality, or personal safety.

## Mental Models
- Use questions to learn what an account or service actually costs and permits.
- Treat each new job as a contract: clarify scope, timing, payment, and who is responsible.
- Pause when an activity involves strangers, property, animals, or danger; involve a responsible adult.

## Anti-patterns
- **Entering an unsafe property to investigate**: The narrative’s child characters do so; real readers should not imitate this.
- **Assuming every referral is suitable**: Vet the customer and task.

## Worked Example
The protagonist and cousin visit a bank to open an account and choose a staff member with whom they can communicate comfortably. They later receive a dog-care referral. In practice, a guardian should confirm account terms and supervise minors’ work arrangements.

## Key Takeaways
1. Learn institutions by asking specific questions.
2. Trust and reliability generate referrals.
3. Safety and permission outrank adventure or possible reward.
