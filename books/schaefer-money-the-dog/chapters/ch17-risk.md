# Chapter 17: 爷爷奶奶害怕风险

## Core Idea
Market declines create paper losses that become realized losses only if holdings are sold, but recovery is not guaranteed. Cash reserves and a long horizon reduce the pressure to sell; trying to predict market “seasons” is unreliable.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Paper vs. realized loss**: A lower price is an unrealized decline until the holding is sold, though its value has still changed.
- **Reserve before investing**: Keep enough accessible money for living needs so market volatility does not force a sale.
- **No reliable market timing**: Prices may rise or fall next; waiting for a perfectly identifiable low can also mean missing gains.

## Key Concepts
- **Volatility**: Prices can move substantially in either direction.
- **Inflation**: Rising prices can reduce the purchasing power of cash over time.
- **Rebalancing/continuation**: The story favors staying with a long-term plan rather than reacting to one decline.

## Mental Models
- Decide the holding period and cash reserve before buying.
- Review whether the original thesis and time horizon still hold during a decline.
- Compare the risk of loss with the risk that cash loses purchasing power.

## Anti-patterns
- **Panic selling solely because prices fell**: This locks in a decline without a changed plan.
- **Blind holding**: Staying invested is not automatically right if the goal, horizon, or risk has changed.
- **Predicting exact market seasons**: The narrative’s seasonal metaphor is explanatory, not a timing system.

## Worked Example
The club’s fund falls about 25%, and members feel they have lost money. Their mentor distinguishes a current price decline from a sale and reminds them that funds intended for years ahead need not be sold immediately. They also learn why grandparents may rationally prefer safer assets when they need access to money.

## Key Takeaways
1. Separate price movement from a decision to sell.
2. Keep reserves so investing does not threaten essentials.
3. Do not rely on predicting the next market turning point.
