# Chapter 2: 梦想储蓄罐和梦想像册

## Core Idea
Specific priorities make saving actionable. Choose a few goals, keep them visible, and save toward them consistently; visualization is presented as a way to focus attention and motivation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Top-three goals**: List wishes, choose the three that matter most, and make them concrete enough to plan for.
  - When to use: When competing wants make it hard to choose where to put limited money.
  - How: Name the goal, estimate its cost and timing, and revisit the priority as circumstances change.
- **Dream album / visual reminder**: Keep images of goals visible and review them regularly to keep attention on useful opportunities.
  - Why it may help: A vivid reminder can sustain motivation and prompt noticing relevant actions.
  - Failure mode: Treating visualization as if it creates money or guarantees an outcome.

## Key Concepts
- **Goal specificity**: A defined destination is easier to plan for than “more money.”
- **Earmarking**: Saving separately for different goals makes progress observable.
- **Opportunity attention**: A clear aim can make relevant ways to earn or save easier to notice.

## Mental Models
- Use “which three matter most?” to ration attention and scarce funds.
- Pair aspiration with a cost, timeline, and next action.
- Treat imagery as a prompt for behavior, not a financial instrument.

## Anti-patterns
- **Vague abundance goals**: “I want more” provides no decision rule.
- **Uncosted dreams**: Goals without budgets or timelines can conceal infeasibility.

## Worked Example
The protagonist chooses an exchange trip, a computer, and helping her parents with debt from a longer wish list. She gives each a separate visual reminder and saving destination. In real use, she would estimate full costs, check whether the goal is feasible, then set a sustainable contribution.

## Key Takeaways
1. Prioritize before allocating money.
2. Make goals visible, measurable, and revisable.
3. Link every goal to a realistic funding plan.
