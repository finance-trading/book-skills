# Chapter 4: 表哥的挣钱之道

## Core Idea
Opportunities become visible through deliberate search and customer conversations. Match work to genuine interest, but build more than one source of income and expect difficulty to test commitment.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Opportunity search**: Set aside focused time to observe needs, ask people, and test ideas instead of waiting for luck.
- **Interest-to-service match**: Start with an activity one enjoys, then identify who would pay for a useful version of it.
- **Income diversification**: Do not rely on one customer or one job lasting as expected.

## Key Concepts
- **Customer discovery**: Direct conversation reveals willingness to pay and service requirements.
- **Pricing conversation**: State the value offered and negotiate terms clearly.
- **Adversity test**: Easy conditions reveal little; difficulties reveal reliability and adaptability.

## Mental Models
- Ask “who has this recurring need?” before asking “what job exists?”
- Treat the first agreement as a trial, not a permanent plan.
- Keep searching for a second source while the first is working.

## Anti-patterns
- **Passive waiting**: Assuming no opportunity exists because none has appeared.
- **Single-point dependence**: Letting one customer, job, or idea carry the entire plan.

## Worked Example
The cousin combines cycling with Sunday bread delivery, charging both a product margin and a delivery fee, then adds paid assistance for elderly residents. He prompts the protagonist to connect her interest in dogs to walking and training services. She identifies a neighbor struggling with an untrained dog, proposes a trial, and agrees a daily fee plus bonuses for learned commands.

## Key Takeaways
1. Search actively and talk to potential customers.
2. Convert an existing interest into a service someone values.
3. Diversify early and prepare for setbacks.
