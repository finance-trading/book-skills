# Chapter 10: 在旧地下室里

## Core Idea
The plot turns curiosity into a dangerous, legally sensitive situation. The useful lesson is to stop, secure safety, and involve authorities—not to imitate the children’s search of a stranger’s house or handling of valuables.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Safety-first response**: Leave a hazardous or unauthorized area, avoid touching property, and alert a responsible adult or emergency services as appropriate.
- **Professional escalation**: When suspected theft, valuables, or criminal activity appear, hand the problem to qualified authorities.

## Key Concepts
- **Consent**: Permission is required to enter or search property.
- **Chain of custody**: Handling objects can compromise evidence and create legal risk.
- **Personal safety**: Unknown people or locations can present risks that children should not manage alone.

## Mental Models
- Use “safe, authorized, necessary?” before entering a place or touching something.
- Treat clues as a reason to report, not a mandate to investigate.
- Preserve distance and let trained professionals handle evidence.

## Anti-patterns
- **Investigating a suspected burglary**: The protagonists continue searching after noticing signs of intrusion; do not imitate this.
- **Taking or moving valuables**: Even protective intent can create danger and legal confusion.

## Worked Example
The children find evidence of a break-in and a box of valuables in a cellar. The safe real-world response is to leave, tell the homeowner or police, and provide what was observed without moving or examining items.

## Key Takeaways
1. Do not enter or search someone else’s property without clear authorization.
2. Leave suspected crime scenes undisturbed.
3. Acknowledge when narrative adventure is not practical advice.
