# Chapter 14: 投资俱乐部

## Core Idea
An investment group needs shared purpose, explicit rules, and a basic understanding of ownership before contributing money. The chapter introduces shares through a small-business example and emphasizes patience and simplicity.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Club governance**: Set meeting cadence, attendance expectations, contributions, withdrawal limits, and unanimous or otherwise explicit decision rules before investing.
- **Three investment tests**: Seek reasonable safety, growth potential, and simplicity; “safety” is relative and never absolute.
- **Ownership example**: A share represents part ownership whose value depends on the business and what buyers will pay.

## Key Concepts
- **Share**: A claim on part of a company’s ownership and, potentially, its future value.
- **Price vs. value**: Market price reflects what buyers and sellers accept, which may differ from an estimated underlying value.
- **Investment horizon**: Money should match the period for which it can remain invested.

## Mental Models
- Agree on rules before discussing products.
- Understand the claim being bought: ownership, loan, cash, or fund interest.
- Use group decisions to slow impulse, not to replace individual responsibility.

## Anti-patterns
- **Pooling money without governance**: Informal arrangements can create disputes and loss of control.
- **Confusing speculation with understanding**: Knowing a word such as “stock” is not understanding the asset.
- **Treating a story valuation as a pricing method**: Willingness to pay is not a complete valuation framework.

## Worked Example
The children name their club, record five principles, meet monthly, contribute an agreed amount, and require joint decisions. They learn shares by imagining ownership in a delivery business. A real club should also document ownership, custody, fees, and legal requirements.

## Key Takeaways
1. Establish rules and responsibilities before investing together.
2. Understand what ownership means before buying shares.
3. Favor understandable investments and money that can stay invested.
