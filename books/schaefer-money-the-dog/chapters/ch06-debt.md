# Chapter 6: 债务——爸爸妈妈犯下的错误

## Core Idea
Debt decisions require a full view of obligations, interest, repayment terms, and remaining cash. The book criticizes consumer borrowing and proposes balancing repayment with continued saving, but its simplified rule is not universally safe.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Debt reality check**: List every obligation, its interest and fees, required payments, and consequences of delay before choosing a strategy.
- **Surplus split**: The narrative suggests directing half of money left after living expenses to consumer-debt repayment and half to saving.
  - When to use: Only as a discussion model after essential obligations and a basic emergency buffer are secured.
  - Failure mode: A loan with very high interest, penalties, collateral, or a contractual schedule may require faster repayment or professional restructuring.
- **Need pause**: Before borrowing for a purchase, ask whether it is genuinely necessary and whether cash or a lower-cost alternative exists.

## Key Concepts
- **Consumer debt**: Borrowing for spending that does not create lasting value or repayment capacity.
- **Interest burden**: Repeated borrowing can preserve monthly comfort while increasing total cost.
- **Liquidity**: Repayment plans should account for unavoidable expenses and emergencies.

## Mental Models
- Compare total cost and risk, not merely the relief of making a purchase now.
- Preserve enough cash to meet obligations; then attack expensive debt deliberately.
- Separate a child’s wish to help from responsibility for adult legal obligations.

## Anti-patterns
- **Borrowing to repay borrowing**: Rolling balances can obscure the growing cost.
- **All-cash repayment without a buffer**: Eliminating savings may cause new borrowing after the next emergency.
- **Ignoring contractual terms**: “Pay slowly” is unsafe when minimums, penalties, or default rights apply.

## Worked Example
Parents use credit for a couch, vacation, and car, then discuss taking a new loan to repay older ones. The teaching response is to stop nonessential borrowing, list all balances and rates, maintain essential payments, and divide genuine surplus between repayment and savings. A real household should verify every term and seek qualified debt advice when repayment is unsustainable.

## Key Takeaways
1. Know the complete debt picture before acting.
2. Avoid consumer debt and repeated refinancing used to postpone repayment.
3. Balance speed of repayment against liquidity and actual loan terms.
