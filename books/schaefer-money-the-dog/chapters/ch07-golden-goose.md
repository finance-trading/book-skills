# Chapter 7: 在金先生家

## Core Idea
Distinguish money earned from capital retained: savings can produce future income, while spending everything requires continued labor for every future need. The goose-and-eggs metaphor makes the cost of consuming principal visible.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Golden goose**: Treat the capital base as a productive asset; spend with awareness that consuming principal reduces future options.
- **Income allocation**: The story uses 50% for long-term savings, 40% for named goals, and 10% for discretionary spending. Adapt these proportions to essentials, debt, income stability, and local costs.

## Key Concepts
- **Principal**: The original capital invested or saved.
- **Return**: Income or growth generated by capital; uncertain, not guaranteed.
- **Compounding**: Returns can generate further returns when retained and reinvested over time.

## Mental Models
- Ask whether a purchase consumes income or productive capital.
- Use separate accounts or categories to keep goals and long-term savings visible.
- Start saving when income arrives rather than relying on leftovers.

## Anti-patterns
- **Consuming the goose**: Spending all available capital sacrifices future earning potential.
- **Treating interest as guaranteed**: The metaphor simplifies returns; real assets can lose value and have fees or taxes.

## Worked Example
The protagonist receives payment for dog care and divides it into long-term savings, goal funds, and spending money. A realistic plan would first cover essentials and required debt payments, then assign an affordable amount to savings and discretionary goals.

## Key Takeaways
1. Separate saving from spending by purpose.
2. Protect capital intended for long-term goals.
3. Remember that investment returns are not guaranteed eggs.
