# Patterns

## Turn a wish into a funded goal
**When to use**: A vague wish competes with other spending priorities.
**How**: Choose the top few goals; estimate full costs and dates; create separate savings categories; define a sustainable contribution; review progress.
**Trade-offs**: Focus improves clarity but goals may change; revisit rather than treating the original plan as binding.

## Build confidence from evidence
**When to use**: Self-doubt blocks a useful action.
**How**: Record completed actions; note what made them possible; review evidence; choose the next small step.
**Trade-offs**: Encourages agency but does not erase structural limits or guarantee outcomes.

## Discover value-based work
**When to use**: Seeking income with limited starting resources.
**How**: Identify a recurring problem; talk to potential customers; offer a narrow trial; clarify terms; deliver reliably; ask for feedback; track costs and referrals.
**Trade-offs**: Low-cost experiments limit downside but demand may not persist. Account for time, permission, safety, and fair compensation.

## Allocate income by purpose
**When to use**: Income arrives and goals compete.
**How**: Cover essentials and contractual obligations; maintain a suitable reserve; split remaining money among long-term savings, named goals, and discretionary spending. The book’s 50/40/10 is illustrative, not a default.
**Trade-offs**: Earmarking improves discipline; rigid ratios can fail with irregular income or high-cost debt.

## Triage debt
**When to use**: Repayments feel unmanageable or borrowing is recurring.
**How**: List balances, rates, fees, due dates, collateral, and default consequences; stop avoidable new borrowing; protect essentials; contact creditors early; compare repayment approaches; seek qualified help when needed.
**Trade-offs**: Fast repayment reduces interest but can deplete liquidity; the book’s half-surplus split is not universal advice.

## Evaluate a long-term fund
**When to use**: Considering pooled exposure to shares or other assets.
**How**: Understand holdings, fees, strategy, risks, liquidity, custodian, tax treatment, and time horizon; diversify; invest only money not needed soon.
**Trade-offs**: Diversification reduces single-company risk but cannot prevent broad market losses. Historical returns are not promises.

## Respond to a frightening decline
**When to use**: A portfolio falls and panic prompts selling.
**How**: Check whether needs, horizon, or original assumptions changed; confirm reserves; avoid decisions based solely on headlines; seek advice if the plan no longer fits.
**Trade-offs**: Patience avoids impulsive decisions but blind holding can be wrong when circumstances or fundamentals change.

## Grow by delegation
**When to use**: Repeatable work exceeds personal capacity.
**How**: Document the task; identify a trustworthy helper; train and supervise; track quality and economics; retain tasks that depend on distinctive strengths.
**Trade-offs**: Frees capacity but adds coordination, cost, and responsibility for fair treatment.

## Safety escalation
**When to use**: A child encounters suspicious, unsafe, or unauthorized circumstances.
**How**: Leave; avoid touching property; inform a responsible adult or authorities; give observations without investigating.
**Trade-offs**: Curiosity pauses, but safety and lawful handling take precedence over possible reward.
