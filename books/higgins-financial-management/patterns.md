# Patterns — Analysis for Financial Management

## DuPont ROE Decomposition
**When to use**: any time you must explain or change a firm's return on equity.
**How**: `ROE = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage`. Compute the three ratios individually; the product must equal reported ROE. Attribute any movement to exactly one lever.
**Trade-offs**: three levers can substitute — a discounter runs high turnover/low margin, a luxury firm the reverse. Don't prescribe one mix; find which lever the firm controls.

## Sustainable Growth Rate (PRAT)
**When to use**: to set or test a growth target, or to diagnose a cash squeeze/surplus.
**How**: `g* = P × R × A × T̂` (profit margin × retention × asset turnover × leverage at opening equity). Equivalent: `g* = Retention × ROE`. Compare to actual growth.
**Trade-offs**: raising any of the four raises g* but shifts policy (margin, payout, efficiency, leverage). Each has its own cost and risk.

## Percent-of-Sales Forecasting with a "Plug"
**When to use**: to answer "how much outside financing do we need this year?"
**How**: forecast sales, hold stable drivers (days' sales, payables period, turnover, cost ratios) constant, project pro-forma statements, and let the balance-sheet gap be external funding required.
**Trade-offs**: sound only when historical ratios are genuinely stable; drifts in margin or payables break the extrapolation. Always pair with a cash-flow forecast.

## Sources-and-Uses Reconciliation
**When to use**: to explain a change in cash or trace an anomaly in the balance sheet.
**How**: list increases in liabilities/equity and decreases in assets as sources; increases in assets and decreases in liabilities/equity as uses. A reduction in an asset is *cash in*.
**Trade-offs**: mechanical but manual; easy to miscount a sign. Still the fastest first diagnostic.

## Discounted Cash Flow / NPV
**When to use**: capital budgeting and firm valuation with forecastable cash flows.
**How**: `NPV = Σ CFₜ/(1+r)ᵗ − Outlay`. Accept if NPV > 0; report IRR only for communication; draw a cash-flow diagram first.
**Trade-offs**: demands a correct discount rate (risk-specific, portfolio-derived) and honest cash-flow estimates. Garbage-in garbage-out.

## Control Ratios (Asset-by-Asset Turnover)
**When to use**: to localize which asset (receivables, inventory, fixed) is absorbing cash.
**How**: compute sales ÷ each asset class over time; a falling ratio flags a build-up before it becomes a loss.
**Trade-offs**: simple but needs trend or peer comparison to be meaningful.

## Risk-Analysis Ladder
**When to use**: as stakes and uncertainty in a project grow.
**How**: escalate sensitivity (one variable) → scenario (coherent states) → simulation (distributions) → decision trees (contingencies) → real options (flexibility).
**Trade-offs**: each rung adds fidelity and cost; don't skip straight to simulation when a sensitivity run names the driver.

## Comparable-Multiples Cross-Check
**When to use**: to sanity-check a DCF valuation against the market.
**How**: apply peers' P/E, EV/EBITDA, or other multiples to the target's metric.
**Trade-offs**: multiples embed market sentiment; they confirm or challenge a DCF but never substitute for one.

## Acquisition Price Discipline
**When to use**: to set the maximum bid in M&A.
**How**: max price = standalone DCF value + PV of realizable synergies. The control premium is the value of synergies you can actually deliver.
**Trade-offs**: overpaying for undelivered synergies is the classic value destroyer; stand firm at the sum.
