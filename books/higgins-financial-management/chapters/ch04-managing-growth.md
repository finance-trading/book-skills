# Chapter 4: Managing Growth

## Core Idea
Growth is not free: new sales require new assets, which must be financed. The **sustainable growth rate** is the single maximum rate at which a firm can grow sales without issuing new equity or changing its financial policy — and it is fully determined by four ratios.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Sustainable Growth Equation**:
  `g* = P × R × A × T̂`  (the "PRAT" four factors)
  where **P** = profit margin (NI/sales), **R** = retention ratio (retained earnings/NI), **A** = asset turnover (sales/assets), **T̂** = financial leverage (assets/equity, at opening-of-period). Equivalently `g* = retention rate × ROE`.
  - When to use: to find how fast a firm *can* grow on internal funds, given its profitability and policy.
  - How: multiply the four ratios; the product is the growth rate that keeps the firm's capital structure and dividend policy intact.
- **Balanced growth**: actual growth equal to sustainable growth — all four ratios constant, no external equity.
  - When to use: as the baseline against which any "actual vs. sustainable" gap is judged.
- **Too-much-growth policy menu**: when actual growth exceeds g*, the firm must sell equity, increase leverage, cut payout, prune profitably, outsource, raise prices, or merge.
- **Too-little-growth policy menu**: when g* exceeds actual growth, the firm is generating more cash than it can reinvest — it can ignore it, return cash to shareholders (dividends/buybacks), or buy growth.

## Key Concepts
- **Sustainable growth rate (g*)** — the product of the four ratios; the growth achievable without new equity or changed policy.
- **Retention ratio (R)** — the fraction of earnings reinvested (`1 − payout`).
- **Profit margin (P)** — earnings per dollar of sales.
- **Asset turnover (A)** — the sales produced per dollar of assets.
- **Financial leverage (T̂)** — the equity multiplier (assets ÷ equity).
- **"Growing broke"** — the failure mode when actual growth persistently outstrips sustainable growth and the firm runs out of financing before it runs out of orders.

## Mental Models
- Think of g* as the **nexus of growth-return combinations that balance the balance sheet**.
- Read "actual > sustainable" as a **cash deficit to be financed**; "actual < sustainable" as a **cash surplus to be deployed**.
- Use `g* = Retention × ROE` to see instantly that growth capacity is *profitability × reinvestment*.

## Anti-patterns
- **Chasing sales growth as an unalloyed good**: growth beyond g* consumes cash faster than it generates it; many dead companies were profitable on paper the quarter they failed.
- **Ignoring the opening-of-period equity convention** in T̂, which changes g* materially at high leverage.
- **Treating g* as a target**: it is a ceiling; the task is to *manage* the gap, not always to close it.

## Worked Example
**DLH Holdings Corp.** (a government-services healthcare firm) is run through the sustainable-growth analysis for 2016–2020. Its four ratios move over the period — profit margin 4.0→3.4%, retention 100%, asset turnover 1.32→1.14×, leverage 2.85→4.03× — so for each year `g* = P × R × A × T̂` yields a *different* sustainable rate. Plotting g* against actual sales growth shows whether each year's gap is a deficit (actual > g*, a financing squeeze) or a surplus (actual < g*, cash piling up). Management's job is then the menu: during deficit years, raise equity, increase leverage, prune or repurchase; during surplus years, pay dividends, buy back stock, or acquire. The example makes the abstract equation operational as a year-by-year decision tool.

## Key Takeaways
1. Compute `g* = P × R × A × T̂` as the very first step of any growth-planning discussion.
2. Compare actual vs. sustainable growth; the *gap* is the real management problem.
3. A growth deficit is a financing problem (equity, debt, payout, pruning); a surplus is an allocation problem (dividends, buyback, acquisition).
4. Sustainable growth depends on all four ratios — a change in *any one* shifts the ceiling.
5. Never hand a growth target to an operator without stating how it will be financed.
